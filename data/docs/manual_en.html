Puerto Bravo: Ley del Asfalto

Game manual, version 1.7.2. An open-world audio game, in English, Spanish and Vietnamese, and fully accessible: you play it by ear alone, with the keyboard or with an Xbox or PlayStation controller, and with your screen reader.

The other guides are in the same folder, data\docs: keys and buttons, what's new in each version, changing the sounds and the music and mod editor manual.

This manual is organized by headings. With NVDA or JAWS you can jump from section to section with the H key.

1. What this game is

At the start you choose between 4 characters, each with its own advantages, or you create your own, and you decide whether people address you as a man or as a woman. With the first one your name is Dani Roca and you have just got out of prison after serving three years for a robbery you did not commit. You go back to your city, Puerto Bravo, which is now controlled by the businessman Octavio Belmonte. From there, do whatever you want: follow the story, or don't.

  • An open world with 9 maps: the cities of Puerto Bravo, San Lucero and Monteferro; the towns of Villaseca, Cala Brava and Fuentefría; Isla Tortuga, the Neptuno Platform and the Mar de Poniente, which you can sail by boat.
  • 311 places: homes, stores, banks, repair shops, casinos, gyms, cinemas, hospitals, piers, heliports and monuments, each with its own sound and description.
  • 101 missions between the main story and the side stories of the other towns. On top of that, jobs you can repeat endlessly.
  • 40 vehicles: cars, motorcycles, a bicycle, trucks, emergency vehicles, a race car, 8 boats and a helicopter.
  • 28 weapons, from your fists to the rotary machine gun, including silent weapons and explosives.
  • Jobs: taxi driver, pizza delivery, ambulance and bus driver, security guard, hauler, ice cream seller, sea courier and fisher.
  • Repeatable jobs: contracts from the tavern notice boards, car orders at the scrapyards, boxing matches at the gyms and bank robberies.
  • Random street events, four skills that improve with practice and 40 achievements.
  • 9 races and regattas, a casino with slot machines, blackjack and roulette, a shooting gallery, dice, the lottery, a bank, properties, businesses that make money and bodyguards.
  • Police with five wanted levels, day and night, rain, traffic with traffic lights and pedestrians.
  • Two ways of living, which you can mix: the honest life, with a job center, careers with promotions, courses and volunteering, and the life of crime.
  • Background music with several tracks for each situation, which goes quiet when you turn on the radio.
  • An optional mode with content for over-18s, turned off by default.
  • A living city: you can talk to anyone on the street, each with their own name, job and personality, and every day is different: prices go up and down, there are holidays, strikes and market days. No two games are the same.
  • Play with friends over the internet or at home, without anyone's servers: your own game hosts the session.
  • Updates from inside the game, choosing where they download from, and separate components: languages, voices, guides, sound packs and mods.
  • In Spanish, English and Vietnamese, with recorded voices in Spanish and English, and ready to be translated into any other.
  • An accessible mod editor to create your own expansions.
  • Seven radio stations with original music, a news station that comments on what you do and another with your own music.

2. Requirements and starting the game

  • Windows 10 or 11.
  • Stereo headphones. They are essential: the game places each sound where it belongs around you.
  • A screen reader running, such as NVDA or JAWS. If there is none, the game speaks with the Windows voice.
  • A 64-bit processor. The game adapts on its own to your processor: on current ones it uses its normal sound engine and, on old ones, from before 2009 or 2010, one made for them. You don't have to choose anything.

Run PuertoBravo.exe. The game installs nothing: everything it uses or creates is in the data folder, next to the executable. You can copy the whole game folder to another computer.

The window shows the last spoken message in large, high-contrast letters, in case you play with some remaining sight or with someone who can see.

The game's language

The game is in Spanish, English and Vietnamese (Tiếng Việt), and more languages can be added. When it opens it looks at your Windows language: if it is any variant of Spanish, it runs in Spanish; if it is another one and the game has that language, in that one; and if it doesn't, in English.

  • To choose it yourself, go into Options: the first line is Language, and you change it with the left and right arrows. Automatic is what was just explained; if you choose a language, the game always runs in that one, even if your Windows is in another. The change takes effect when you open the game again. Each language is named in yours and, in brackets, in its own: Vietnamese (Tiếng Việt), Spanish (Español). That way you find yours even if you don't understand the game's language.
  • Everything that is said and read is in the chosen language: the menus, the story, the places, the items, the keys, the mod editor and the server panel. This manual and the other guides too: each document has one version per language, and the game opens the one in its language.
  • With version 1.7.2, Vietnamese is installed as a component, in Options, Updates, Components, and then chosen in Options, Language. A language whose guides are not translated yet, like Vietnamese, opens the English ones.
  • The story scenes have recorded voices in Spanish and English; in other languages your screen reader says them. If you play with a character you made yourself, the lines that say your name are generated on your computer with voices in the game's language.
  • Your saved games work in any language: a game saved in Spanish loads in English, and the other way round, with your clothes, items and everything else in place.
  • On an online server each player plays in their own language, and what the server says reaches you translated into yours.

If it's your first time: the playable tutorial

The game starts in Grandma's Apartment. Right there, with Enter, you can start the mission Learn to get around: a tutorial you play. It is three short exercises, with no time limit, no enemies and no police, in which you cannot fail:

  1. Walk to a pharmacy using the GPS and the beacon, or letting the character walk on their own.
  2. Find a store with the radar and mark it. This exercise checks that you use the radar: if you reach the store another way, it tells you, and you only have to press Tab right there.
  3. Get into a car and take it to a gym, driving yourself or with the autopilot.

It is optional and you can do it at any time: it is on the phone, in Missions, under Learn to play. It takes about five minutes.

There are three more tutorials, also optional, and a briefing. You start them from the phone, in Missions: they appear with the word Tutorial in front, and with Enter they start right away, wherever you are: if you are far from where it takes place, or in another town, the tutorial itself takes you to its starting point and tells you. You cannot fail any of them, and while they last the police do not come. All of them check that you do what they teach: just reaching a place is not enough.

  • Learn to defend yourself, at the Olympic Shooting Club: lock on to a target, hit with a bat, shoot and reload, against dummies that don't fight back.
  • Learn to earn a living, at the Job center: you get into a practice taxi, start work with J and do a real fare: pick up a customer and take them to their destination. The tutorial doesn't move on until you do it, and whatever you earn is yours. When it ends you can keep working with that taxi.
  • Learn to sail, at the Harbor Pier. There are three exercises: set sail, in a practice boat the pier lends you for free while the tutorial lasts; steer, sailing about two hundred meters and changing course; and dock at a pier, whichever one you like. Just walking somewhere no longer completes it.
  • Learn to play with friends is no longer a mission, because without a friend there is nothing to check: it is a briefing. It appears in that same list with the word Briefing in front, and also on the phone, in Play with friends, under the name How to play with friends. You read it right there, without moving from where you are.

3. The menus

  • Up Arrow and Down Arrow: move through the options.
  • Enter: choose.
  • Left Arrow and Right Arrow: change the value of an option.
  • Escape: go back.
  • Home and End: first and last option.
  • A letter: jumps to the next option that starts with it. Accents don't count: A also takes you to what starts with Á.
  • Delete, in Adjust the volume of each sound: sets the chosen sound back to its default volume.
  • F1: explanation of the current option.
  • Page Up and Page Down: review previous messages. Control Page Up goes to the first one in the history and Control Page Down to the last one.

The history keeps the last three hundred messages. By default, each new message takes you to the end. If you prefer to stay where you were while you review, in Options, Voice, set When a new message arrives, the history jumps to the end to no; then you go to the last one with Control Page Down. With a controller, the actions menu has Go to the first message in the history and Go to the last.

When you type text, such as your character's name or a message, accented vowels typed with the accent key also work, as do Windows input methods.

In the story dialogues, Enter moves to the next line, Up Arrow repeats the line, Left Arrow goes back to the previous one and Escape skips the whole dialogue. No dialogue moves on by itself: you read at your own pace.

4. Finding your way

The city

The cities and towns are a grid. Streets run from west to east and avenues from south to north, with an intersection every 90 meters. Each street has the roadway in the middle and a sidewalk on each side. The blocks are solid buildings, except for squares, parks, beaches and piers, which you can walk through.

The sound

What sounds to your right is to your right. What sounds muffled, as if through a blanket, is behind you. The farther away, the quieter. On foot, in compass mode, your hearing always faces north: north is in front, east is to the right. At the wheel, the sound turns with the vehicle.

Orientation tools

None of them are required and almost all can be turned off in Options. Use the ones you like.

  • C, where am I: street, between which avenues, neighborhood and which way you are facing. With Shift it adds the coordinates.
  • W, A, S and D, directional scanner: your cane. It says what is in front, to the left, behind and to the right: people, vehicles, doors, where the roadway begins and how many meters away the wall is. A tone also plays from the wall.
  • Tab, radar: goes through everything nearby, from nearest to farthest, with distance and direction, and plays each thing from its position. Shift Tab goes backwards. F6 filters by places, vehicles, people or items.
  • B: points the GPS at the last thing the radar said. If you were already going somewhere, it is a detour: the game says so, and when the detour ends the GPS goes back on its own to the destination you had, and says that too. A detour to a vehicle ends when you get into it; to a person, when you reach them; to an item, when you get on top of it or pick it up; and to a place, when you arrive. If what you were looking for disappears, it also ends. It is useful for going after money, an item or a car nearby without losing your route. With Shift G you cancel the detour and go back to your destination; with another press you clear the GPS. The detour notice always reminds you of this, whether you are going after something that stays still or following someone who moves.
  • L, list of places: by category and in alphabetical order. It includes a list of unvisited places and the other towns. Enter sets the destination.
  • F9, map explorer: moves a cursor from intersection to intersection with the arrows, without moving your character, so you can learn the town. Tab goes through the places next to the cursor, N describes them, D says how far it is from you, Home goes back to your position and Enter sets the destination. It also works at sea.
  • Personal markers: on the phone you can save the exact spot where you are and return to it with the GPS.
  • G, GPS: repeats the direction. It says the distance and the next turn. Shift G clears the destination: if you were on a detour, you go back to what you had, and with another press the GPS is left with no destination. Control G brings back the last destination or detour you cleared, without having to look for it again; if you are now going somewhere else, it comes in as a detour. During a mission, if the GPS is left with no destination, after two seconds it goes back on its own to what the mission asks for and says so. If you clear it yourself with Shift G, it doesn't come back on its own: you get it back with G or with Shift O. With no mission and no destination, G brings back the last one you cleared.
  • O, objective: first says where the thing the mission asks for is, with its name as it appears in the list of places, how many meters away, in which direction and on which street, and then the rest. If the objective moves, as in a chase or when following someone, or if the clock is running, one press says only where it is and where it is heading, and two presses in a row read the whole explanation. If the GPS is on a detour or doesn't have the objective set, O says so.
  • Shift O, back to the objective: always does the same thing. It drops any detour, sets on the GPS what the mission or job asks for now and, if it is within radar range, leaves it selected on the radar. The same is done, on the phone, by Back to the objective and by the current mission, which comes first in Available missions; and, in the list of places on L, by the first entry, Mission objective.
  • Sound beacon: a little bell that rings in the direction you have to go, faster the closer you are. On foot it rings at the corner of the sidewalk, not in the middle of the intersection. You turn it on and off with F4.
  • Sounds of places: each type of place has its own sound, which you hear as you get close. A gun shop sounds like a gun being cocked, a bar like music through the wall, a store like a door chime. They are all in the Learn the sounds menu.
  • Learn the sounds: it is in the main menu and in the pause menu, and it has every sound in the game, with none missing. First comes the group The most important, with the ones you hear most and that help most to find your way and survive, in the order in which it is best to learn them. Then comes Search for a sound, and then all the sounds divided into groups, according to what you are doing in the game when they play. When you enter a group the game says how many sounds it has. Inside, they are in alphabetical order of their names, and a letter jumps to the next one that starts with it, whether it has an accent or not. Press Enter on a sound to hear it: the game says what it is called and what hearing it means, and when you press, only that sound plays, with the sound pack you have set. The groups, which are the same and in the same order in Options, Adjust the volume of each sound, and in the guide to changing the sounds, are these:
    1. Orientation and GPS
    2. Sounds of places
    3. Moving on foot and swimming
    4. Vehicles and driving
    5. Breakdowns, repair shop, gas and vehicle theft
    6. Boats, helicopters, train, tram and plane
    7. Weapons, the shot of each one
    8. Combat, aim and wounds
    9. Police and emergency services
    10. People on the street and animals
    11. Home, kitchen and food
    12. Stores, money and items
    13. Menus, phone and game alerts
    14. Missions, work, reputation and mood
    15. Territories and gangs
    16. Online play and roleplay
    17. Fishing, casino and games
    18. Ambience and weather
    19. Relationships, only with adult content
    20. Substances and dealing, only with adult content
    Search for a sound saves you from guessing the group: type a word or part of one, for example siren, door or police, and press Enter. You get the list of sounds that have it in their name or explanation, each with the group it belongs to. It doesn't matter whether you type it with or without accents, or in upper or lower case; with several words, you get the ones that have all of them. With a controller you type with the letter wheel: D-pad up and down to choose the letter, D-pad right to type it, and A or Cross to search.
  • Footsteps: they sound different on sidewalk, asphalt, grass, sand and pier.
  • Accessible traffic signals: at each intersection, a fast ticking means you can cross the street walking north or south, and a slow chirp means you can cross the avenue east or west.
  • N: description of the place or the neighborhood. For the neighborhood it also says who it belongs to at that moment: a gang, you, or at war. K: which way you are facing, with a tone that plays from the north. T: time and weather. V: your status.
  • Shift N: the next intersection. It says which street is the next one you are going to cross in the direction you are facing or driving, how many meters away it is and which street you are on. It works the same on foot and at the wheel, and says nothing else.
  • Shift L: where your vehicle is. It says where you left the vehicle you got out of and sets it on the GPS. If you were going somewhere else, for example to a mission location, when you reach the vehicle the GPS goes back to that destination on its own. It is also on the phone: Where is my vehicle. If you have more vehicles, it tells you how many, and that they are all on the phone, in My vehicles.

The GPS has a single destination

The GPS always guides you to one single place, and that is the one G tells you: the directions, the beacon and the autopilot all go there. Underneath, another one can be waiting, which you go back to when the one on top ends, and the game always says so.

  • What you set yourself while a mission or a job has its destination set is a detour: when you arrive, the GPS goes back to the mission's destination.
  • What you set with the radar's B or with Shift L is a detour from whatever you had, whatever it was.
  • If the mission changes destination while you are on a detour, the game tells you that you are still on the detour and where the GPS will go afterwards.
  • If the mission ends during a detour, your destination stays and there is nothing underneath any more.
  • O talks about the mission objective and G about the GPS destination. When they don't match, O says so and reminds you that Shift O goes back to the objective.

Walking on your own

With a destination set on the GPS, the U key on foot makes your character follow the route on their own until they arrive, along the sidewalks and turning the corners. Hold Shift to make them run. Any arrow, or U again, stops them. You still hear the intersections, the places and the traffic, and cars don't stop for you: when crossing, listen. At the wheel the same thing exists with the Y key, the GPS autopilot.

How directions are given

In Options you choose how they are said: with compass points, with clock positions or with in front, right, behind and left.

This list comes from the game itself, so it is always complete. Inside the game you have it with F1, and also in the document teclas_en.html.

Any time

  • V: status: health, money, weapon, wanted level and vehicle.
  • T: time, day and weather.
  • O: objective of the current mission or job: it first says where it is, how many meters away, in which direction and, if it is moving, where it is going, and then the rest. If the objective moves or time is running, one press says only that, and two in a row read the whole explanation. In a heist with your gang it also says the phase and who does what; with a commercial flight or a hijacked aircraft, where you have to take it.
  • Shift O: go back to the objective of the mission or job: it drops any detour, sets it on the GPS and, if it is within range, selects it on the radar. It always does the same thing.

  • Shift F10: on a server with roleplay channels, speak in character: only those near you hear it.
  • Ctrl F10: on a server with roleplay channels, describe what your character does: you type "opens the door" and those nearby hear your name and "opens the door".
  • F9: map explorer: moves through the town with a cursor, intersection by intersection, without moving the character. Tab says the places next to the cursor and Enter sets the destination.
  • Tab and Shift Tab: radar: sweeps what is around you, from nearest to farthest.

  • F6: changes the radar filter: everything, places, vehicles, people or objects.
  • L: list of places in the city to set a destination.
  • G: repeats the GPS instruction. In a mission, with no destination set, it sets again what the mission asks; with no mission, it recovers the last destination you removed.
  • Shift G: removes the GPS destination. If you were on a detour, the GPS goes back to what you had; another press removes that too.
  • Ctrl G: recovers the last destination or detour you removed with Shift G, without having to look for it again. If you are now going somewhere else, it enters as a detour.
  • M: opens the phone: missions, places, map explorer, bookmarks, services, skills, achievements, properties and statistics.   • P: at sea, casts the fishing rod; when you hear a bite, press Space. In emergency vehicles, turns on the siren.

  • Page Up and Page Down: go back over earlier messages. Period repeats the latest one.
  • Ctrl Page Up and Ctrl Page Down: go to the first message in the history and to the last one. In Options, Voice, you choose whether each new message takes you to the end.
  • F1: this help.
  • F2: switches between assisted and free driving.
  • F3: switches walking between compass and turning.
  • F4: turns the GPS sound beacon on or off.

  • F7 and F8: lower and raise the music volume.
  • F12: puts a marker in the session log. Press it when you notice something odd: that way, whoever reviews the log will know when it happened.
  • Escape: pause menu.

On foot

  • Arrow keys: walk. In compass mode each arrow goes toward a cardinal point: up is north, right is east. In turning mode, left and right turn 45 degrees and up moves forward.
  • Left Shift with the arrow keys: run. The right Shift key does not make you run or switch between walking and running. Combinations, like Shift N, work with either one and do not change the pace.
  • W, A, S and D: directional scanner: it says what is ahead, to the left, behind and to the right, and how far away the wall is.
  • Space: attack or shoot. With automatic weapons, hold it down.
  • R: reload.
  • Shift R: the music player with headphones, if you bought it: it turns it on and changes station, so you can hear the radio and your music on foot. One of the stations is off.
  • X: crouch or stand up. When crouching you are hit less, but you move slower.
  • Shift X: take cover behind a wall or a parked vehicle, or leave it. In cover you are hit much less; if you move, you leave cover.
  • Backspace: slow time: everything goes slower while you have concentration left. Another press turns it off.
  • H: shout to scare off pedestrians. On duty as a police officer, orders suspects to stop.
  • E: with a weapon in your hand, rob the pedestrian next to you.
  • Y: if you have a dog, you ask it to sniff out the nearest object.
  • Shift Y: your dog's menu: how it is, pet it, feed it, send it home or call it.
  • J: talk to the person next to you. Each one has their own name, job and personality: you can greet them, ask about a place, invite them for a coffee, make friends or type whatever you want to tell them.
  • Shift J: when playing with friends or on a server, says how the nearest player is dressed: their clothes and accessories. On a server with roleplay channels, it also reads their character sheet, if they wrote one. It only says it when you ask.
  • U: walk by itself: with a destination set on the GPS, the character follows the route on its own until arriving. Hold Shift to make it run. Any arrow key, or U again, stops it.

At the wheel, on a boat and in a helicopter

  • Up arrow: accelerate.
  • Down arrow: brake and, once stopped, reverse.
  • Space: handbrake.
  • Left and right arrows: in assisted driving they signal: the car will turn by itself at the next intersection. In free driving they turn the wheel.
  • A and D: in assisted driving, hold them to change lanes: A takes the opposite lane to overtake and D pulls you to the curb. Flying a helicopter or a light plane, with real flight, D goes up and A goes down.
  • A quick tap of A: the GPS autopilot settings: turn it on or off and choose how it drives: Automatic, Calm, Normal obeying the rules, Hurried but obeying the rules, or Hurried breaking the rules.
  • Automatic braking: in assisted driving the car brakes by itself if there is a vehicle ahead in your lane. It is turned off in Options.
  • U: U-turn, with the car almost stopped. With the GPS autopilot, it does it by itself when the destination is behind you.
  • Y: GPS autopilot: the car goes by itself to the set destination; it accelerates, brakes, turns, waits at traffic lights and stops on arrival.
  • E: in free driving, straightens the car toward the nearest cardinal point.
  • W: current speed.
  • H: horn.
  • R: changes the station. Shift R turns the radio off.
  • Ctrl: shoot from the vehicle without getting out, with a firearm in your hand: first Q locks onto whoever is near, and Ctrl shoots them. I changes weapon. With an automatic weapon, hold it down.
  • J: starts or ends a job with the vehicle: taxi, ambulance, bus, patrol car or truck. If you are a police officer, in a patrol car it starts or ends the shift.
  • Helicopter: and light plane: they fly above buildings and traffic and are flown by hand, like a boat. With real flight, up arrow takes off, D goes up, A goes down and a note says the altitude; to land, brake and press F. When you reach the edge of the town, or with Enter at a heliport or an airport, you choose a destination and make the crossing: there, the arrows control heading and altitude, Tab is the autopilot, Enter says how the flight is going, N speeds it up and F1 explains it. With real flight turned off in Options, you always fly at the same height and Enter at a heliport takes you in one jump.
  • At sea: boats are always steered by hand: left and right turn the helm and the heading is announced. Enter next to a pier docks the boat.

Walking around inside a house, a venue or a shop

  • Left arrow and right arrow: go to the room, hall or corridor next door. You hear your footsteps toward that side and the game says where you arrive.
  • Enter: use the place where you are. In your home kitchen, put away the groceries, open the fridge or the pantry and cook. In a food shop aisle, see its products: Enter or right arrow puts one in the basket and left arrow puts it back. At the register, pay for the basket.
  • C: in a food shop, what you have in the basket and how much it adds up to. With a controller, X or Square.
  • F1: where you are, what Enter does there and where you have to go to be served.
  • Escape: leave. In a food shop, with something in the basket, it first asks what you do with it: pay, leave it, take it without paying or keep shopping.

6. Playing with a controller

The game recognizes Xbox controllers, PlayStation controllers, DualShock 4 and DualSense, and compatible ones on its own. Connect it by cable or by Bluetooth, before or after opening the game: it is announced when detected. Controller and keyboard can be used at the same time. Everything the keyboard does can be done with the controller.

The game's help, with F1 or with the Y or Triangle button inside a menu, says what each button does using your controller's names.

In the menus

  • D-pad or left stick: move. A or Cross: choose. B or Circle: go back.
  • Y or Triangle: help for the option. X or Square: repeat. LB and RB, or L1 and R1: review previous messages.
  • D-pad left and right: change the value of an option. In the aisle of a food store, right puts the product in the basket and left puts it back.

Walking around inside a house, a venue or a store

  • D-pad or left stick to the sides: go to the next room, hall or aisle. A or Cross: use the spot where you are. B or Circle: leave.
  • X or Square: what you have in the basket, in a food store. On the keyboard it is C.
  • View button, or Create or Share: where you are and what you can do there.
  • While you cook, A or Cross skips the wait.

On foot

  • Left stick: walk in the direction you push it; all the way, run.
  • Right stick: directional scanner. Push it to one side and it tells you what is there.
  • A or Cross: interact. Y or Triangle: get into a vehicle. X or Square: reload. B or Circle: crouch.
  • Right trigger or R2: attack. Left trigger or L2: lock on to the enemy. LB and RB, or L1 and R1: change weapon.
  • D-pad up: where am I. D-pad down: status. D-pad to the sides: radar.
  • Press the left stick: repeat the GPS. Press the right one: first aid kit.
  • View or Share button: phone. Menu or Options button: pause. Touchpad: map explorer.

Combinations: a second function on each button

It works the same with an Xbox controller as with a PlayStation one. The View button on Xbox, which is the small one on the left, next to the Xbox button; or Create or Share on PlayStation, does two things. Pressed and released, it opens the phone, as always. Held, it turns the other buttons into a second function: hold View and press another button.

On foot, holding View:

  • A or Cross: walk on your own following the GPS. B or Circle: talk to the person next to you.
  • X or Square: GPS to the last thing the radar said. Y or Triangle: where your vehicle is, and set it on the GPS.
  • D-pad up: the next intersection. D-pad down: hunger, thirst, sleep and hygiene.
  • D-pad left: list of places. D-pad right: the music player with headphones, turn on and next station.
  • LB or L1: which way you are facing. RB or R1: the time. Press the left stick: cancel the GPS. Press the right one: list of weapons.
  • Left trigger or L2: surrender to the police, and lower your hands. Right trigger or R2: the actions menu.
  • Share button, on the Xbox controllers that have it, or touchpad or microphone button, on PlayStation: how the player next to you is dressed and, on a server with roleplay channels, their character sheet. If your controller doesn't have that button, it is also in the actions menu.

At the wheel, holding View:

  • A or Cross: the GPS autopilot, on and off. B or Circle: the speed.
  • X or Square: repeat the GPS direction, with the meters left to arrive. Y or Triangle: the mission objective, with the distance.
  • D-pad up: the next intersection. D-pad down: hunger, thirst, sleep and hygiene.
  • D-pad left: turn off the radio. D-pad right: next station.
  • LB or L1: GPS to the last thing the radar said. RB or R1: which way you are heading. Press the left stick: cancel the GPS. Press the right one: the time.
  • Left trigger or L2: surrender to the police, with the vehicle stopped. Right trigger or R2: the actions menu.
  • Share button, on the Xbox controllers that have it, or touchpad or microphone button, on PlayStation: list of places. It is also in the actions menu.

While you hold View, the other buttons don't do their normal function: you don't brake or shoot by accident. All the combinations can be changed in Options, Gamepad: Reassign the on-foot and driving combinations.

The actions menu

The controller has fewer buttons than the keyboard has keys. Whatever doesn't fit on a button or a combination is in the actions menu: hold View, or Create or Share, and press the right trigger, or R2. It works on foot and at the wheel. It is a menu like the others: you move with the D-pad, choose with A or Cross, go back with B or Circle, and Y or Triangle explains the option. Only the actions that apply at that moment appear, and the ones that best fit the situation come first.

  • On duty as a police officer: order suspects to stop. Someone who surrenders is handcuffed with A or Cross, next to them.
  • Shout to scare off pedestrians, rob the pedestrian next to you, take cover and slow time.
  • On a server: speak in character, describe what your character is doing, type a message, check how the player next to you is dressed and read their character sheet, and pull another player out of their vehicle. When playing with friends, type a message.
  • Lock on to anyone, the list of weapons and the first aid kit, which have no button at the wheel, and your substances with adult content.
  • If you have a dog: have it sniff around, and its menu.
  • At the wheel, in free driving: straighten the vehicle. At sea or in an emergency vehicle: fish or the siren.
  • Objective, back to the mission objective on the GPS and the radar, bring back the last destination you cleared from the GPS, list of places, map explorer, description of the place, where your vehicle is, where you are with coordinates and the radar filter.
  • Repeat the last message. To review the previous ones, inside this menu or any other, LB and RB, or L1 and R1.
  • Switch between assisted and free driving, and between compass and turns; the GPS beacon; quick save; turn the music down and up, without the menu closing; and the mark in the session log.

That way, everything you do with the keyboard can be done with the controller. If you change the combinations and leave the actions menu without a button, you can put it back in Options, Gamepad.

Typing with the controller

When the game asks for text, such as a message for other players, a vehicle's name or your character sheet, with the controller you type with a letter wheel:

  • D-pad or left stick, up and down: go through the letters, and the game says each one. Holding the stick moves through them quickly.
  • D-pad right: types the chosen letter. D-pad left: deletes the last one.
  • LB or L1: a space. RB or R1: switches between lower case, upper case, numbers and symbols.
  • Y or Triangle: reads what you have typed. View button, or Create or Share: says all these buttons.
  • A or Cross: accept. B or Circle: cancel.

So you don't have to type, in some places there are suggestions: X or Square moves from one to the next and types it in, and with A or Cross you accept it as it is or after editing it. On the keyboard you do it with Tab. There are ready-made phrases when typing a message to the other players, speaking in character, describing an action and speaking out of character; and random names, different each time, when naming a vehicle. The phrases and names can be changed with the mod editor.

At the wheel, by boat and by helicopter

  • Right trigger or R2: accelerate. Left trigger or L2: brake and reverse. B or Circle: handbrake.
  • Left stick to the sides: a tap turns on the turn signal in assisted driving; in free driving, in boats and in helicopters it is the steering wheel.
  • Right stick to the sides: change lanes. Flying a helicopter or a light plane, right climbs and left descends. Up: next station. Down: fish at sea or siren.
  • On a flight crossing: D-pad up and down, altitude; D-pad left and right, heading; X or Square, autopilot; A or Cross, how the flight is going; Y or Triangle, speed up; LB or L1, repeat the tower; RB or R1, or B or Circle, the flight menu; and the View, Create or Share button, the help.
  • X or Square: horn. Y or Triangle: get out. A or Cross: interact and, with the line cast, reel in.
  • LB or L1: lock on to a target. RB or R1: shoot. Press the left stick: U-turn. Press the right one: job.

Vibration

The controller vibrates when you are hurt, in crashes and explosions, when your wanted level goes up, when you reach a destination and, above all, when a fish bites. In free driving, the curb vibrates on the matching side. Vibration reinforces the sound, it never replaces it, and it can be turned off.

The buttons, as the game says them

With a controller connected, what the game tells you while you play, and what the tutorials and scenes explain, names the button that does each thing instead of the key: «press the left trigger to lock on to it», instead of «press Q». It follows your mapping: if you change a button, the game says the new one. What is on a combination is said with it: «with the View button held plus the left trigger», or «with the Create or Share button held plus the L2 button» on a PlayStation controller. What has no button or combination is still said with its key, because the keyboard works the same with the controller connected: that is in the actions menu. When walking around a store, when cooking, on a server's waiting screen and when typing, the game also says your controller's buttons. The lines that change are read by your screen reader, not the recorded voice. You turn it off in Options, Gamepad, with Say the gamepad buttons instead of the keys. The menus and this help still name the keys; F1, or the Y button in a menu, says what each button does.

Changing the buttons

In Options, Gamepad, you can see what each button does, reassign any of them on foot and at the wheel, test the vibration and go back to the original mapping. The mapping is saved with your options.

7. Driving

Assisted driving, the default option

The car follows its lane on its own. You decide the speed and where to turn. Before each intersection the game tells you which street you are crossing; if you want to turn, press left or right and you will hear the turn signal. If you press nothing, you go straight on.

  • Proximity sensor: a beep that speeds up when there is a vehicle in front of you.
  • Automatic braking: the car brakes on its own so as not to ram the one in front. Hold A to overtake using the oncoming lane. It can be turned off.
  • GPS autopilot, with Y: the car drives itself. Set a destination on the GPS, get into the car and press Y: it accelerates to city speed, brakes if there is a vehicle in front, waits at red lights, turns at intersections following the route and stops when it arrives, and says so: that way you can go in, deliver or carry on with the mission without braking yourself. It stops at the traffic light line whatever the vehicle, including a bus or a truck, which brake worse: it starts braking earlier the heavier and faster it is, and if the light is going to turn red before it has time to cross, it stays put. If you are in a hurry, the autopilot goes faster: with the siren on or in a mission against the clock, it also doesn't wait at traffic lights; in a job against the clock, such as the bus, the taxi or a delivery, it does wait, because running a red light with the police in front would cost you a fine and license points. You don't have to touch anything. With any arrow or with Space you take control while you hold it, and with Y again you turn it off. It works in assisted driving and on land, with any land vehicle, including the bicycle. If you leave it on in Options, when you get into a vehicle the game reminds you that it is on and why it doesn't start: because you are in free driving or because no destination is set. For chases and races you still need to drive yourself.

Free driving

You hold the steering wheel. A low rumble warns you, on the matching side, when you go off the roadway. E straightens the car toward the nearest compass point. You can go off-road through parks and beaches.

Where you left the vehicle

The vehicle you get out of waits for you where you left it, however far you go. If later you don't remember where it was, press Shift L, or open the phone and choose Where is my vehicle: the game says how many meters away it is and on which street, and sets it on the GPS. If you go to another town without it, by train, by bus or by plane, it stays where it was: from outside, the game tells you in which town and on which street you left it, and when you come back it is still there.

This applies to the last vehicle you used, whoever it belongs to. If it is just any vehicle, you lose it when you take another one. The ones that are yours are never lost: the next section explains this.

My vehicles: yours are never lost

A vehicle is yours when you buy it at a dealership, when you fit an upgrade at a repair shop, when you take it out of a garage or when you give it a name. This applies to any land vehicle and to those that fly, including work vehicles, such as an ambulance or a taxi you have upgraded. Boats are separate, moored at their pier. Mission vehicles cannot be yours: the repair shop tells you so before charging you anything.

  • A vehicle of yours doesn't disappear when you move away or take another one. It stays where you park it, in whatever town, and it is saved with the game: when you load it, or when you go back to that town, it is still in its place, with its condition, its upgrades, its fuel and its name.
  • On the phone, the My vehicles section lists them all: name, model, where it is, condition and upgrades. For those on the street it says the street and how many meters away; for those in another town, the town and the street; for those stored away, which garage.
  • With Enter on a vehicle you choose what to do: mark it on the GPS, have it brought to you, send it to a garage or rename it. Bringing it costs 150 euros and sending it to a garage, 100, wherever the vehicle is, including in another town.
  • The last vehicle you used also appears in the list even if it isn't yours, with the warning that it is lost if you take another one. Right there you can put it in your name.
  • Six of your vehicles fit on the street. When you go over six, your mechanic takes the oldest one to the garage of one of your homes, and tells you. If there is no room in any garage, the game warns you and none is lost: sell one or buy a home with a garage.
  • If you are knocked out or arrested in another town and you had a vehicle of yours, it stays parked where it was.
  • A vehicle of yours can still be wrecked: if it blows up, it is lost, along with its upgrades, unless you have insurance, which the next section explains.
  • Games saved with earlier versions load as usual. If they had a vehicle with repair shop upgrades, it becomes yours.

Insurance for your vehicles

A car with every upgrade still catches fire if it takes too many hits or shots. When the game warns you that smoke is coming from the engine, take it to a repair shop, which leaves it as good as new. And if you never want to lose it, take out insurance.

  • You take it out on the phone, in My vehicles: it is the option after the list, Vehicle insurance. Enter takes it out, and Enter again cancels it. The option says how much you pay per day and how much you have paid so far.
  • There is only one, and it covers all your land vehicles, including the ones you make yours later. It doesn't cover aircraft, which go to their hangar, or boats.
  • It covers you from the day after you take it out: taking it out with the car already on fire doesn't work.
  • Every midnight it charges 12 euros for each vehicle of yours that is on the street, from your pocket or, if that isn't enough, from the bank. The ones you have in the garage don't pay. If one day you have nothing to pay with, your insurance is canceled and the game says so.
  • If a vehicle of yours explodes, the insurance tow truck takes it away. The next day they return it to you repaired, with all its upgrades, in a garage of yours with room; if you have no room, at the door of the nearest repair shop in the town you are in.
  • When they return it you pay the deductible: ten percent of what that model costs at the dealership, and at least 150 euros. If you don't have it, they keep it for you until you do, even if you have canceled the insurance.
  • Without insurance, when a vehicle of yours explodes, the game tells you it has been lost and that with insurance it would have been returned to you.
  • On an online server there is no insurance, because the vehicles on the street are handled by the server.
  • The fee and the deductible are adjusted in the mod editor, in My vehicles and driver.

Upgrading your vehicles

At any repair shop, besides repairing and painting, you can upgrade the vehicle you arrived in. There are seven upgrades. Each one has its levels, and each level costs more than the one before: the second, twice the first, and the third, three times.

  • Engine, three levels: higher top speed and more acceleration. You can hear it, because the engine sounds higher-pitched.
  • Brakes, three levels: it stops in fewer meters.
  • Armor, three levels: it withstands more hits and more shots.
  • Reinforced tires, three levels: in the rain you brake almost as on a dry road and, if you play with breakdowns, you get far fewer flat tires. You can hear it: the wheels sound deeper and with less noise.
  • Reinforced suspension and bumpers, three levels: crashes into walls and other vehicles do less damage to yours, and to you inside. You can hear it: the impacts sound duller and lower.
  • Exhaust silencer, two levels: the engine sounds much quieter. When the police are after you, they hear you from less far away and lose you sooner when you drive off.
  • Bigger fuel tank, two levels: it holds more liters. It only matters if you play with fuel.

The last line of the repair shop menu says which upgrades the vehicle has and at what level.

Upgrades go with that particular vehicle. From the first upgrade, the vehicle is in your name: it no longer disappears even if you leave it on the street and take another one, and you have it on the phone, in My vehicles. This also applies to work vehicles. If you store it in the garage of a home, it keeps its upgrades, also when you ask by phone to have it brought to you. If it gets wrecked, they are lost with it, unless you have vehicle insurance, which returns it to you repaired and with its upgrades.

A mission vehicle, or the one your driver brings when it belongs to the gang, can't be kept: the repair shop says so before charging, and doesn't charge.

At the repair shop you can also give the vehicle a name, for free.

Naming your vehicles

You can name any vehicle of yours. There are three places: in your home menu, Name a vehicle, which works for the one you arrive in and for those in the garage; at the repair shop, Name it; and on the phone, in My vehicles, Rename it. You type up to thirty letters and press Enter. If you don't want to type, Tab suggests random names, different each time; with a controller, X or Square suggests them and you can also type it with the letter wheel, as explained in Playing with a controller. If you leave it empty, it is called by its model again. The game says the name when you get in, followed by the model, when you locate it with Shift L and in all the lists. Naming a vehicle puts it in your name. What you type is not written to the game log.

U-turn

If you go past your destination, the GPS tells you to make a U-turn: brake almost to a stop and press U. With the GPS autopilot, it makes it on its own: it brakes, turns and says so.

Fuel, optional

It is turned off by default. You turn it on in Options, Combat and difficulty: Fuel. With it, each car, motorcycle or truck has its own tank.

  • It burns fuel as it drives, more the faster you go. A full tank lasts about nine kilometers at a normal pace.
  • The V key, at the wheel, says how much is left. When you hit the reserve a warning sounds and the game tells you where the nearest gas station is. With the tank empty, the engine coughs and stops.
  • You refuel at gas stations. There is at least one in each town with cars. Drive up with the vehicle, stop and press Enter, without getting out: fill the tank or put in ten liters. You can also leave the vehicle parked next to it and go in on foot.
  • Gas stations sell ten-liter cans to carry with you. If you are stranded, you pour it in from the phone, in Services, while in the vehicle or next to it.
  • Roadside assistance: on the phone, in Services. It takes half an hour, brings fifteen liters and doesn't come if the police are after you.
  • Vehicles you take on the street have their tank as their owner left it: between half and full. The ones you buy, full.
  • Boats, anything that flies, bicycles and mission vehicles don't use fuel. It doesn't count when playing on a server either.

Breakdowns, optional

It is turned off by default. You turn it on in Options, Combat and difficulty: Breakdowns. With it, each vehicle counts the kilometers it drives.

  • Flat tires: every now and then, a tire bursts. You hear it, and the vehicle can't go above half speed and brakes worse until you change it.
  • There are three ways to fix it. With a spare tire you carry: stop the vehicle, open the phone and, in Services, choose Replace the flat tire; it takes you a quarter of an hour. At any repair shop. Or by calling the tow truck from the phone, which costs twice as much and takes half an hour.
  • Spare tires are sold at repair shops and gas stations.
  • Inspection: every fifty kilometers the game warns you that it is due. You get it at a repair shop. If you skip it, the brakes slowly get worse.
  • The V key, at the wheel, says the kilometers it has done and whether anything is pending. All of this is saved with the vehicle, in the garage and in the saved game.

Traffic rules and the points license, optional

It is turned off by default. You turn it on in Options, Combat and difficulty: Traffic rules.

  • Running a red light, or going more than thirty percent over the limit, which is fifty an hour, for a few seconds, costs a fine and license points. But only if the police see you: a patrol or an officer nearby. With no witnesses, nothing happens.
  • You start with twelve points. A red light takes away four; speeding, three. The fine is taken from what you are carrying and, if that isn't enough, from the bank.
  • With no points, your license is taken away. You can keep driving, but if an officer sees you, they come after you.
  • You get points back with a course at any academy: four hours, and you have eight again.
  • There are no fines with the siren on, when the police are already after you, or during the tutorials. Traffic lights count in assisted driving.
  • The V key says the points you have left.

Getting a vehicle

Find it with the radar, stand next to it and press F. A parked car has to be broken into: it takes a few seconds and the alarm may go off, unless you carry lock picks. If it is moving, you pull the driver out. You can also buy them at a dealership: the ones you buy are yours and are never lost even if you leave them on the street. You can keep them in the garage of your homes. In front of hospitals, police stations, stations and taxi ranks there are service vehicles.

Helicopter

The helicopter flies over buildings, traffic and police. It is flown by hand, like a boat: left and right turn, up accelerates, and the game announces the heading. It doesn't crash into anything, but you can only get out if you are stopped over a street or an open space; the C key tells you what you are over. There are helicopters at the heliports. The same goes for the light plane. Since version 1.7.1 you really fly, with altitude, fuel and crossings between towns: the next section explains it. If you turn off real flight in Options, everything is as before: you always fly at the same altitude and, at a heliport, Enter takes you to another town in one jump, free with your own aircraft, or paying for a flight if you are on foot.

Flying for real: altitude, fuel and crossings

With the Real flight option, which is on by default and is in Options, Movement and driving, a helicopter or a light plane take off, have altitude, burn fuel and really travel from one town to another. Everything is done by ear. The whole explanation is also inside the game: at any heliport, airport or airfield, How flying works.

Taking off, altitude and landing:

  • When you get in with F you are on the ground. Up Arrow takes off. The helicopter climbs vertically. The light plane builds up speed down the runway: hold the arrow until it lifts off; if you let go earlier, it doesn't take off.
  • In the air, D climbs and A descends. With a controller, the right stick to the sides: right climbs and left descends.
  • Altitude tone: a note plays every fifty meters you climb or descend, higher-pitched the higher you are. The game says the altitude every hundred meters.
  • Below 150 meters you are flying low, and everything is as usual. Above that you are flying high: the ground police lose sight of you, and the game tells you so. Each aircraft has its ceiling: the helicopter, 900 meters; the light plane, 2600.
  • To land, slow down and press F: the aircraft descends on its own, you hear the altitude tone going down, and when it touches the ground you get out. You don't need to descend first with A.
  • A helicopter can set down at a heliport, on a street or in an open space; not on a building. A light plane needs the runway of an airport or an airfield: anywhere else the game warns you that it will be a forced landing and, if you press F again, you do it, with damage to the aircraft.

Fuel:

  • Each aircraft has its own tank. When full, the helicopter covers about 260 kilometers and the light plane about 520. Flying over a town uses little; what uses fuel is the crossings.
  • You refuel with Enter at a heliport, an airport or an airfield, on board the aircraft, and also inside the hangar. The menu says how much is left and what it costs to fill it up.
  • The game warns you when you hit the reserve, at twenty percent, and sounds the cockpit alarm when eight percent is left.
  • If it runs out, the engine stops and you glide: you steer the aircraft with left and right and set down wherever you get to. Over a street or an open space it is a forced landing; over a building you crash, the aircraft is badly damaged and you come out injured.
  • An aircraft of yours with no fuel isn't left stranded: on the phone, in My vehicles, you can ask for half a tank to be brought to it.
  • It can be turned off in Options, Aircraft burn fuel.

The crossing to another town starts in two ways: by flying to the edge of the town, or with Enter at a heliport, an airport or an airfield, on board, choosing Flight plan. In the flight plan you choose where to go and how you arrive. For each town it says the kilometers, the heading and the minutes, and warns you if you don't have enough fuel. There are two ways to arrive at each place:

  • Land at the airfield: you end up on the ground, at the heliport or on the runway, with the tower guiding you.
  • Fly in low over the town: you end up in the air, on the side you come from, and you set down wherever you like. To an island that has no runway, a light plane can only arrive this way.

During the crossing the aircraft has a position in the region, a heading, altitude and speed, and there is wind, different on each flight and stronger when it rains. The autopilot is on by default and does everything: it climbs to cruising level, corrects for the wind, gives way to traffic and, with assisted landing, descends and lands. You can speed it up. A forty-kilometer trip by helicopter lasts just over a minute, and sped up, less than half a minute; the game clock moves forward by as long as the flight really takes, a quarter of an hour in that case. The crossing keys:

  • Up Arrow and Down Arrow: climb and descend a hundred meters.
  • Left Arrow and Right Arrow: turn ten degrees. They turn off the autopilot.
  • Tab: turn the autopilot on or off.
  • Enter: how the flight is going: the distance left, the heading, whether you are lined up, the altitude, the wind and the fuel. Any other key says the same.
  • N: speed up the crossing or leave it at its own pace. While something is happening it goes at its own pace, and afterwards it carries on sped up by itself.
  • Home: repeat the last thing the tower said.
  • D or Escape: the flight menu: change destination, also to divert to a closer airfield; how you want to arrive; which airfield, if the town has more than one; and assisted landing.
  • F1: help for the crossing.

What you hear: the aircraft's engine and the wind; the radio before each message from the tower; the altitude tone; the cockpit alarm when there is danger; and, flying by hand or when approaching the airfield, the alignment tone: two clicks that sound toward the side you have to turn to. When they sound in the center and higher-pitched, you are on the right heading, which already accounts for the wind.

What happens in the air, different on each flight:

  • Turbulence: the aircraft is shaken about for a few seconds.
  • Traffic: the tower warns you of another aircraft at your same altitude, and you have to climb or descend a hundred meters. The autopilot does it on its own. By hand, if you don't do it, the other one passes very close and you get a penalty.
  • A storm on your heading: the tower warns you in good time and a menu opens for you to decide. Go around it, with a few extra kilometers; go over it, if your aircraft's ceiling allows it, like the light plane's; or fly through it, with strong turbulence and damage. There is at most one per crossing, and they are more likely when it rains.
  • When you pass near another town, the game says which one and on which side it is.

Flying low on a crossing, below 150 meters, has its price: the autopilot doesn't do it, so you fly by hand; the aircraft goes slower and uses more fuel; and every so often the alarm goes off because of an obstacle, a power line or a hill, and you have four seconds to climb with Up Arrow or you clip it. It is useful for one thing: keeping off the radar.

Arriving: at six kilometers the destination tower gives you the approach. With assisted landing, the aircraft descends and lines up on its own, even if you are flying by hand. Without it, and without the autopilot, you do it yourself: descend to 200 meters or less and keep the clicks in the center. If you come in too high or crooked, the tower tells you to go around; the second time, it guides you itself. If you come in just right, you land, but the landing is hard and the aircraft pays for it.

If you run out of fuel in the middle of a crossing, the engine stops and you glide about ten kilometers for every thousand meters of altitude. If the airfield is within your reach, you make it. If not, you set down where you can: the aircraft is badly damaged, you are injured, and you end up on the outskirts of the nearest town.

Hangars

At each heliport, airport and airfield you can have a hangar space. It is in the airfield's menu, on foot or on board.

  • Renting a space costs 120 euros a day, charged every midnight. Buying it costs 26,000 euros, and then there is no rent.
  • Each space holds two aircraft. A light plane, which needs a runway, doesn't fit in a heliport's hangar.
  • Storing: take the aircraft to the airfield and choose Store in the hangar. Taking it out: in the same menu, on the aircraft, Take it out to the runway; the GPS marks it for you.
  • Inside the hangar you refuel, service and repair it, paying according to what it needs, and you can rename it.
  • What is in a hangar is never lost. If one day you don't have enough for the rent, the debt is recorded: your aircraft stay there, but you aren't allowed to take them out until you pay.
  • Your aircraft appear on the phone, in My vehicles, with the others. Those in the hangar say which airfield they are at, and you can mark it on the GPS. One that is on the street, here or in another town, can be sent to a hangar of yours for 250 euros: a company pilot takes it there. An aircraft isn't brought to your door, like a car: you go and get it at the airfield.
  • A home's garage no longer takes aircraft: their place is the hangar. Any you had stored in a garage are still there and can be taken out.

Hijacking an aircraft, and the radar

Stealing a parked helicopter was already possible. Hijacking an airliner is something else, and it is a major crime. At a heliport, an airport or an airfield, on foot and with a firearm in your hand, the airfield's menu has the option Hijack the airliner that is about to depart. The pilot jumps out onto the runway and the aircraft is yours: a helicopter at a heliport, a light plane at an airport or an airfield.

  • You get wanted level 4 and a serious criminal record entry, if records are turned on. If you have the police helicopter turned on, your wanted level rises to the level at which it comes out, and it comes after you.
  • From that moment the radar tracks you. While you fly above 150 meters, the radar sees you: you can't shake off the police, and if you land at a heliport or an airport, they are waiting for you.
  • To lose the radar, fly below 150 meters: twenty-five seconds in a row over a town, or twelve on a crossing. The game tells you. If you climb again, it sees you again.
  • Where to land: far from airfields. In the flight plan, choose to fly in low over the town and set down on a street or in a vacant lot. At an airfield, even if the radar has lost you, someone recognizes the aircraft.
  • A hijacked aircraft can't be yours or be stored in a hangar. If you leave it and walk away, it disappears.
  • The job: every day, at each airfield, someone pays between 3500 and 7000 euros for the aircraft delivered next to a discreet spot in another town. It is chosen when you hijack. They only pay if you set down there without the radar on you. The O key reminds you where, and the flight plan puts it first.
  • At each airfield, one hijacking a day. On an online server and when playing with friends it isn't possible, and the game says so.

Commercial pilot

Flying can also be a legal job. At an academy you get the Commercial pilot license: six sessions, one a day, like the other qualifications. With it, the menu of every heliport, airport and airfield has Commercial flights.

  • Each airfield offers three flights a day, different each day and at each airfield: passengers or cargo to another town, with their kilometers and their pay, which grows with the distance.
  • If you have an aircraft of yours at the airfield that is suitable for that route, you fly with it and get a twenty-five percent bonus. If not, the company leaves you one on the runway, which you return when you arrive.
  • You take off, make the crossing, which the flight plan puts first for you, and land at the airfield for the job. You get paid when you touch down. The O key reminds you where you are going.
  • If the flight has been smooth, there is a ten percent bonus. If it has been rough, because you flew into a storm, had a scare with traffic or made missed approaches, they deduct the same.
  • It is legal money, and each completed flight raises your prestige a little. If the police are after you, the company takes the flight away from you.
  • On an online server there are no commercial flights.

On an online server you fly the same way, with altitude, fuel, crossings and hangars; what there isn't is hijackings or commercial flights. Games saved with earlier versions just load: aircraft start on the ground and with a full tank. The numbers and the lines for all of this, how each aircraft flies and where each town is in the region are in the game data, and they can be changed with the mod editor.

8. Combat

  • Q locks on to the nearest enemy. You will hear a tone coming from where they are: low and double if they are within range, high if they are out of range.
  • Space shoots the locked-on enemy. The closer you are, the more you hit.
  • Half a second before shooting at you, the enemy cocks their weapon. Crouch with X so they hit you less.
  • R reloads. Z uses a first aid kit. With low health you will hear your heartbeat.
  • Explosives are thrown toward the locked-on enemy and damage everything around. Don't use them up close.
  • You can also shoot from a vehicle, without getting out: Q locks on to someone near you and Control shoots, with a firearm in your hand. With an automatic weapon, hold Control. I changes weapon without getting out. The game reminds you of this once, when it is relevant: the first time you are at the wheel with a firearm and there are enemies nearby, or they shoot at you. The F1 help at the wheel also says it. With a controller, the reminder and the help name the buttons.
  • In Options you can switch aim assist to manual and choose the difficulty.

If you go down, you wake up in the nearest hospital and pay the bill. If you are arrested, you lose your firearms and pay a fine. The money in the bank is always safe.

Slow time, cover and hit alert

  • Slow time: the Backspace key makes everything go much slower, and sound deeper, as long as you have focus left. It gives you time to hear where the shots come from and to lock onto a target. Focus runs out in about five seconds and recovers by itself in half a minute; another press turns it off. The V key tells you how much you have left. It can't be used with friends connected.
  • Taking cover: with Shift X, if you are right next to a wall or a stopped vehicle, you take cover behind it. In cover you take less than a third of the damage, but your aim is a bit worse. If you move, you leave cover. The same key takes you out of it.
  • Hit alert: every time you hit the target you hear a sharp tap and the gamepad gives a short jolt. When you lock onto a target with Q, the gamepad also lets you know. It can be turned off in Options, Combat and difficulty.
  • If you are crouching without realizing it, you walk very slowly: V tells you so, and so does the game if you try to run.

What each weapon does

In the gun shop, in the phone's inventory and in the list of your weapons, which opens with I, each weapon states its damage and its range. With F1 on it you hear the whole sheet, in words and in figures:

  • Damage: the points each bullet, each blow or each explosion takes away, and whether that is low, medium, high or very high. For comparison: a pedestrian can take 50 points and you, 100.
  • Range: how many meters it reaches, and whether that is short, medium or long. Melee weapons are close combat. For explosives it also says how many meters around the explosion reaches.
  • Rate of fire: how often it fires or strikes. For automatic weapons, how many shots per second.
  • Accuracy: how many shots out of a hundred it hits at close range. At long range it hits less.
  • And the rest: the bullets in the magazine and whether it makes noise, a lot of noise or none.

This also applies to weapons added by mods.

Where each weapon is sold

Almost all weapons are bought at gun shops, but not all of them. Each weapon says in its description where it is sold:

  • At gun shops: the usual ones and, in addition, the burst-fire pistol, the semi-automatic rifle and the grenade launcher.
  • In shops: the fire axe, which is with the hardware. It is a slow melee weapon that hits very hard.
  • At pawn shops, second-hand: the pump-action shotgun and the lever-action rifle. The pawnbroker has them on top of whatever you sell him.

Ammo is bought where you bought the weapon and also at any gun shop. The six new weapons each have their own sound:

  • Fire axe: the whistle of the swing and the dull thud of the blade.
  • Burst-fire pistol: automatic. Hold down fire and it empties the magazine in two seconds, with a dry, very short sound.
  • Pump-action shotgun: a deep bang and, after it, the pump, back and forth. Five shells; it hits harder and reaches farther than the sawed-off shotgun. It can't take a silencer.
  • Lever-action rifle: after each shot you hear the lever, two metal clicks. Seven bullets, accurate and long range.
  • Semi-automatic rifle: one shot each time you pull the trigger, and fast. Ten bullets that reach seventy meters.
  • Grenade launcher: a hollow, tube-like thump. It launches a grenade sixty meters, twice as far as throwing by hand. Like all explosives, don't use it up close.

Enemies who arrive in a vehicle, and what they shout

Not all enemies wait for you on foot. Part of the escort in some contracts, the reinforcements in the last assault when you conquer a neighborhood and the enemies in missions that say so arrive in a vehicle. This is how it sounds:

  • In the distance you hear tires screeching: they have just set off toward you. The game tells you how many are coming in the vehicle.
  • You hear the engine getting closer. With Q you can lock onto the vehicle, just like a person, and shoot at it.
  • If you destroy it before they get out, the ones inside go down with it.
  • When it reaches you and you are going slowly or on foot, it stops, you hear the door and they get out. The game says how many and where, and now you can lock onto them one by one. If you are in a vehicle, it rams you.
  • The vehicle is left empty where it stopped: you can take it.

Many enemies shout a phrase when they see you, with their name first: bodyguards, guards, gang members and officers. It tells you who has seen you and what type they are. Some have better aim than others, and a deeper or higher voice. When playing on a server, mission enemies are always on foot.

Self-defense

Defending yourself is not a crime. If someone attacks you without you having touched them first, whether a mugger, a gang member who has spotted you in the street or anyone who turns on you, you can shoot them and knock them down without it counting: neither the shots nor the kill raise your wanted level. The first time it happens, the game tells you. The same goes for the muggers and gang members of street events: stopping them is not a crime.

  • Who started it counts. If you hit them first, or you mugged them and they fought back, what you did is not self-defense.
  • It doesn't apply to mission enemies, or to those in a neighborhood war: there, you are the one going after them.
  • Explosives are always a crime. And if you hurt a passerby in the fight, that does count.
  • Officers go after them too. If there is a patrol car nearby, or someone calls the police, a patrol arrives with its siren on, the officers get out and go after whoever attacked you, not after you. When they are done, they get back in the car and leave. If they are also after you, each officer goes after whoever is closest.
  • What an officer knocks down doesn't count among your enemies, and leaves no loot.
  • Gangs understand it too. Knocking down a member of a gang who attacked you first costs you very little reputation with them, instead of the four points it costs when you start it.
  • If you are a good person, that is, if your standing with the neighbors reaches 40, gangs treat you differently. When one of them sees you in their neighborhood, they often let you through with a scowl, and the game says so; if you provoke them, they come after you as always. And defending yourself from those who do attack you costs you nothing with their gang and doesn't count toward starting a neighborhood war: everyone knows it was self-defense. On top of that, the police come to help you sooner. Your standing is checked on the phone, in Life, reputation and consequences.

It can be turned off in Options, The street: then everything goes back to how it was before, and any shot and any kill count. On a server with shared police, defending yourself is not a crime either, but the officers are run by the server and don't come to help you.

Ammo and weapons from fallen enemies

A fallen enemy, besides money, sometimes leaves on the ground ammo for the weapon they were carrying, if you carry the same one: between half a magazine and a full one. It is picked up by itself when you walk over it, like money; you hear a magazine going in and the game says how many bullets there are and how many you have. The radar and your dog find it like any other item. It can be turned off in Options, The street.

In that same section there is a difficulty option, off by default: fallen enemies sometimes drop the whole weapon, if you don't have it. You keep it when you walk over it, with whatever bullets it had. It makes the game easier and the gun shop less necessary; that's why you have to turn it on on purpose. On an online server it doesn't apply.

Upgrading your weapons

At any gun shop you can fit two upgrades on each firearm you have:

  • Silencer: shots can no longer be heard from far away, so people don't run and nobody calls the police because of the noise. What you do in front of someone is still seen. Shotguns, machine guns and the crossbow can't take one.
  • Extended magazine: it holds half as many bullets again.

Upgrades go with the weapon: if you are arrested and they take it away, they are lost with it.

Fights at the gym

Besides boxing, gyms have mixed martial arts nights: three rounds in a row in the cage, each against a tougher opponent, with no rest and only with your fists. If you go down in any of them, you lose the night. It pays quite a bit more than boxing, and each night you win raises the prize and the toughness of the next one.

Gym fights are sport: knocking down your opponent is not a crime, doesn't call the police and doesn't lower your standing with the neighbors, whoever is watching. That said, punching someone who was passing by is still assault.

9. The police

Crimes only count if someone sees them. The wanted level goes from 1 to 5. At level 1 they try to arrest you; from 2 upward, they shoot. To get rid of them:

  • Get away from the sirens. When there are no police nearby you hear a deep heartbeat: hold on for a few seconds and they stop looking for you.
  • Repaint the vehicle at a garage.
  • Look after the vehicle: every crash lowers its condition. The game warns you at 50 and 25 percent; at 25 smoke comes out of the engine. At zero it catches fire, and you have about seven seconds to get out with F before it explodes. A garage leaves it like new.
  • Hide in one of your houses, change clothes in a shop or pay a bribe at the police station.
  • Leave the town by road or head out to sea.

Who comes after you at each wanted level

The more they want you, the better armed they come. Each patrol sent after you brings officers of one type, and the type depends on the level:

  • Levels 1 and 2: officers with pistols, two per patrol.
  • Level 3: almost all with pistols, and the odd patrol with a submachine gun.
  • Level 4: no more pistols. Some patrols bring submachine guns and others, combat shotguns. They can take more damage.
  • Level 5: the same and, on top of that, the Borrasca Unit, the police assault unit. The game warns you when you reach this level. They come in a van, which sounds deeper than a patrol car and takes twice as much damage. Three officers with assault rifles get out of each van, with better aim and almost twice the toughness of an ordinary officer.

Officers say who they are when they see you: "Stop, police!", or "Borrasca Unit!". Which type of officer appears at each level is in the game data and can be changed with the mod editor. On a server, the police are run by the server: the officers carry the weapons from this same table, but they always come in a patrol car.

Fine or bribe

If an officer catches you with one or two wanted levels, with no mission in progress and enough money, they don't just arrest you: the game lets you choose.

  • Pay the fine on the spot: your wanted level is cleared, you keep your weapons and you don't go through the police station.
  • Try a bribe: it costs less, but it can go wrong. It works more often the more street reputation you have. If it goes wrong, you lose that money and you are arrested.
  • Let them arrest you: the usual.

First, the officer frisks you. If you are carrying product or something stolen in your backpack, they find it, the game tells you and no fine will do anymore: all that's left is to try the bribe, which then costs more than a fine and works less often, or to let them arrest you.

When you are arrested, at the police station they keep your firearms, all the product you are carrying and the stolen items in your backpack, and the game tells you everything they have confiscated. Found items, like what you bring up diving, are not taken from you, and neither is what you have in the wardrobe of your houses.

The menu comes up by itself, as soon as the officer reaches you, and each option says what it costs. With F1 on the bribe you hear how many times out of a hundred it works at that moment. With three levels or more, or with no money, you are arrested straight away: there is no negotiating when you are very wanted. It can be turned off in Options, Combat and difficulty.

If you want to know what it would cost you before you get caught, go into a police station, walk around the place and read the notice board: it says your wanted level and how much the fine would come to.

Police patrols on the beat

A few patrol cars drive around the streets even when nobody is looking for you, as on online servers: two near you by day and one at night. They drive without sirens, stop at traffic lights and the radar announces them as police. They don't do anything to you, but they see what you do: a crime committed less than eighty meters from a patrol counts even if nobody else is watching. With traffic rules turned on, they are the ones who give you the fines. If you are attacked and one is nearby, it comes. And if you earn a wanted level, the one nearby turns on its siren and comes after you.

They are turned off in Options, The street: then the police only show up when they are looking for you, as before. How many there are is in the game data. On a server with shared police, the server puts them there.

Surrendering

With the police looking for you and an officer or a patrol car nearby, Shift H raises your hands. With a gamepad, the combine button and the left trigger. Stop the vehicle first, if you are in one. The officers stop shooting and come for you: don't move or attack. If you move or attack, the surrender is broken, they start shooting again and for a while they won't trust another one. Another press lowers your hands.

Surrendering keeps you from being killed, but it isn't free, and it costs more the more they want you. When you raise your hands, the game tells you what it is going to cost you:

  • At level 1 or 2 it is an ordinary arrest: if you have the option turned on and money, you can settle it with the fine or the bribe.
  • From level 3 there is no deal. The fine is 300 euros per level plus five percent of what you are carrying per level; the cell, eight more hours for each level from the second onward, that is, 16 hours at level 3, 24 at level 4 and 32 at level 5; and you lose your firearms, as in any arrest.
  • At level 5 you also lose all your other weapons and the vest.

Anyone who isn't police, like a gang member, doesn't understand hands up and carries on with what they're doing. On a server with shared police there is no surrender: there, the police are run by the server.

The police helicopter, optional

It comes turned off, because it makes level 5 quite a bit harder. It is turned on in Options, The street. With it on, about twenty seconds after reaching level 5 a helicopter arrives:

  • You hear it coming from far away and circling above you, with an engine deeper and bigger than that of a civilian helicopter. With the surround sound engine, it sounds overhead.
  • When it spots you, it speaks to you through the loudspeaker: you hear the feedback and the game says what it announces. It repeats it every half minute.
  • As long as it has you spotted you can't lose the police, however far away the patrol cars are.
  • To make it lose you: on foot, stick close to a building front, a little over a meter from the wall, and hold on for eight seconds. While it can't see you, you hear, higher pitched, the same heartbeat as when the police are looking for you and can't find you, and the game tells you when it has lost you. Or go into any building, whether a business, your house, a garage or a parking garage: going in makes it lose you instantly. Open-air places, like a monument or a dock, don't count.
  • When it loses you, it circles looking for you for a little over half a minute. If during that time you come out into the open just as it saw you, on foot or in the same vehicle, it finds you in a few seconds. If you come out in a vehicle different from the ones it has seen you in, it doesn't recognize you. But if an officer on the ground has you in front of them, they tell it where you are.
  • If it doesn't find you, it withdraws, and then the police lose you as usual. If you are still at level 5, another one comes back after a minute and a half.
  • It can be shot down: lock onto it with Q and shoot. Pistols, submachine guns and assault rifles do nothing to it, and the game tells you so. You need a weapon with a lot of damage per bullet that can reach it: a rifle, a carbine or a lever-action rifle; or an explosive fired at it, like a rocket or the grenade launcher, which explodes up there, wherever it is.
  • Shooting it down has consequences: it falls in flames and blows up whatever is under it, so get out of the way; for two minutes the police take twice as long to forget you; you gain street reputation and lose it with the neighbors; and the next time you are arrested, or surrender, you will pay 2000 euros more for each helicopter.

On a server with shared police it doesn't appear. Its numbers and what it says through the loudspeaker are in the game data.

Ambulances

A while after a shootout or a hit-and-run an ambulance arrives: you hear the siren getting closer, it pulls up to the sidewalk next to the injured, the paramedics treat them and they leave. While the shooting goes on, it waits. It is just ambience: it doesn't chase you, it brakes if you are in front of it and it doesn't let you get in while they work. It is turned off in Options, The street. Only when playing solo.

10. The phone

With M you open the phone: current objective, available missions, places and GPS, map explorer, personal markers, where is my vehicle, my vehicles, services, your career, life, reputation and consequences, skills and achievements, inventory, properties, neighborhoods and statistics.

Life, reputation and consequences is where you check how the city sees you, how the street sees you and what you gain or lose because of it, along with your criminal record, your contract, your studies, your businesses, your loans, what you are going to be charged and the matters waiting for a decision from you. It is explained in the section on honest life.

With a mission in progress, Available missions starts with it: it says where its current objective is, and Enter takes you back to it, not to the place where it started. The phone also has Back to the objective, which does the same as Shift O.

In Available missions, the summary of each mission says whether you are given a vehicle, whether you have to take a specific one, whether yours will do, whether you have to go in one or whether there is a vehicle chase. You hear the same thing when you reach the contact's door, before pressing Enter to accept it.

My vehicles lists all of yours, whether they are on the street, in another town or in a garage, and lets you mark them on the GPS, bring them to you, send them to a garage and rename them. It is explained in the section on driving, in My vehicles: yours are never lost.

In Services you can ask for a vehicle from your garages to be brought to you, send the vehicle you are driving or the one you have just parked nearby to one of your garages, for 100 euros, ask for a driver from your gang, call a bodyguard, ask for an ambulance to heal you wherever you are or a taxi to travel fast.

In Services you can also dismiss your bodyguards when you no longer want them: they stop charging you.

The journal: what has happened, replaying and skipping missions

  • Journal, on the phone, says at the top how far you are in the story and what comes next. Below are the decisions you have made and the missions you have done, from the latest to the first.
  • With Enter on a mission you have already done you can replay it: it takes you to the place where it starts. Replaying it is just for fun: it gives no reward and doesn't change the story.
  • If you have gone two days or more without playing, when you load the game it reminds you where you were.
  • If you fail the same mission three times, Skip this mission appears in the failed mission menu: it counts as done, with no reward, and the story goes on. You can replay it later from the journal.

11. Traveling between towns

  • By road: drive a vehicle to one of the town's exits and, without getting out, press Enter; a menu with the road opens, and with another Enter you take it. You arrive with your vehicle. Any car, motorcycle or truck will do. You don't need to hug the sidewalk or hit an exact point: when you reach the exit with the vehicle, the game says "you're there now" and which key to press. You can't leave by road on foot: if you try, the game tells you and, if you were traveling with the GPS, guides you to the bus station.
  • By bus: walk to a station and buy a ticket.
  • By helicopter: from a heliport, flying it yourself or paying for the flight.
  • By boat: at a marina you can rent a speedboat, set sail in one of your boats or steal one. At sea, get close to a dock and press Enter to moor.

If you mark a place in another town on the GPS, it guides you to the right exit, station or marina and carries on when you get there. If you are at the wheel, it always takes you by road as long as you can get there that way, even if you have to cross another town on the way: at each exit, Enter, and the GPS continues on the other side. On foot it takes you to the bus. When you reach the exit, the station or the marina, the GPS says what you have to do there, and G repeats it.

Monteferro, the mining city, is in the mountains, beyond Villaseca; Fuentefría is even higher up. The Neptuno Platform is in the middle of the sea, to the southwest.

Isla Tortuga can only be reached by sea or by helicopter. The Mar de Poniente has the coast to the north, with Puerto Bravo to the west, Cala Brava in the center and San Lucero to the east; the island lies to the south.

The taxi and bus ride

When you take a taxi or a bus, you hear the trip: the engine, the road and the radio station the driver has on. A taxi takes between twelve and forty-five seconds depending on the distance, and a bus, about fifty. If you don't want to wait, press Enter and you arrive instantly; any other key tells you how much is left. If you prefer to always arrive at once, turn off the option Taxi and bus rides with a route, in Options, Movement and driving.

Helicopter flights with a pilot also have a ride, with the sound of the rotor. If you fly the helicopter yourself, they don't.

Tram: for getting around within the city

The four cities, Puerto Bravo, San Lucero, Monteferro and Bahía del Ocaso, have a tram line that goes around the city, with stops in every neighborhood: sixteen in Puerto Bravo. Each stop sounds with two rings of a bell and appears in the list of places and on the radar.

  • Walk to a stop and press Enter. The menu offers you all the other stops, from nearest to farthest, and says how many stops there are to each one. The ticket costs two euros.
  • During the trip, the tram stops along the way and announces each stop and the next one. It takes the shorter way round the line.
  • You get off wherever you want: with Enter you get off at the next stop, even if it isn't the one you chose. Any other key tells you which is the next one and how far it is.
  • With the rides with a route option turned off, you arrive at the chosen stop instantly.

The tram doesn't leave the city. There are none in the towns or on the islands.

The tracks, and the tram in the street

The tram tracks run down the middle of the roadway on some streets and avenues, forming a circuit that passes by all the stops. Trams run on them all the time: three in Puerto Bravo, two in the other cities.

  • You hear the tram coming from wherever it is: an electric hum that gets closer, passes and fades away. At each stop it halts for a few seconds and rings the bell when it arrives and when it pulls away.
  • The tracks can be crossed on foot, like any street. When you step on them, the game says Tram tracks, just as it says Sidewalk or Road, and your footsteps sound different: a metallic tap, from the rail, with gravel underneath. When you step off them it says Road again.
  • If you are on the tracks and a tram is coming, the driver rings the bell nonstop and the game warns you which way it is coming from. Get onto the sidewalk. If you don't move, it runs you over: you lose health and it knocks you onto the sidewalk.
  • The tram doesn't ram a vehicle: if you drive along the tracks with it behind you, it brakes, waits and rings the bell until you clear them. There is only a crash if you are the one who runs into it, and it is a small one.
  • When you get on at a stop, the tram that carries you is one of those running: when you get off, it continues on its way from there.

Near the airport you can hear, now and then, a plane taking off.

The railway, and the trains that pass

In each town with a train station there is a track that crosses it from side to side, in a straight line, along the street or avenue that runs in front of the station. The station menu tells you which one.

  • About every minute and a half a train comes in at one end of the track, stops at the platform for a few seconds, whistles and leaves at the other end. You hear it coming from wherever it is, louder and louder, and then fading away: you know where the track runs without having set foot on it.
  • The track can be crossed on foot, like a street. When you step on it the game says Train tracks, and your footsteps sound like metal, as on the tram tracks.
  • If you are on the track and the train is coming toward you, it whistles nonstop and the game warns you which way it is coming from. You have a few seconds: get onto the sidewalk. The train is faster and harder than the tram: if it hits you, you lose a lot of health and you are thrown off the track. It leaves a vehicle badly damaged.
  • When stopped at the platform it doesn't run anyone over.
  • When you travel by bus from one town to another, a train almost always overtakes you on the track that runs alongside the road: you hear it pass from one ear to the other.

If you use auto-walk, with U, your character crosses the tracks without stopping: if you hear the bell, stop with any arrow key.

Train: for going to other towns

Each town that can be reached by road has its train station, which is a different place from the bus station and sounds with the puffing of a locomotive.

  • From a train station you can go to all the others. The neighboring towns come first, as they are the cheapest: the ticket costs more the farther you go.
  • The train doesn't reach the islands or the platform: there is sea in between.
  • The trip has a ride: the whistle on departure and the rattle of the carriage. It lasts longer the farther you go, and with Enter you arrive right away.

It works the same for towns added by mods.

Airports and airfields: fly anywhere

The four cities have an airport on the outskirts, in one corner of the city. The towns that can be reached by road have an airfield: a short dirt strip, also on the outskirts. Both sound with the public address chime: three descending notes.

  • Passenger plane from one airport to another, for 180 euros. It is for long trips between cities. It has a ride: the takeoff and then the hum of the cabin.
  • Scheduled light plane, with a pilot, to the towns: from an airport or from another airfield, for 120 euros. And from a town's airfield, to any city or any other town. It is the flight you get whenever the strip at one of the two ends is short.
  • Scheduled helicopter, with a pilot, to the islands and the platform, where there is no room for a strip: it lands at their heliport. It leaves from any airport or airfield.
  • From an island's heliport, as a passenger, you can fly back to the cities' heliports and the towns' airfields.
  • At airfields only light planes can be rented; rental helicopters are at the airports.
  • Rent a light plane, for 600 euros, or a helicopter, for 900. They leave it for you on the runway, next to the terminal, with the GPS marking it: get in with F and fly it yourself.
  • If you arrive flying a light plane or a helicopter, with Enter at the airport you get the airfield menu: the flight plan to go to any other town, fuel and the hangar. With real flight turned off in Options, you fly in one jump to any other airport, airfield or heliport, for free.
  • The menu of each airport, airfield and heliport also has the hangar, commercial flights for those who have a pilot license, and How flying works.

The Gaviota light plane can also be bought, where the helicopter is. It is flown the same way: by hand, above buildings and traffic, and it only lands on clear ground. It is faster and cheaper.

Swimming and diving

At sea you no longer have to stay on board all the time:

  • With the boat stopped, F drops you into the water. The boat stays where it is.
  • You swim with the arrow keys, slowly, and you hear each stroke. Swimming uses up energy; staying still, hardly any. The V key tells you how much you have left. If you run out of energy, a fishing boat picks you up half drowned, as in a shipwreck.
  • X dives you under to swim underwater and brings you back up. Underwater everything sounds muffled and with bubbles, and you can hold your breath for about thirty seconds. While diving, you sometimes find something of value on the bottom, like some old coins or a doubloon: it goes into your backpack, if it fits, and can be sold at a pawn shop. If you run out of air, you come up swallowing water and lose health.
  • To get back on the boat, go up to it and press F. If you swim to a dock, you get out onto land.

12. Making a living

  • Missions: marked with a warm, pulsing sound. Press Enter at the place.
  • Vehicle jobs, with J: taxi, ambulance, bus, patrol car, truck, ice cream van and, at sea, courier work with any boat.
  • Trucking, step by step: get into a truck and press J. The load is already inside: you don't have to pick anything up. The job is to take it to a shop in another town, and the game says which. The GPS guides you by road to the town's exit; there, without getting out, press Enter twice to take the road, and on the other side the GPS carries on by itself. If the destination isn't the neighboring town, you cross the one in between the same way. When you stop in front of the destination, the load is delivered by itself, you get paid and you are given another job. Don't take the bus or get out of the truck: if you leave it, the service ends. If you clear the GPS by accident, G marks the destination again, and O reminds you of the job. With the GPS autopilot, the Y key, the truck drives itself to each exit.
  • Tavern job boards: contracts of many kinds, harder and better paid each time. It is explained further down, in The job board.
  • Robberies: besides shops and banks, you can rob any business that has a till. It is explained further down, in Robbing any business.
  • Police: with the mode turned on in the options, you can join the force and work as an officer. It is explained further down, in Working as a police officer.
  • Car orders: at a scrapyard, on foot, they ask you for a specific model.
  • Boxing: at gyms, fist fights with a growing prize.
  • Shooting range: at gun shops. A target sounds on one side and you press that arrow key.
  • Street events: now and then there is a mugging, some gang members or a lost briefcase nearby. Helping is optional and has a reward.
  • Delivering pizzas: at Vesubio Pizzeria.
  • Fishing: at sea with P, or from the breakwaters. It is sold at fish markets and fishmongers.
  • Scrapyards: they buy any vehicle.
  • Races: on the Industrial Estate Straight, in San Lucero, in Villaseca and at the regatta buoy.
  • Businesses: buy them and collect every midnight. You don't need to go and get the money, unless you prefer to. There are three ways to collect, and you switch from one to another on the phone, in Properties, or in the menu of any of your businesses:
    • It is brought to you in person: this is the default. Every midnight it reaches your pocket, wherever you are.
    • It is deposited in the bank: it goes into your account, where it also earns interest. To spend it you withdraw it at a bank or an ATM.
    • You go and collect it yourself: it stays in the till of each business. When you go into the business, Collect the till gives you whatever is there, with ten percent more for coming in person. Each till holds seven days; anything beyond that is lost.
    When you change the method, whatever was left in the tills reaches you anyway.
  • Casino with slot machines, blackjack and roulette, bingo and dice.
  • Comarca Lottery, with a draw every day at nine in the evening:
    • Each ticket costs 10 euros and has a five-digit number. The game reads it digit by digit. You can hold up to five tickets per draw.
    • They are bought at lottery offices, where you can ask for a random ticket or type in the number you want, and also in shops, bars and taverns, which have three numbers on display each day.
    • What you buy before nine plays that night. What you buy after, the following night.
    • At nine the game says the winning number, wherever you are, but it doesn't tell you whether you have won: you check that yourself. On the phone, in Lottery, you have the next draw, the jackpot, your tickets, Check your tickets and the numbers of the last seven draws. If you are sleeping or traveling and miss the time, the draw takes place anyway.
    • Prizes go by the last digits that match the winning number, consecutive from the end: the last one, the refund, 10 euros; the last two, 60; three, 600; four, 6000; and all five, the jackpot.
    • The jackpot starts at 20000 euros and grows every day nobody matches all five digits. Sometimes someone in the region wins it: the game says where it fell, and the jackpot starts over.
    • Prizes are collected where tickets are sold, with Check and collect your tickets. Shops, bars and taverns pay up to 600 euros; bigger prizes, only at a lottery office.
    • A winning ticket expires seven days after the draw.
  • Collectibles: cassette tapes, bottles with messages and doubloons, which sound like a tinkle that rises and falls.
  • More jobs with the J key: firefighter with the fire truck, tow truck driver, and courier on a bicycle, scooter or motorcycle.
  • Companion dog: adopt one at an animal shelter. It follows you everywhere, growls when there are enemies nearby and, on foot, with the Y key, searches by scent for the nearest item and shows you the way.
  • Looking after the dog. With Shift + Y, with Enter next to it, or on the phone, in Your dog, its menu opens:
    • The first line says how it is: whether it is hungry and how much it trusts you.
    • Pet: raises its trust little by little.
    • Feed: uses up one portion of dog food. Dog food costs 12 euros and is bought at the animal shelter and in shops. The dog gets hungry as the hours go by: it lets you know with a whimper when it is hungry and, if it runs out of strength, it stops searching until it eats.
    • Send home: it goes to your home and waits for you there, with food and water. You need to have a house. It can also be left there from the house menu.
    • Call: when it is at home, it comes back to you from this same menu or from the house menu.
  • If you hurt the dog, it doesn't turn on you or get lost: it becomes afraid of you. With one hit it yelps and cowers; with two it stops trusting you, follows you from a distance and doesn't warn you about enemies or search. You fix it by making peace: petting, food, a few hours of play or some time at home.
  • Friendly fire. By default, your bullets and explosives also hurt your own side, as in real life. You can hit a bodyguard or a member of your gang twice: they warn you. On the third, in a row, they turn on you; if a couple of minutes go by, they forget it. If you don't want that to be able to happen even by accident, in Options, Combat and difficulty, set Friendly fire to no: then they can't be aimed at or hurt.
  • Turf wars: from the phone you can conquer the neighborhoods of the three cities. There are three waves of gang members, and each conquered neighborhood pays you every midnight. It is explained further down, in Territories at a glance; and in the game, on the phone, Territories, with the first option, How this works.
  • Stock market: at any bank you can buy and sell shares in five companies. You earn by selling for more than you paid, and you lose by selling for less.
    • Prices move every midnight: some companies a little, like Poniente Shipping, and others a lot, like Cala Brava Canned Goods. Now and then a piece of news makes a company jump, up or down. What happens in the story also moves them.
    • Some pay dividends every seven days: a percentage of what each share is worth, which reaches your bank account without selling anything. Each company's sheet says whether it pays and how much.
    • While you own shares, every midnight the game tells you what they are worth, how much they have changed since yesterday and how much you are up or down since you bought them; and if one of your companies has news, it tells you about it.
    • The stock market menu says, for each company, its price, how much it has changed since yesterday, how many shares you have, what they are worth and how much you are up or down; at the top, what your whole portfolio is worth and the dividends collected. It also has the latest news and How the stock market works.
    • The companies, their prices, how much they move, their dividends and their news are in the game data and can be changed with the mod editor.
  • Sound memory machine, at fairs and arcades: repeat with the arrow keys a sequence of notes that gets longer and longer.
  • Treasure hunting: diving at the wreck and the reef, or with a metal detector on some beaches. It is a game of hot or cold by ear: the beep speeds up and gets higher as you get closer.
  • Less honest: robbing shops, pedestrians and banks, or blowing open an armored van.

Bars and taverns also have four games: seven and a half, dominoes, the coin guessing game and the slot machine in the corner.

  • Dominoes: a hand against a regular, with all twenty-eight tiles, for twenty or a hundred euros. On your turn, the menu offers you the tiles you can play and next to which end. If you have none, you draw from the boneyard. Whoever runs out of tiles wins; if nobody can play, whoever has the fewest points in hand. The game says on each turn the ends on the table and how many tiles each player has left.
  • The coin guessing game: each player hides from zero to three coins in their fist and says how many they think there are between the two of you. Whoever guesses right takes the bet, of ten or fifty euros. If nobody guesses right, it is played again.
  • Slot machine: the same one as in the casino.

Seven and a half is played with a Spanish deck, against the bank. You bet ten, fifty or two hundred euros. You are dealt a card and you decide whether to ask for another or stand. Face cards are worth half a point and the rest, their number. If you go over seven and a half, you lose. When you stand, the bank draws cards until it ties you or goes bust; if it ties you, it wins. Winning pays double, and winning with exactly seven and a half, triple.

Collectibles also give a prize of a thousand euros and some street reputation for every five you find.

The job board: contracts of many kinds

The tavern board doesn't always offer the same thing. When you open it there are three jobs pinned up, and you choose one. They stay the same all day; when you complete one, or the next day, they change. Each one says what it is about, where it is and how much it pays. There are eight kinds:

  • Take out: the usual target, in one place, with their bodyguards.
  • Target with a motorized escort: the same, but part of the escort patrols the area in a vehicle and arrives when the trouble starts. You can hear them coming.
  • Target on the run: they have found out and are fleeing in a vehicle. You have to catch up with them in another one and wreck it, ramming or shooting from yours with Q and Control.
  • In their lair: the target doesn't leave a building. You go up to the door on foot, and when you go in they all come out after you.
  • Tailing the errand runner: nobody knows where they are hiding. You follow their errand runner on foot, by ear, without getting too close and without losing them, and they lead you to the hideout.
  • A warning: this one doesn't have to be killed. You teach them a lesson until they give up, and that's it. Their friends, as you see fit. If you kill them, the job fails; better with your fists or a melee weapon.
  • Recover the stolen goods: you take out whoever is guarding them, pick up what was taken and bring it to the tavern.
  • Protect a witness: you get there before the people coming for them, who arrive in a vehicle; you take them out and bring the witness, on foot or by car, to a safe place.

At first only the simplest ones come up; the others appear once you have completed a few contracts. Each completed contract makes the following ones harder and better paid, as always. They are all paid by going back to the tavern. The kinds of contract, what they pay and who defends them are in the game data, and with the mod editor they can be changed or added.

Robbing any business with a till

Robbery is no longer just for shops and banks. You can rob any business that has a till: garages, gyms, gun shops, pharmacies, bars, taverns, restaurants, clothing stores, pawn shops, cinemas, hotels, dealerships, casinos and the rest. The option is at the end of each business's menu, named Rob this business, and F1 says whether the till is guarded and what wanted level you will get. The garage, which until now only served you if you came with a vehicle, can also be entered on foot.

  • You need a firearm in your hand, and you can only do it once a day at each business.
  • If the till isn't guarded, as in a pharmacy or a shop, it's as always: it is emptied instantly, the alarm goes off and the police look for you.
  • If it is, as in a gym, a gun shop or a casino, you first have to take out the guards; then you pick up the till, which is left on the floor, and lose the police. The money is yours when they stop looking for you.
  • What is in the till changes every day and at each business. The more there is, the more guards and the higher the wanted level.
  • The business remembers you. For a few days, between two and four, they won't serve you: some shut the door on you; others let you in, recognize you and call the police, and from then on they shut it too. Shops do the same. The bank doesn't: it has its own robbery, as before.

On an online server it works the same as robbing shops. What is in each till and who guards it is in the game data, in the businesses' till; the days they remember you, in the robbery data.

If you have a gang, below the robbery option there is Plan the heist with your gang: you hand out roles among your people before starting. It is covered in the section Prepared heists: everyone with their role.

Working as a police officer, optional mode

This is a mode that comes turned off. It is turned on in Options, The street: people, police and gangs, Mode: be able to work as a police officer. With it off, none of this appears. It is only available when playing solo.

  • Joining: at any police station, Join the police. It costs a course of 400 euros and eight hours, and the neighbors need to think somewhat well of you and nobody can be looking for you. You start as a trainee officer.
  • The shift: at a police station, Start the shift. You are given a patrol car, which waits for you at the door with its locator sound. You can also get into any patrol car and press J. J in the car, or End the shift at a police station, ends it, and the borrowed car is returned.
  • The radio: on duty, every so often the radio sounds and dispatch passes you a call, with the place and the seconds you have to get there. The GPS takes you. The O key repeats the call. If you arrive late, another unit takes over and you don't get paid. And if you arrive, start and then go far away, the call is considered lost.
  • Robbery at a business: when you arrive one or two people come out with the till. Some give themselves up when they see you, others run off and others stand their ground.
  • Street fight: two people hitting each other. You can hear them. If you order them to stop, they usually break it up and leave; sometimes one turns on you.
  • Reckless driver: a vehicle being driven the wrong way. You stop it by getting behind it with the siren on, which is P, and holding on for a few seconds. It almost always stops; if not, it turns into a chase.
  • Chase: a vehicle that isn't going to stop. You have to ram it or shoot it until it can't go on. Then they either stay inside or run off.

How to stop someone fleeing in a car without the crime being yours: follow them with the GPS, which already has them marked, and ram them with the patrol car or shoot at the vehicle; with Q you lock onto it. While they are fleeing, and within three hundred meters of you, neither crashes nor shots count as a crime, even if they get ahead of you. When the vehicle is badly damaged, it stops or the driver runs off: then you order them to stop on foot. What does count is hitting people or other cars: each offense is a reprimand, and at the third they take your badge and the police come after you. On your first chase on duty, the game explains it to you.

How each thing is resolved:

  • To order someone to stop, on foot, press H near the suspect. Sometimes that is enough, more so if you have a firearm in your hand. Shouting over and over doesn't help: a few seconds have to pass between one order and the next.
  • Subduing: whoever resists is subdued. When they have less than half their health left, they surrender instead of going down. Fists and melee weapons are the safest.
  • Arresting: next to someone who has surrendered, Enter puts the handcuffs on them.
  • Fining: next to the vehicle that has stopped, Enter. You can give them the fine, of which you get a share; leave it at a warning, which the neighbors like; or arrest them, which is only appropriate if they fled.

What you earn and what you lose:

  • Each call resolved is paid, more if you arrive quickly and more the higher your rank. Every six calls you are promoted: officer, senior officer, deputy inspector, inspector and chief. You see it in the police station menu, and it counts on the phone, in Your career.
  • On duty, using force against the suspects of a call is not a crime, and the police don't come after you.
  • Killing someone you could have arrested pays half. And if they hadn't attacked you, or had already surrendered, it is excessive force: a reprimand.
  • Any other crime someone sees also counts. A small offense, like taking a car or shooting for no reason, is a reprimand, and they don't look for you. With three reprimands, or with a serious crime, they take your badge: the shift ends, the police come after you like anyone else and you can't join again for a week.
  • If you start a mission, leave the town or go out to sea, the shift simply ends, like any job: you are still an officer, and you can start another shift at the police station wherever you are.

Without a badge, the patrol car is still good for the usual vigilante job. The calls, what they pay, the ranks and everything else are in the game data.

Skills and achievements

  • Driving, aim, stamina and fishing go up by themselves with practice, up to level ten.
  • Maximum health goes up by training at a gym.
  • There are 40 achievements. You can check them on the phone.

13. Missions

How story missions and jobs work: the vehicle, the ones played by ear, what happens if you fail and the decisions that change the ending.

Missions and your vehicle

  • Before accepting a mission, its summary on the phone, in Available missions, says what happens with the vehicle: whether you are given one, whether you have to take a specific one, whether you can do it with yours, whether you have to go in one or whether there is a chase, for which it's best to bring a fast one. It is also said when you reach the contact's door, before accepting it.
  • Choosing a vehicle: in delivery missions where the vehicle only carries the load, you can choose between the one they give you and one of yours; the game tells you when it starts. They are Urgent Delivery, Urgent Harvest, Fallen from the Sky, The fish van and Crates for the Food Bank. If you want another of yours that you don't have at hand, ask for it on the phone, in My vehicles.
  • You also choose in other places: at the dealership, which model you buy and, if you take it without paying, which one you ask for the keys to, as the more expensive it is, the more the police look for you; and when asking for a driver, whether they come with one of your vehicles or with one of the gang's, and which of the gang's.
  • When the mission gives you a vehicle, yours is not lost. When that step starts, the game puts the vehicle you are driving, or the one you have just left nearby, in your name, and tells you it is parked and safe. You have it on the phone, in My vehicles, and from there you can send it to the garage without going back for it.
  • Some missions don't require a specific vehicle: what matters is carrying the load. In those, drive your vehicle to where the mission's vehicle is, stop next to it, and you do it with yours. When you deliver, you keep it and carry on in it. It has to be of the same class: for a helicopter mission you need something that flies, and a bicycle won't do for carrying a load.
  • Missions where you have to steal or deliver a specific vehicle still require that one.

Missions played by ear

Besides going to a place, getting a vehicle or facing someone, there are six types of job designed for the ear. In all of them, the O key tells you how you are doing.

  • Following someone. They are walking and you can hear their footsteps. There is no GPS: you have to follow them by sound. If you get closer than six meters, or three and a half while crouching, they notice. If you stay more than fifty-five meters away for a while, you lose them. The game warns you when you are too close or when they are getting away from you.
  • Escorting someone. You go to pick them up; from then on they follow you on foot and get in with you if you take a vehicle. You both have to reach the destination.
  • Getting in without making noise. There are guards walking around the place, and you can hear them walk. Standing, they hear you at six meters; running, at eleven; crouching with X, at less than three. If they hear you, it's over. The game warns you when one is near you.
  • Route against the clock. Several stops in a fixed order, with a time limit for all of them. The GPS takes you from one to the next.
  • Watching a place. You have to stay near the place, within about twenty-five meters, for a while, listening to what goes on there. Every so often someone comes out to walk around and you hear their footsteps getting closer: while they are near, stay still or crouch with X. While crouching you can move. If they see you moving while standing, or if you stray from your post for a few seconds, you fail. When the time is up, the game tells you what you have found out.
  • Race against rivals. You drive a vehicle to the start and stop. After a three-second countdown, you have to pass through the checkpoints of the course and reach the last one before the rivals. You can hear the rivals' engines from where they are, so you know whether they are ahead of or behind you, and the game says your position each time it changes. If a rival gets there first, you fail.

In the main story there are six jobs of this kind, in the Jobs by Ear chapter, which start at the El Faro Newsroom. In Bahía del Ocaso there are another six, which start at the newspaper El Faro del Ocaso once you have done the mission The meter.

If you fail a mission: checkpoints

Long missions save a checkpoint each time you finish a stage. If you fail after that, the game offers three things: retry from the last checkpoint, which takes you back to the start of the stage you were in with what you had then; retry from the beginning of the mission; or leave it for now. There is no checkpoint while you are driving a mission vehicle that still has to be delivered: there, you go back to the previous stage.

When you retry, you go back with the money, health and weapons you had then. Your vehicles don't go back in time, so none is lost: they are wherever you left them, on the street or in the garage. If during the mission you took one out of the garage, it stays out; if you sent one to the garage, it stays stored, even if you sent it after the mission had started. The same goes for a boat of yours: if you were in it, it goes back to a mooring.

The vehicle you were in, or the one you had just left next to you, comes with you: it is parked next to the place you go back to, and the game says so. It doesn't come if it is the mission's, a boat or something that flies. When you retry, the game also reminds you how many vehicles you have on the street and in garages. If during the mission you bought a house and put a vehicle in its garage, the house is no longer yours and the money comes back, but the vehicle is not lost: it goes to another of your garages or, if there is no room, it stays on the street in your name.

If you fail the same mission three times, the option to skip it also appears: it counts as done, with no reward.

If you are arrested or pass out when you have already done what the mission asked for and only its final scene is left, the mission is not failed: after the police station or the hospital, the scene continues and the mission is completed.

In chases, the fleeing vehicle goes slower on easy and normal and, if it gets ahead of you, it eases off. When it starts, the game explains what you need: a fast vehicle, because on foot or with a slow one you can't catch it, and that the GPS and the beacon follow it. Every so often, while it is far away, the game tells you how many meters away it is, in what direction, on what street and where it is heading, and warns you when it is within range. O says that same thing as soon as you press it. To destroy it you can use bullets, with Q to lock onto it and Control to shoot from the car, grenades and ramming: the more damaged it is, the slower it goes.

Chase assist: it is in Options, Combat and difficulty, and comes turned on. It is a gentle aid. With it, the game says where it is every seven seconds instead of every twelve, and from twenty-five meters instead of forty-five; it calls out every turn the fleeing driver makes, with the street; the fleeing driver starts to ease off at sixty meters instead of ninety, and eases off completely at a hundred and ten instead of a hundred and fifty; and, if there is a time limit, you get fifteen percent more. When following someone on foot, it warns you more often, says when they turn a corner, you lose them at sixty-five meters instead of fifty-five, and it takes five seconds longer to lose sight of you. Without the assist, everything works as before. It doesn't apply on an online server. The numbers can be changed with the mod editor.

If a chase is giving you trouble, it isn't the same the second time: whoever already got away from you is more nervous and drives slower, a little more each time you fail, up to three times, and if there is a time limit you get a bit more.

When following someone on foot, they don't start walking until you find them. It doesn't matter how far away you are when you get the job: the GPS takes you to where they are, the game warns you when you are about forty meters away and then, after a few seconds, they set off walking. If you have already lost them before, it takes longer for them to lose sight of you.

Jobs around the region

These are twelve standalone jobs, spread across the towns, that are not part of the main story and can be done in any order after the first missions. They use every type of step: following someone, escorting, getting in without noise, watching, routes against the clock, a race, a robbery job and a trip out to sea. They start at the pharmacy, the park kiosk, the library, the museum, the fire station and the scrapyard in Puerto Bravo; at the Miner's Curve and the offices in Monteferro; at the bakery in Villaseca; at the arcade in San Lucero; at the marine shop in Cala Brava and at the inn in Fuentefría. The phone, in Missions, says which ones you have available.

Decisions, and different endings

In some missions the story asks you for a decision. You choose in a menu you can't leave without choosing, and F1 explains what each option means. It can't be undone. What you decide is noted in the journal on the phone and changes what comes next: which missions appear, how much money and street reputation you have, and how you get on with the gangs.

There are decisions spread throughout the story: what you do with the money you are offered, with an enemy who surrenders, with a treasure or with a gang that wants to talk. There are eight in the main story and one in six of the mods that come with the game. Almost all of them change your money, your street reputation or your mood; some, how you get on with a gang.

Four of those decisions open branches: depending on what you choose, some missions or others appear, each with its own ending, and some leave consequences that last forever, like an income you collect every midnight.

  • The councilman's envelope, in Puerto Bravo: three paths and three endings.
  • The buyer, in Villaseca, once you have done the town's stories: defend the cooperative, and become a partner, or sell it and cash in.
  • The Empty Throne, in San Lucero, once you have done its stories: leave El Caimán's business to the market, keep it yourself or take the books to the police.
  • Around the Circuit, in Bahía del Ocaso: publish the name or get paid to keep quiet.

The journal on the phone notes each decision and what it gives you or costs you every midnight.

The branches cross

The three crossroads of the base game, the one in Puerto Bravo, the one in Villaseca and the one in San Lucero, don't each go their own way:

  • Each branch remembers the others. When you finish the mission of a branch, whoever gave it to you comments on what you did in the other two places, if you have already decided: the editor knows whether you sold the cooperative, the inspector knows who is paying you in Puerto Bravo, and the gangs of one city know how you get on with those of the other.
  • When you have closed all three branches, the mission Three Letters, from the Crossed Accounts chapter, appears at La Gaviota Bar. Aunt Encarna reads you a letter from each place, different depending on what you chose, and tells you what she sees when she puts them together.
  • After that there is one more ending, which depends on what your decisions add up to. If you chose the honest option in all three places, The Region Stands Tall. If in none, Everything has an owner, with one last decision: share it out or keep it all. And if you did some of each, A bit of good, a bit of bad.

With the eighteen possible combinations, there is always one of those three endings, and only one.

The Puerto Bravo one is at the end of the Jobs by Ear chapter and leads to three different endings depending on what you choose. If you want to see the others, start another game, or use New game keeping what you earned.

14. Houses, money, saving and options

In your houses you can save, rest, hide and use the garage. There are two ways to store a vehicle: drive it up to the door and press Enter without getting out, or leave it parked nearby and go in on foot, because the house menu offers to put the vehicle you have left into the garage. The vehicle you get out of waits for you where you left it, even if you go far away, until you get into another one, and it is saved with the game. The ones that are yours are never lost, whichever ones you take: they are on the phone, in My vehicles. In the house menu, Name a vehicle names the one you have brought or any of those in the garage. You can also save anywhere from the pause menu or with F5, except during a mission. The game saves by itself at the end of each mission. If you fail a mission, you can retry it straight away without repeating the journey.

Money: on you, at home and in the bank

  • What you buy is paid with what you are carrying. If you don't have enough, they won't sell it to you, and the game tells you whether you have enough in the bank to withdraw.
  • You withdraw at banks, which also let you deposit, in hundreds, five hundreds, thousands or everything; and at ATMs, which are at gas stations, stations, airports and hotels, where you can only withdraw. In the mod editor, any place can have an ATM.
  • Money in the bank is never lost. What you carry, is: if you are arrested, if you are knocked down, or if you are mugged. At night, on foot and with money on you, someone may step out in front of you: you give them what you have, stand up to them or run away. This happens with street events turned on.
  • You can also keep money in the wardrobe of your houses. You don't lose it if you are mugged or arrested, but a house can be broken into: they take part of the money, the product and some item. With a safe, the money is secure; with an alarm, they break in much less.

The backpack and the home wardrobe

You carry a backpack with limited space: twenty slots. On the phone, in Inventory, the Backpack option says how many slots you have filled, and with Enter you open it: each item says whether it is stolen or found and how many slots it takes up, and Enter on one drops it on the ground.

  • What goes in the backpack: valuables, like a phone, a watch, a gold chain, a tablet, a camera or a laptop, and the product, if you play with adult content, which takes one slot for every ten units. The limit of a hundred units of product stays the same.
  • Where they come from. When you mug a pedestrian, besides the wallet, you sometimes keep something else. When you break into a parked car, sometimes there is something in the glove box. Some of those who go down leave something of value on the ground, which is picked up by itself when you walk over it, like money. And when diving, what you find on the bottom goes into the backpack. What you get diving counts as found; the rest, as stolen.
  • If it doesn't fit, it stays where it was, and the game says so.
  • Selling it: at pawn shops, the pawnbroker buys what you have in your backpack, one by one or all together. For found items he pays quite well; for stolen ones, much less, because he knows where they come from. The money from stolen goods is money you can't account for.
  • A bigger backpack: clothing stores sell one with forty slots, for 120 euros. What you are carrying goes into the new one.
  • The wardrobe of your houses: in the menu of each of your houses there is Wardrobe. It stores items from the backpack, product, weapons and money, and you take them out from there. What is in the wardrobe isn't taken by the police and isn't lost if you are knocked down, although the house can be broken into, and if someone informs on you, the police can search it. If you make product in a house's lab and it doesn't all fit, the rest stays in its wardrobe.
  • If you are arrested, the police keep the stolen goods and the product you are carrying. It is covered in the section Fine or bribe.
  • A house that stops being yours takes with it whatever was in its wardrobe.
  • The numbers, the phrases and the valuables, with their name, slots, value and where they come from, are in the game data, and can be changed or extended with the mod editor.

Renting out your houses

A house of yours that you don't use can earn you money. In its menu, Rent out the house says how much the tenant would pay per day, which depends on what the house is worth. The garage needs to be empty, your partner can't be living there and you need another free house to live in yourself.

  • From then on someone lives there, different each time, and every midnight their rent reaches you, a little different each day. It counts for taxes like what your businesses bring in.
  • Sometimes something breaks, like a broken water heater or a leak, and you pay for the repair. Occasionally the tenant is a day late and pays the next day.
  • While it is rented out you can't sleep, save the game or use the garage there: when you reach the door, the menu says who you are renting it to and how much you have collected.
  • To get it back, in that same menu, Take the house back: the tenant leaves and you return their deposit, which is two days' rent.
  • On an online server houses can't be rented out, because the money is managed by the server.

New game keeping what you earned

In the main menu, New game keeping what you earned starts the story from the beginning with what you had in a saved game: money, weapons, skills, houses, vehicles in garages, boats and achievements. Missions, territories and decisions start from zero. On each playthrough, mission enemies take fifteen percent more damage. The saved game you start from is not touched.

Games saved with earlier versions of the game are converted by themselves when you load them. A game saved with a newer version than the one you have won't load: the game tells you to update.

Walking around your house

In a house of yours, the option Walk around inside the house takes you inside. You go from one room to another with the left and right arrow keys, and with Enter you use the room you are in: in the living room you rest for a while and your mood goes up, in the kitchen you eat something from what there is at home once a day and, if you have bought food, you put the shopping away, open the fridge and the pantry and cook, in the bathroom you take a shower, in the bedroom you sleep eight hours and in the garage you see which vehicles you have stored. Escape takes you back to the house menu.

In the house menu, Furnish the house lets you buy eight things, each for one room, which stay in that house:

  • Plants, pictures and a rug, 300 euros: when you sleep you wake up in a slightly better mood.
  • A big sofa and a new TV, 600 euros: watching TV raises your mood twice as much.
  • Fitted kitchen and a big fridge, 700 euros: what you make at home gives you twice the health, burns less often and the fridge holds more.
  • Renovated bathroom, with a bathtub, 500 euros: a long bath raises your mood more and heals some bruises.
  • A really good bed, 800 euros: sleeping here raises your mood quite a bit.
  • A workbench, 900 euros, only if the house has a garage: while you sleep, the vehicles stored in that garage are repaired.
  • A built-in safe, 1200 euros: the money you keep in that house's wardrobe is safe from burglars.
  • A connected alarm, 900 euros: that house is broken into much less often.

What you buy shows when you walk around the house: each room says how it is.

Walking around inside places

You can also walk around inside bars, taverns, restaurants, stores, pharmacies, gun shops, hospitals, police stations, repair shops, banks, casinos, gyms, cinemas, hotels and almost any other place. When you go in on foot, the place's menu has the option Walk around inside the place.

  • You move from one room to another with the Left and Right Arrows, and the game describes each one.
  • Enter uses the room you are in. At tables and in waiting rooms you can sit for a while or listen in on what people are saying. In the restrooms you freshen up.
  • The counter, the bar or the service window is where they serve you: when you press Enter the game says you walk up and you go back to the place's menu, which is where the options to buy or order are. It doesn't throw you out: it's how you go from walking around to buying.
  • Notice boards are read with Enter and tell you what's on that day. The one at the police station: whether you are wanted, at what level and what the fine would cost you, your arrests, the gangs wanted in the city, and the day's alerts and incidents; every time you read it, it says something different. The one at the lottery office: the numbers drawn in the latest draws and the jackpot. The one at the job center: some day jobs with their pay.
  • F1 explains where you are, what Enter does in that room and which room you have to go to in order to be served.
  • Escape goes back to the place's menu.

Walking around a place is optional: everything you can buy or do is still in its menu, as always. Outdoor spots, such as squares, piers or stops, have no inside.

Food stores, supermarkets, markets and gas station stores are walked through differently: aisle by aisle, with a basket, and you pay at the checkout. It's explained in the section Shopping by walking around the store.

15. Territories, your gang and the other gangs

The neighborhoods you conquer, the gang you build with them, the heists you plan and what the other gangs do.

Territories at a glance

On the phone, in Territories, the first option is How this works: it explains it with the numbers from your game. In short:

  • What they are: every neighborhood in the cities can be yours. Towns and islands have no neighborhoods to conquer. You don't need them to follow the story: it's another way to earn money and grow strong.
  • What they're for: each territory pays you tribute every midnight, without you doing anything; it lets you have a gang, with one more person for each neighborhood; and it raises your street reputation. In your neighborhoods, the gang members of the gang you drove out no longer walk around.
  • How to conquer them: on the phone, Territories, Conquer and the neighborhood's name, while you are in that city. Three waves of gang members come, each one tougher; if you have a gang, it comes to help you. If you fall or leave, you only lose the chance. With neighborhood war on the street mode, you conquer them by going to the neighborhood. And without firing a shot, in Negotiate with the gangs you can buy one.
  • How to defend them: from time to time a gang attacks one of your neighborhoods. An alert sounds and it appears at the very top of Territories: you defend it in person or send your gang, before midnight. If you do nothing, you lose it.
  • Reconquest: with many neighborhoods, the defeated gangs regroup and try to win them back, with a warning and a deadline. You prepare for it in Neighborhood defense, and it can be turned off in the options.
  • If you lose a neighborhood: it stops paying you and becomes the property of the gang that took it from you. It can be conquered again.
  • Who owns each place: the N key, the map, the list of places and the notice boards always say who runs each neighborhood right now. Places whose name or description mentioned the previous gang, such as a hideout, also change when the neighborhood changes hands.

Picking a fight with a gang, without conquering anything

If what you want is to fight, with no neighborhoods involved, on the phone, in Territories, there is Look for a fight with a gang. Only when playing offline.

  • You choose the gang, and whether you want a short fight, of a single round, or a long one, of two, with a few seconds to reload in between.
  • They come for you wherever you are, on foot. You have to stay in the neighborhood until it's over: if you leave, it counts as lost, with no other consequences. The O key tells you how it's going. If you have a gang, it comes to help you.
  • You don't win any neighborhood or money, except what they drop when they fall. Your street reputation goes up a little, once a day.
  • It has consequences: that gang dislikes you more, for challenging it and for each one you take down, and if there is shooting the police do the usual.
  • You can't do it during a mission or a job, with the police after you, at sea or inside a place. You have to wait a little between one fight and the next.
  • With a controller: it's a menu like the others, inside the phone.

Missions adjust to your escort

With your whole gang and two bodyguards, which is up to eight people by your side, missions played themselves. That's why, by default, when you arrive at a mission with people by your side, a few more enemies are waiting for you, and they take more damage and aim better. You change it in Options, Combat and difficulty: Missions adjust to your escort.

  • Moderate, which is the default: one more enemy for every two of yours, at most half again as many as the mission brings; six percent more health for each one, up to forty; and somewhat better aim, up to fifteen percent.
  • Strong: one more enemy for each of yours, at most double; ten percent more health for each one, up to eighty; and up to thirty percent better aim.
  • No: the same enemies always wait for you, whoever you bring.
  • The first of yours doesn't count: going with a single companion changes nothing. Your gang members and bodyguards who are nearby when the enemies appear count; the dog doesn't count.
  • When the opposition goes up because of your escort, the game says so, once per mission.
  • It doesn't count in the tutorials, with practice dummies, gym opponents or people you only have to scare, or on an online server or playing with friends. Neighborhood wars don't change: there your gang always comes.
  • All the numbers are in the game data and can be changed with the mod editor.

Your own gang

When you conquer your first neighborhood you can have a gang. On the phone, in Territories, go into Your gang:

  • Recruiting someone costs 400 euros. Each territory lets you support one more: with one neighborhood, two people; with five or more, six, which is the limit.
  • Call your gang makes them come to where you are. They follow you on foot, get into the vehicle with you and fight by your side.
  • Each member is paid 60 euros every midnight. If you don't have enough, one leaves. If you run out of territories, the gang breaks up.
  • You can give it any name you like.
  • Driver: one of yours comes to pick you up and drives. The next section explains it.
  • Your crew, one by one: each person in your gang has a name and four things that make them different, from 0 to 100: marksmanship, driving, nerve and loyalty. They are different in each game and are saved with it. When you call them, each one arrives with their name.
  • Prepared heists: before a robbery you can hand out roles among those by your side. It's explained in the section Prepared heists, further down, and in How heists work, in this same menu.

A driver from your gang

If you have someone recruited, you can ask them to come pick you up with a vehicle and drive. You ask on the phone, in Services, Your gang's driver, or in the Your gang menu. It costs 80 euros each time.

  • You choose what they come in: a gang vehicle, or one from your garages. You can also ask from My vehicles, on a vehicle stored in a garage.
  • It takes between half a minute and a bit more than a minute to arrive, different each time, and longer if it's raining. When you ask, it tells you roughly how long. When they set off, the game tells you which way they are coming; when they arrive, they honk the horn twice, say a line to you, and the vehicle gives off a tone so you can find it.
  • Get in with F: you ride as a passenger. Set a destination with L or from the phone, as always, and they take you: they speed up, brake, turn, wait at traffic lights and stop when they arrive. If there is no destination, they wait and ask you for one. It works even if you have free driving selected.
  • While they drive, the arrows, U, Y and J do nothing: the game reminds you that you are a passenger. Everything else stays the same: the radio, the radar, and shooting, with Q to lock on and Control to fire.
  • If you get out with F, they wait for you there, however far you go, with the tone sounding so you can find it when you come back.
  • You can dismiss them at any time, from the same menu. If the vehicle is yours, you keep it: if you were inside, you take over the driving; if not, it stays parked, in your name. If it's a gang vehicle, they take it away, and if you were inside they drop you off on the sidewalk.
  • They don't come if the police are after you at level 3 or higher, or in the middle of the sea. If you change town without them, they go back; if they came with one of your vehicles, they leave it parked where it was.
  • The gang vehicle can't be stored, sold or upgraded.

Prepared heists: everyone with their role

A regular robbery, of a bank, of any place with a till or of a supplier, can be planned beforehand if you have members of your gang by your side. It's not mandatory: the regular robbery is still in its option and works the same as before. When it's planned, each of yours does a job, the safe takes time to open, the heist has phases that the game announces, and your crew talks to you over the radio.

In the menu of the bank, the place or the supplier, below the robbery option, Plan the heist with your gang appears. It shows up when you have someone recruited. Inside:

  • How this works: the whole explanation, with the numbers from your game.
  • One line for each of yours who is nearby. With Enter you give them a role, and the menu tells you how good they are at that role. Each role is done by one person only. Anyone you don't give a role to comes with you and fights by your side, as always.
  • Call the missing members of your gang, if not all of them are there.
  • You, inside: you open the safe yourself, or you cover whoever opens it. To cover, someone has to have the safe role.
  • You, on the getaway: you drive, or you ride as a passenger. To ride as a passenger, there has to be a driver.
  • Getaway vehicle, if there is a driver: one of yours that is nearby, or a gang one.
  • The split: what percentage your crew takes and how much you would keep today.
  • Start the heist. You need the same as for the regular robbery, a firearm in your hand and nothing else going on, and at least one person with a role.

The roles:

  • Driver: waits outside, with the engine running, in the vehicle you choose. The vehicle gives off a tone so you can find it. When you come out you get in with F, as a passenger or at the wheel, and the getaway starts with a head start: if the police haven't arrived yet, they take longer to find you; if they are already after you, it can take one wanted level off you. Their performance depends on their driving.
  • Lookout: stays outside, on a corner. When the alarm goes off they hold the police back, about twenty seconds depending on how good they are, and tell you over the radio how long is left, again when only a few seconds remain, and when the police are already there. Without a lookout, the police arrive as soon as the alarm goes off. Their performance depends on their nerve.
  • Escort inside: goes in with you and keeps the people and the guards in check. With them it's much harder for someone to play the hero and attack you while you open the safe, or for someone to press the alarm too early. When they prevent either of these, they tell you. Their performance depends on their marksmanship.
  • The cash grabber: with you, the safe opens quite a bit sooner; and if you choose to cover them, they open it on their own while you stay close, less than twenty-five meters away, free to fight. Their performance depends on their nerve.
  • Shooter: rides in the getaway vehicle and fires through the window at the patrol cars and officers following you. Their performance depends on their marksmanship.

The five phases, which the game says out loud and which the O key reminds you of at any time, with who does what:

  1. Entry: everyone goes to their spot and says so over the radio.
  2. Control: if there is a guard, you have to take them out. If there isn't, the people get down on the floor.
  3. The safe: it's no longer taken instantly. Go up to it, press Enter and stay next to it: it takes between eight and sixteen seconds depending on the place, and twenty-six at a bank. You hear it progress, with a sound that gets higher and higher, and the game says when it's halfway. If you move away, it stops, and it continues when you come back.
  4. Exit: with the loot in hand, the alarm goes off.
  5. Getaway: the police arrive, with that place's wanted level, and you have to shake them off. The money is yours when they stop looking for you.

What can go wrong, different each time and depending on how good each one is:

  • Someone whose nerve fails: the driver arrives late or stalls the engine and there is no head start; the lookout doesn't see the police coming; the cash grabber takes longer than you would.
  • The escort can get wounded if there is a guard: they drop out of the heist and are out of action for a few days, between two and five. They still get paid.
  • The lookout can get arrested when the police arrive: they leave the gang and don't get paid. If their loyalty is low, they give your name: your wanted level goes up one and you get a criminal record entry, if criminal records are turned on. If they are loyal, they keep quiet.
  • If the heist fails, everyone who had a role loses trust in you. Anyone left with none leaves the gang.

The split: when it goes well, each one gets their share of the loot: the driver and the cash grabber, ten percent each; the lookout, the escort and the shooter, eight. Together they never take more than sixty. Also, with each heist that goes well they learn, and they get three points better at everything up to the fifth heist, and become more loyal to you.

On the phone, in Territories, Your gang, there are Your crew, one by one, with how good each person is, how many heists they have done with you and whether they are out of action, and How heists work.

Your crew: pay, loyalty, tasks and betrayals

  • Gang pay, in your gang's menu, is low, normal or high. With low pay you save money, but they cool toward you a little each day; with high pay, they become somewhat more loyal each day. If someone goes days without you talking to them, treating them or giving them something, they feel forgotten and their loyalty drops on its own.
  • If one day you can't afford to pay, one leaves: the least loyal one, not the last to join. The others stay, but angry, and lose loyalty.
  • Their profile says how they are doing: is with you to the death, is happy, is getting by, is unhappy or is badly burned out.
  • Someone badly burned out can betray you: steal what you keep in a house's closet and disappear, go off with another gang, or tip off the police, who search one of your houses, take the merchandise and sometimes shut down the lab.
  • Tasks: in each person's profile, the Task option. They can sell on the street the merchandise you keep in your houses' closets, or cook in one of your labs with the supplies you have, which are bought from a supplier. At the end of the day they bring you what they sold, in cash and minus their cut, and what they cooked stays in that house's closet. A dealer can get arrested: they lose what they were carrying and are out for a few days; and if they aren't loyal to you, they keep a bit more than they should and, if they get arrested, they reveal where you keep the rest. A fire can also break out in a lab. Tasks need adult content.

In Your crew, Enter on someone opens what you can do with that person, by name: talk for a while, and they tell you things about themselves; treat them to something, for thirty euros; give them a bonus, for one hundred fifty; or kick them out of the gang. Each one can be done once a day and makes them more loyal, the bonus most of all, and someone loyal doesn't give you away if they get arrested.

With a controller everything is done from the menus, with the usual buttons: there are no new keys.

On an online server and when playing with friends, heists aren't planned with the gang, and the game says so: there the option doesn't appear, and the regular robbery still works. A game saved with an earlier version, which only knew how many of you were in the gang, gives them names and skills the first time. The roles, their shares, the times and the radio lines are in the game data, and they can be changed with the mod editor.

Owning neighborhoods has consequences

  • Who owns each neighborhood: the N key, the map explorer, the phone's list of neighborhoods and the people you ask say who the neighborhood belongs to at that moment. A gang's, while it runs it; yours, when you conquer it; at war, while you are conquering it or while it's being attacked; and the gang that took it from you, if you don't defend it. On a server, the player gang that holds it.
  • Street reputation: taking over a neighborhood becomes known throughout the city. The first one gives a lot of street reputation; the following ones, less and less.
  • Attacks: from time to time, at midnight, another gang attacks one of your neighborhoods. If you have a gang, it may defend it on its own: the more people, the more likely. If not, you have until the following midnight to go: on the phone, Territories, Defend. There are two groups of gang members. If you don't go, you lose the neighborhood. When you hold a whole city or many neighborhoods, these one-off attacks give way to the reconquest, which is explained further down.
  • Pacts: with three neighborhoods or more, a gang may offer you a pact. If you accept, you get paid and for a week nobody attacks. If you turn it down, you gain some street reputation.
  • The police: an inspector may let you know that you are on their list. If you pay, they look the other way. If not, a raid costs you a day's tribute.
  • Everything is answered on the phone, in Territories, before the following midnight. If you turn off street events in the options, none of this happens.

How you get along with the gangs

You have a relationship with each gang, which you can see on the phone, in Territories. If a gang respects you, its members don't attack you on the street as long as you don't touch them, and they don't try to take neighborhoods from you. If it's out to get you, the opposite happens. The relationship changes with story decisions, with pacts and every time you kill one of its members.

Negotiating with the gangs

Besides the peace pact they sometimes offer you, now you make the proposals. On the phone, in Territories, there is Negotiate with the gangs, with three deals. What they answer depends on your strength on the street, which comes from your gang members, your neighborhoods and your street reputation, and on how you get along with each gang; and it isn't the same two days in a row.

  • Give a neighborhood back for money: you offer one of your neighborhoods to the gang that wants it. If it's interested, it names a figure. You accept it, ask for more or leave it. You lose the neighborhood and what it pays you every midnight, but you get paid in one go and your relationship with that gang improves a lot. If you are at each other's throats, it won't even listen to you.
  • Buy a neighborhood: you get it by paying, without firing a shot. They ask for more if you get along badly and less if they respect you or if you carry a lot of weight on the street. A gang that has it in for you, when you carry little weight, won't sell to you.
  • Collect tribute: you demand that a gang pay you every midnight for leaving it alone. You need to have some neighborhood. If they fear you, they pay for a few days, between five and ten, and while they pay they don't attack your neighborhoods. If not, they refuse, your relationship gets worse and, if you carry little weight, they may send someone after you. Now and then, one digs in its heels and stops paying.
  • Haggle: in deals with a figure you can push once. If it works, it improves by a quarter; if not, they walk away from the table.
  • Make a truce: with neighborhood reconquest under way, each gang also has this option. You pay them and for a few days they don't raid your neighborhoods. It's explained in the next section.
  • When a gang has answered you about something, it won't talk to you about it again until three days have passed.

With the police after you, or outside a city, there is nobody to sit down with. It doesn't exist on an online server: there the neighborhoods belong to the players' gangs. The numbers and the lines are in the game data.

Reconquest: the gangs try to win back your neighborhoods

Conquering isn't the end. When playing offline, the gangs you have defeated regroup over the days and come back for what was theirs: whoever runs a city has to keep watch so as not to lose it. It's the option Gangs try to reconquer your neighborhoods, in Options, The street, with four values: off, calm, normal, which is the default, and hard. Everything is handled with menus, on the phone, in Territories, Neighborhood defense; with a controller, just like any other menu.

When it starts

  • When you conquer the last neighborhood of a city. Before that there are days of calm, between three and five, and the game warns you only once, with its own sound, that this exists and how to defend yourself.
  • With four neighborhoods or more, even if you don't hold any whole city, a mild version starts: the gangs regroup at half the pace.
  • With all the cities conquered, they regroup a little faster.
  • In a game saved with an earlier version that already had the neighborhoods: when you load it there are three days of calm and the same warning, only once.
  • With fewer neighborhoods none of this happens, and the usual one-off attacks continue.

The gangs regroup

  • Each gang has a strength, from 0 to 100, which grows every midnight, sometimes more and sometimes less. On calm it grows more slowly and on hard more quickly.
  • When a gang feels strong enough, it plans an assault, which takes two or three days to arrive. It chooses the neighborhood by its own rules: the one that used to be theirs, the worst defended, the one you have neglected most or the one that brings you the most money.
  • Sometimes two gangs team up for a big strike: they arrive with more strength, and it's announced.
  • While a peace pact is in force, one of those they sometimes offer you, nobody plans a new assault.
  • A gang that pays you tribute doesn't regroup or attack while it pays. One that respects you, or one that has a truce with you, regroups at half the pace.
  • There are never two assaults at once, and after each one there are days of rest: at least seven on calm, four on normal and two on hard. With reconquest under way, the usual one-off attacks don't happen.

How you find out

  • You can always check the threat level of each neighborhood, in Territories and in Neighborhood defense: low, medium or high. If something is being planned against a neighborhood, it says high at least, even if you don't know more.
  • The rest depends on your informants, who get paid every midnight, 45 euros per level. With level 1 you hear the rumor one day before, with the neighborhood. With level 2, a tip-off two days before, with the gang and the neighborhood, and you see the exact strength of each gang. With level 3, a message to your phone as soon as they decide, with the gang, the neighborhood, the day, the time, the strength they come with and why they chose that neighborhood.
  • A neighborhood that is loyal to you warns you itself, without paying anyone: with 50 loyalty, like level 1; with 75, like level 2.
  • With no informants and a neglected neighborhood, the warning arrives late or not at all: at most, a vague rumor on the same day.
  • When the assault starts you always find out: the threat alert sounds and the game says who, where and how many hours you have. It warns you again when two hours are left and when half an hour is left. While it lasts, the O key, the objective key, says who is attacking which neighborhood and how much time you have left.
  • In Neighborhood defense, Latest reports repeats the last things that happened, in case you missed them.

The assault

From the moment it starts you have a deadline: 18 game hours on calm, 12 on normal and 8 on hard. You can do three things.

  • Defend in person: go to the neighborhood. In Neighborhood defense, the option to go in person marks it on your GPS. The fight starts on its own when you enter it, even if you don't have neighborhood war on the street mode turned on: they come in waves, each with one more, and your gang comes to your side. There are two waves; three if they arrive with a lot of strength, and one more if it's an alliance. With good defense in the neighborhood, fewer come in each wave. While you fight, the deadline doesn't run. If you leave the neighborhood or fall, the fight is lost but the assault goes on: you can come back or send your crew. If you win, you take what they leave behind as they flee.
  • Send your crew: in Neighborhood defense, and you need to have someone in the gang. Before you send them, the option tells you the chances, out of ten. The result is calculated on the spot and told with its numbers: their strength against the neighborhood's defense plus what each of yours adds, and each side's luck. One of yours may fall.
  • Do nothing: when the deadline runs out, if the neighborhood has a permanent guard, it defends itself, with somewhat less strength. If it doesn't, the neighborhood is lost.

Rules to keep it fair: in the middle of a mission, no assault starts and the deadline doesn't run. If you sleep, or end up in the hospital or in a cell, the hours go by, but your crew holds out: the first time, when you come back you always have four hours left, and the game tells you so. The same if the assault started while you were away. Short stretches, like a bus ride or a meal, just go by. Nobody loses a neighborhood for sleeping one night; for falling asleep twice in a row with the neighborhood under attack, yes.

Getting ready beforehand

  • Permanent guard: up to three guards per neighborhood, at 60 euros each every midnight; the first day is paid when you hire them. They raise the defense, make loyalty drop at half the speed and defend on their own if you don't arrive. If one midnight you can't pay them, they leave.
  • Reinforce the neighborhood: three levels paid only once, 1500, 3000 and 4500 euros. Each level raises the defense for good; if you lose the neighborhood, it drops one level.
  • Informants: the ones from the previous section.
  • Truce: with each gang, in Neighborhood defense or in Negotiate with the gangs. You pay them and for a few days, between six and ten, they don't attack you; if they were planning an assault, they drop it. They ask for more the stronger they are and the worse you get along, and you can ask for a discount, once. It can be broken: by them, some day, and then you are warned and they can't strike the next day; or by you, if you hit their hideout.
  • Hit their hideout: a short mission, different every time, in the city you are in. If you complete it, the gang loses more than half its strength, it counts as a defeat and, if it was the one planning an assault, the assault is called off. The gang has to be strong enough to have a hideout, and you can't repeat it against the same gang until four days have passed.
  • Your gang members count in the defense of all your neighborhoods, whether you send them or not.

Each neighborhood's loyalty

  • It goes from 0 to 100 and starts at 70. A loyal neighborhood defends itself better, tempts the gangs less and warns you of what is being planned.
  • It drops if more than three days go by without you setting foot in the neighborhood, three points each day, and also if one midnight you can't pay the guards or your gang.
  • It goes up the first time you enter it each day, when you defend it, which is what bonds it to you the most, and when you invest in the neighborhood: 500 euros, once a day per neighborhood.
  • Before it drops too far, the game warns you: once when it falls below 45 and again, more seriously, when it falls below 25. On its own, loyalty never takes a neighborhood from you: it only makes it easier for it to fall.

What you win and what you lose

  • If you lose a neighborhood, you lose what it paid you every midnight and some street reputation, and the neighborhood lost sound plays. It becomes the property of the gang that took it from you, and you win it back the usual way, in Territories.
  • The gang that regains ground gains morale: it regroups faster and comes back sooner.
  • If you fight it off, the neighborhood defended sound plays, the gang loses almost all its strength and a defeat is recorded against it. On the third one, counting the hits on its hideout, it is dismantled for between twenty and thirty days.
  • So that the game doesn't simply end, someone always shows up eventually: when few gangs are left standing, or after a month, an outside gang can settle in the area, with its own name. It's treated like the others; defeated three times, it leaves for good, and in time another will come.

All of this is saved with the game. It doesn't happen on an online server, because there the neighborhoods belong to the players' gangs; nor while you are playing with friends: the reconquest stays paused, with the deadline stopped, and it continues when you go back to playing alone. All the numbers are in the game data and can be changed with the mod editor.

Neighborhood war on the street, optional mode

By default, neighborhoods are conquered and defended from the phone, in Territories, as always. Anyone who prefers to fight for them on the street can turn on the Neighborhood war on the street mode, in Options, The street. With the mode turned on, this changes:

  • Every neighborhood in the cities that isn't yours belongs to a gang, always the same one in your game. The N key, the map and the phone say which one.
  • Its members walk the streets of each neighborhood: of every three people you pass, roughly one is from the gang. They attack you on sight, unless that gang respects you.
  • You conquer a neighborhood by going there. The war starts when you take down two of its members in their neighborhood, and the game warns you the first time; or from the phone, in Territories, once you are already inside the neighborhood, with Stand up to them. From outside, the phone only marks the way on your GPS.
  • The gang comes for you in three waves, each with one more and tougher. The game says how many there are and which way they are coming; in the last one, some arrive in a vehicle. Between waves there are a few seconds to reload. The O key tells you how it's going.
  • If you have recruited people, they come to fight by your side as soon as it starts.
  • Don't leave the neighborhood: the game warns you when you leave, and after twenty-five seconds outside the fight counts as lost. It's also lost if you fall, if you get arrested, if you change town or if you start a mission. Losing it costs nothing else: the neighborhood is still theirs and you can try again.
  • If you win, the neighborhood is yours, with its tribute every midnight, and you take what the gang leaves behind as it flees.
  • The gangs counterattack as always, at midnight. But the neighborhood is defended in person: go there, and when you enter it two waves start. The phone, in Territories, Defend, marks the way for you. If you haven't gone before the following midnight, you lose it.

A war in progress isn't saved: if you save and load in the middle of one, you have to start it again. On an online server the mode doesn't count, because there the neighborhoods belong to the players' gangs. How many gang members walk the street, how many waves there are and what you win are in the game data.

16. The radio and your music

When you get into a vehicle the radio plays. With R you change station. To hear your music, copy your songs into the my music folder and choose the My music station; inside it, R skips to the next song. So you don't have to look for that folder, go into Options, Sound and music, and choose Open my music folder: it opens in Windows File Explorer, ready to copy into, and the option itself says how many songs it has. mp3, ogg, flac and wav files work. You don't need to close the game: when you tune back in to My music the new songs are already there. The folder is data\radio\mi_musica, inside the game's folder; if the station is empty, it explains how to fill it. Any audio file you put directly in data\radio is a new station.

Adding your own music, step by step

You don't need to look for any folder: the game opens it for you. In the game, press Escape and choose My music: put your songs on the radio. These same steps are there, along with the option that opens the folder. It's also in Options, Sound and music.

  1. Choose Open my music folder. A Windows window opens with a folder: it's the one for your songs. The game stays open behind it.
  2. Copy your songs there, as in any folder: find them on your computer, copy them with Control and C, and paste them into that window with Control and V. mp3, ogg, flac and wav files work, and so do whole folders.
  3. Go back to the game with Alt and Tab. You don't need to close or restart it.
  4. Get into any vehicle and change station with R until you hear My music. Inside My music, R skips to the next song.

The option to open the folder says how many songs there are. Your songs don't leave your computer, and updates don't touch them. With a controller, the pause menu and its submenus are handled with the usual buttons.

Songs can be as long as you like: a whole album, a radio show or a recording of several hours in a single file. Those longer than five minutes are read and played in one-minute chunks, without gaps, so they don't take up your computer's memory.

The radio pauses when you get out

When you get out of the vehicle, the song that was playing stops, and when you get into any other one it continues right where it left off. This works for all stations and for My music. Each station remembers its own: if you change station and then come back, it continues with the song it had. That way you can put on a show several hours long and hear the whole thing in pieces, without missing anything.

If you'd rather it behave like a real radio, which keeps going even when you aren't listening, change it in Options, Sound: Radio when you get out of the vehicle. Then, when you come back, you find it further along. Songs shorter than a minute and a half always start from the beginning.

The radio on foot: the music player with headphones

Stores sell a music player with headphones. With it you hear the same stations, and your music, walking down the street.

  • On foot, Shift R turns it on. Each press goes to the next station; one of the stations is off, and when you reach it you take off the headphones.
  • It plays a bit quieter than in the car, so you can still hear the street.
  • If you get into a vehicle with the headphones on, the song keeps playing without a break on the vehicle's radio, and when you get out it continues in the headphones.
  • With the player off, everything is as usual: the radio pauses when you get out.
  • If you save the game with the headphones on, they are still on when you load it.

Music in places

In stores, bars, restaurants, taverns, gyms, repair shops and other places, one of the game's stations plays softly in the background. Each place has its own, always the same. If you have your headphones on, you keep hearing your own.

If the sound clicks or cuts out

On some computers, short on power or with delicate sound cards, the audio can click or stutter. There are two remedies, both in Options, Sound. The first, Relaxed sound: the game prepares the sound in bigger chunks; sounds arrive a few hundredths of a second later and in exchange they stop cutting out. The second, the surround sound engine, which mixes outside the program. Both take effect when you close and reopen the game.

Background music

Outside vehicles, or with the radio off, soft background music plays that changes with the situation: the main menu, exploring by day, the night, the sea and moments of tension, with the police after you or enemies nearby. Each situation has several pieces: it starts with a random one and, when it ends, a different one plays. When you turn on a vehicle's radio the background music goes quiet, and it comes back when you turn off the radio with Shift R or get out. You turn it off and adjust it in Options, Sound and music. You can add your own music to each situation by copying files into data\musica, in that situation's folder: all the files there are used, at random, however many there are and whatever they are called. All the music, both the background music and the radio, is leveled automatically when it loads, so no track sounds louder than another.

Sound engine: classic or surround

In Options, Sound and music, the Sound engine option chooses how each sound is placed around you. You change it with the Left and Right Arrows and it takes effect when you close and reopen the game.

  • Classic: the usual one and the default. It spreads each sound between the left and right ear and muffles a bit what is behind you.
  • Surround: it uses binaural sound, a technique that imitates how sound reaches each ear. With headphones you can tell whether a car is coming from in front or from behind, and not just from which side. It also copes better when many things are playing at once. It needs headphones: with speakers it makes no sense.

With the surround engine, each place also has its own echo: almost none in the countryside, on the beach and at sea; some on the street, between building fronts; more between warehouses and at the harbor; long in the mountains; and short and close inside a house or a place. What flies sounds above you, and if you are flying in a helicopter, the city sounds below you. The echo can be turned off in Options, Sound and music, Echo depending on the place.

If the surround engine can't start, the game uses the classic one without saying anything, so you are never left without sound. On computers with an old processor and with the mono sound option, the classic one is always used. The session log notes which engine was used.

The surround engine uses the OpenAL Soft library, which is free software. Its license comes in the game's folder.

Sound packs

In Options, Sound and music, the Sound pack option switches with the Left and Right Arrows between the default sounds and the packs. They are all in data\sonidos, each in its own subfolder: por_defecto holds the standard ones, which the game recreates if any is missing, and each of the other subfolders is a pack. You hear the change right away. Three complete packs come installed, with all the game's sounds. Realista and Alternativo use real public domain recordings for footsteps, doors, weapons, voices, vehicles, sirens, ambiences, water, wind, alerts and menus; Retro sounds like an old console. All three replace everything that isn't orientation and keep the original sound for beacons, tones, traffic lights, radar, locators and the sounds of places. A pack of your own can bring just some sounds: the rest keep the original. You can add your own by copying their folder into data\sonidos.

The four packs that come with the game bring exactly the same sounds as the standard ones, not one more and not one less. Since version 1.7.1, in all four, these are also real recordings: the police helicopter's loudspeaker, the officer's radio, the house doorbell, the shopping basket, the fridge, the knife on the cutting board, the frying pan, the pot, the plate on the table and the three neighborhood alerts: a kettledrum for the threat, a bell for the neighborhood lost and a drum roll for the neighborhood defended. The tones of the three roleplay channels and the one for waiting for the server are the same in all packs, on purpose: they are a code, and they should always sound the same. Where each recording comes from is written in the CREDITOS.txt file in each pack's folder.

To compare how a sound plays in each pack, choose the pack in Options and go into Learn the sounds, or into Options, Sound and music, Adjust the volume of each sound: both places have all of them, in the same groups and with the same names, and both have a search.

The Envolvente pack is the ambience pack: it carries the sounds of the realistic pack and eleven neighborhood ambiences really recorded in stereo. Its orientation sounds are the standard ones.

Since each recording comes at a different volume, the game matches the volume of each changed sound to that of the original. Also, in Options, Sound and music, Adjust the volume of each sound has all the sounds, in the same groups and with the same names as in Learn the sounds, and in alphabetical order within each group: Enter plays it and the Left and Right Arrows raise or lower that particular sound, from 30 to 200 percent. When you adjust it, it plays, so you hear how it turns out.

  • How it combines with the other volumes: each sound's setting comes first and the general volumes above it, which are the master, effects, ambience, alerts and help cues volumes, are applied on top. If you lower the effects to half, a sound you have at 150 percent still plays louder than its neighbors, but all of them at half.
  • Search for a sound: the first option. Type a word from its name or from what it means and you get the list of those that match, each with its group and its volume, ready to adjust right there. It works the same as in Learn the sounds, also with the controller's letter wheel.
  • What you have changed: a sound that isn't at 100 percent says "changed" after its volume; each group says how many it has changed, for example "changed: 3", in the list of groups and when you enter it; and the Changed sounds option says how many there are in total.
  • Returning a sound to its default volume: with the keyboard, Delete on that sound. With keyboard or controller, go into Changed sounds, which is the list of all the ones you have touched, from any group, and press Enter, or A or Cross, on the one you want: it goes back to 100 percent and plays. You can also bring it to 100 yourself with the arrows.
  • Returning a whole group: inside the group, at the end, Reset this group. The other groups aren't touched.
  • Returning all of them: Reset all, at the end of the list of groups.
  • The settings are saved with your options, by each sound's name: the ones you already had from earlier versions are still there, each in its current group.

Changing the sounds and the music

All the sound effects and all the music are ordinary files, in data\sonidos\por_defecto, data\radio and data\musica. You can replace any of them with a recording of your own. The guide data\docs\cambiar_sonidos_en.html explains how and describes each file.

What format each thing is in, and why

  • The sound effects, in all packs, are in FLAC. FLAC compresses without loss: when it's read you get exactly the same samples as from a WAV, but it takes up less than half the space. Up to version 1.6.1 they were WAV and took up 141 megabytes; now they take up 55, and they sound identical.
  • The background music and the radio are in MP3, and the voices in the scenes and the long ambiences of the Envolvente pack, in OGG. These are formats that already drop some quality to take up little space, and there is no better original: converting them to another format would mean compressing what is already compressed, and they would sound worse, or making them several times bigger for no gain. That's why they stay as they are.
  • When updating from an earlier version, the game deletes only the old WAV files that are the same sound as the new FLAC files, so they don't take up space without being used. If you had replaced a sound with a recording of your own, that one isn't touched and is the one that plays.
  • Your recordings can still be WAV, OGG, FLAC or MP3. If you put one with the same name as a game sound, yours plays; to go back to the original, delete it.

17. Expanding the game: mods

A mod is an expansion: a file that adds places, missions, vehicles, weapons or whole towns to the game, without touching anything original. Mods are in the data\mods folder and load on their own at startup. If a mod has an error, the game says so at startup and keeps working.

Mods that come with the game

The game includes eight complete mods, already turned on. Together they add a city, three towns, an island and many hours of play:

  • Bahía del Ocaso: a large city on the west coast, reached by the west exit of Puerto Bravo that bears its name, by bus, by sea or by helicopter. It hasn't rained there in three years and water is the contraband. It has six neighborhoods, more than fifty places and three gangs: Los Aljibes, the Copper Club and Los Vigías. Its story is twenty-one missions in four chapters, and it starts at Barral Cranes and Tankers, in the El Cauce Seco neighborhood. Also: four honest jobs, a tanker truck, a lowered coupe, a station wagon, a dirt bike, a barge, three weapons, three races, houses, two businesses, two characters to choose from and, with adult content, a club, two people and a mission.
  • Costa Dorada: a fishing town west of Puerto Bravo, reached by the west exit, by bus or by sea. It has its own story in five missions, an honest job, almost every type of place, three weapons, a buggy, a delivery van, a sailboat and an autogyro, a race, meals, fish and two characters to choose from. With adult content, also a club, a supplier, a person, a gift, a date plan and a substance.
  • Valdeolmos: a wine-growing town to the north, reached by the northeast exit of Puerto Bravo or by bus. Three missions about a wine fraud, an honest job, a tractor race, river fishing, a hunting shotgun, a sickle, a tractor with a trailer and a character.
  • Peñas Altas: a mining town high in the mountains, reached by the north exit of Puerto Bravo that bears its name, or by bus. Four missions about a closed mine that someone is working at night, an honest job, a mountain carbine, a pickaxe and sticks of dynamite, a quad bike, a trial motorcycle and a dump truck to haul loads with, a hill climb, fishing in the Black Lake, treasure hunting in the slag heap and two characters. With adult content, a cocktail bar, a supplier, a person and a story.
  • Isla de los Vientos: an island out at sea, to the east, which can only be reached by boat or by helicopter. Three missions against some wreckers who raid the boats, an honest job, a house, salt flats that bring in money, fishing in the surf, treasures in the dunes, a silent speargun, a gaff hook, a catamaran, a jet ski and a seafaring character.
  • Neon Nights: Puerto Bravo's nightlife, in a neighborhood full of venues to the northeast. A concert hall, an all-night churro shop, a hotel, a penthouse, a night taxi cooperative with its taxi to work in, a limousine, a scooter, a night race, three chauffeur missions and a character. With adult content, a party hall, two people to meet, date plans, gifts, a cocktail and two stories.
  • Trades and Errands: more honest life in Puerto Bravo, with a sport shooting club, a food bank, a courier company, an apartment, a delivery motorcycle, a character and two jobs.
  • Engine and Asphalt: for those who just want to drive. Three new cars and a new motorcycle, the Viento Sur Racing Team with its workshop and its driving school, three races around Puerto Bravo, a driver to choose and two missions behind the wheel.

To remove one, turn it off in the editor with Control M or delete its file from data\mods. To add a mod someone has given you, copy its file, with the .pbm extension, into that same folder.

Translating the game into another language

Each language is a folder inside data\idiomas, with the language's two-letter code: en is English, it would be Italian, fr French. Inside is the textos file, which says how each of the game's texts, always written in Spanish, is said in that language. To translate it into a new language:

  1. Copy the data\idiomas\en folder and give it the code of the new language, for example data\idiomas\it.
  2. In its textos file, which opens with Notepad, change the text on the right of each line to the one in your language. The one on the left, the Spanish, is not touched. Whatever is between curly braces, like {dinero}, is left as is: that's where the game puts the data. A text you leave untranslated appears in Spanish.
  3. Open the game: it already recognizes it by the folder's code and it appears in Options, Language, with its name in its own language.
  4. If you also translate the guides, save them in data\docs with the code at the end: manual_it.html, teclas_it.html. The voices of the scenes go in data\voces, in one folder per story with the code at the end, like historia_principal_it, and with the same file names as the Spanish ones.

If you send us your translation, we publish it as a component, and it reaches everyone from Options, Updates, Components, without waiting for a new version of the game.

A mod's texts appear exactly as its author wrote them. Those of the mods that come with the game are translated, like the rest of the game.

The mod editor

To create your own mods there is the EditorMods.exe program, next to the game. It's an editor accessible with a screen reader that lets you create everything a mod supports by filling in forms: places, missions with all their steps, weapons, vehicles, races, food, items, fish, characters, whole maps, the people on the street, the gangs, the enemy types, the police at each wanted level, the repair shop upgrades, the businesses' cash and, for adult mode, people, date plans, gifts and substances. It checks the mod before saving it and puts it in data\mods, ready to play. With it you can also open the mods that come with the game to see how they are made. Its manual is editor_mods_en.html, and it opens from the editor itself with F1.

With the editor you can create your own vehicles, without knowing how to program: cars, motorcycles, trucks, boats and aircraft. In the Vehicles section, press Add and fill in the name, the top speed, the acceleration, the braking, the toughness, the engine sound and its pitch, the horn, the siren if it has one, whether it's a boat or whether it flies, the tank and the price. Then tell the game where it appears: in the neighborhoods where it drives and parks, at a car dealership or a boat dealer, which sell it if you have given it a price, or as the vehicle for a job or a mission. The quickest way is to bring one in from the game with the Copy from the original game button and change whatever you like.

18. Your character: who you are and how you dress

When you start a new game you choose a character. The story is the same for everyone, and the others call you by the name of the one you choose; what changes are the advantages you start with.

  • Dani Roca: a bit of everything. Starts with a first aid kit and no weak points.
  • Álex Montes: was a rally driver. Starts with driving at level five and three lockpick sets.
  • Sam Ferrer: eight years in the army. Starts with marksmanship at level four, more health, a switchblade and two first aid kits.
  • Cris Navarro: used to sell insurance. Starts with eight hundred euros, good mood, some street reputation and the neighbors' goodwill.

As male or as female

After choosing one of these four, the game asks how you want to be addressed: as male or as female. It changes how others treat you and how the game itself talks to you: Encarna's nephew or niece, son or daughter. The story, the missions and the advantages are exactly the same. It's saved with the game and can't be changed later.

The characters that some mods add are already he or she, and with them you aren't asked.

Creating your own character

The last option on the list is Create your own character. It's a menu with these details; you go through it with Up Arrow and Down Arrow:

  • First and last name: Enter opens a box to type it. Letters, spaces and hyphens are allowed, up to twenty. The last name can be left empty. In the story they will call you that.
  • They address you: as male or as female. You change it with Left Arrow, Right Arrow or Enter.
  • Your voice in the scenes: Dani's, Álex's, Sam's or Cris's. It's the voice you speak with in the story; each one has a male and a female version, and the one that matches how you are addressed plays. You change it with the Left and Right Arrows.
  • How your name is pronounced: by default, the same as it's written. If it doesn't sound right when you listen to it, Enter opens a box to type it the way it's said, for example Yónatan instead of Jonathan. On screen and in braille it still appears as you wrote it.
  • Listen to how you will be called: generates a line with your name, spoken by a character from the story. It takes a moment.
  • Listen to your voice: generates a line spoken by you, with the chosen voice.
  • Driving, marksmanship, endurance and fishing: Right Arrow raises a level and Left Arrow lowers it. You have eight levels to share out, with five at most in each skill. Each option says how many you have left. Afterwards you keep raising them with practice and at the academies, up to ten.
  • Starting advantage, choose one with the Left and Right Arrows: none; savings, eight hundred euros; toughness, more health and two first aid kits; quick hands, three lockpick sets; a switchblade and two first aid kits; or people skills, with good mood, some street reputation and the neighbors' goodwill.
  • Start the game. You need to have entered the name. If you have levels left unassigned, the game warns you once and with a second Enter it starts anyway.

F1 explains each detail. The name you type isn't written to the session log. The last three voice options, the pronunciation one and the two listening ones, only appear if your computer can handle the voice generator.

When you start, the game prepares the voices that say your name: it's explained further on, in the section Your own character's voices.

Ready-made characters can be added with the mod editor.

Clothes, accessories and protection

  • In clothing stores, clothes are sorted by style: casual, sporty, elegant, work, party, beach, cold-weather and tactical. There are more than thirty outfits, from jeans and a T-shirt to a tuxedo or an evening gown, and F1 describes each one.
  • Footwear: street, designer or running sneakers, hiking, rain, work or military boots, dress shoes, loafers, high heels, ankle boots, sandals and flip-flops, each with its style. If the footwear is the same style as the clothes, your outfit matches.
  • Accessories: glasses, chains, rings, watches, caps, earrings, bags, belts, bracelets, scarves, ties, perfumes and piercings, cheap and designer. They are worn in addition to the clothes, one of each kind. Wearing a new one for the first time lifts your mood, more the more expensive it is.
  • What you buy is yours forever. In the store, what's yours says "It's already yours: you put it on for free"; and in your houses' menu, Get dressed gathers all your clothes, footwear and accessories so you can put them on or take them off without paying.
  • Style matters. Each person in your social life likes a style: if you go dressed that way on a date, it shows and brings you a bit closer, and even more if your outfit matches. After a couple of dates, their profile tells you which style they like. Dressing elegantly helps sell cars at the car dealership.
  • Changing clothes, in a store or at home, throws off the police if they are after you at one or two levels and there are no officers nearby.
  • The store, the Get dressed menu and the status key say what you are wearing, including the footwear. Clothes, footwear and accessories are in the game data: with the mod editor you change them and add more, each with its style and description.
  • Two garments have reinforcements: the tactical clothing and the camouflage uniform. When you put them on, your armor goes up to 40 or 30 if you had less.
  • In gun shops, besides the vest, which sets armor to 100, there are the tactical chest rig, which sets it to 150, and the full tactical gear, at 200.

19. The honest life

You can finish the game rich without committing a single crime. Everything honest raises your standing among the neighbors; crimes lower it. With good standing you earn more at jobs and the police overlook small offenses. And you can be both: a good person by day and a criminal by night, with everything that brings. It's explained in the section Life, reputation and consequences, a little further down.

  • Job center, one in each city: it gathers all the jobs. Day jobs are jobs without a vehicle: eight hours go by and you get paid; only one a day. In trades with a vehicle, Enter marks on the GPS where one is parked; get in with F and press J.
  • Day jobs: waiter, shelf stocker, loading dock laborer, carpenter, welder, mechanic's assistant and lifeguard. The hardest ones pay more and tire you out.
  • Car salesperson: at any car dealership you can work a day. You get a fixed salary and a commission of two percent of each car you sell; you sell more with a good reputation among the neighbors, with experience and when you are well dressed. In the same place is the temptation: ask for the keys for a test drive and not come back. It's a robbery in plain sight and the police come after you in force.
  • Promotions: every eight services in a trade you go up a rank and earn twelve percent more. A short fanfare plays.
  • Academies and driving schools: a course costs 250 euros and four hours, and raises a skill much faster than practice. One a day.
  • Soup kitchens and food banks: you can donate money or do four hours of volunteering, which isn't paid but raises your mood and your standing a lot.
  • Street events: helping counts too.
  • Your career: on the phone, it sums up your honest life and your criminal life, with your ranks in each trade.
  • Permanent contracts, studies with qualifications, growing businesses, loans and mortgages, and a story of your own without crimes: all of that is further down, in Life, reputation and consequences.

Life, reputation and consequences

In Puerto Bravo you can be a good person, a criminal or both at once. Everyone chooses, and each path has its responsibilities and its consequences. The game keeps two separate scores, which don't depend on each other:

  • Your standing among the neighbors, from 0 to 100: how the city sees you. It rises with honest work, studies, donations, volunteering and good deeds. It drops with the crimes people see you commit, with arrests and with scandals. It has four steps: the neighbors avoid you, below 10; you're just another person; the neighbors like you, from 40; and you're a model citizen, from 75.
  • Your street reputation: how the criminal world sees you. It rises with missions, contracts, the neighborhoods you conquer and heists. It drops when you're arrested.

You can have both high, just one or neither. Everything is checked in one place: the phone, in Life, reputation and consequences. There you can read how the city sees you, how the street sees you and what you are gaining or losing because of it, and you'll find your criminal record, your job, your studies, your businesses, the bank and what you're going to be charged, what is known about your other life, the matters waiting for a decision from you and how far you've got in the story of the straight path.

None of this forces anyone. Whoever wants to play as always can: what you choose yourself, like a permanent contract, a degree, a loan or upgrading a business, doesn't show up unless you go looking for it, and what comes your way without looking for it can be adjusted or turned off in Options, in the Life, reputation and consequences section. By default it is set to mild.

How the city sees you: what standing brings

  • Pay. You earn ten percent more in honest jobs if people like you, and twenty if you're a model citizen. The bank gives the model citizen double interest.
  • Shops. In shops, in bars and restaurants and in gun shops you get a 5 percent discount if people like you and 10 if you're a model citizen. If the neighbors avoid you, they charge you more: 7 percent on mild, 12 on normal and 18 on demanding.
  • Credit. In shops, bars and restaurants, pharmacies and clothing stores, if you're short of money and people like you, they put it on your tab: up to 60 euros, or up to 150 for a model citizen. You pay it whenever you want on the phone and, if you let a week go by, it's collected automatically with the bills.
  • The neighbors. If they like you, they sometimes look the other way at a minor offense, and then it neither counts nor lowers your standing. If they avoid you, they're keener to call the police and the crime counts more.
  • The police. From 50 standing they overlook some of your offenses, as always. Also, if people like you and your record is clean, once a day an officer gives you the benefit of the doubt over a minor offense. With a bad reputation or a criminal record, at the normal and demanding levels, an officer stops you now and then and makes you lose a few minutes.
  • At the demanding level, where people avoid you, some shops refuse to serve you on some days.

How the street sees you: what street reputation brings

  • Bodyguards in the taverns charge you up to 12 percent less if your name rings a bell, and more if nobody knows you.
  • Bribes go better the more street reputation you have: the one for the officer who has caught you and the one for the inspector who has found something in your business.
  • With 20 street reputation, gangs respect your businesses. They also do if you have your own gang, a neighborhood, a pact or an escort.
  • Without any of that, anyone with a business is easy prey: every now and then a gang comes by asking for money "for protection". It's a pending matter with a deadline, and you decide: pay for a few weeks; report it, which goes better the more the neighbors like you; refuse and hold out, with the risk that your business wakes up damaged and earns less for a few days; or stand up to them, if you have some street reputation or an escort. If people like you, a neighbor warns you the day before.

Criminal record

Every arrest goes on your record, more serious the more you were wanted: a minor offense with one or two wanted levels, a medium one with three and a serious one with four or five. The fine you pay on the spot to an officer is also recorded, as a minor offense.

  • What they close off: joining the police, positions of trust and, if they're serious, loans and mortgages. While you have a record, every crime counts a bit more. If you have a permanent contract, your boss finds out: a warning, or dismissal if the offense is serious or the job is a position of trust.
  • They expire on their own: minor ones after 10 days, medium ones after 25 and serious ones after 50.
  • They can be reduced in two ways. With community service, at a police station, at a soup kitchen or from the phone: four unpaid hours take five days off the most serious one, once a day. And with a lawyer, who appeals the conviction: it drops one degree, or disappears if it was minor, only once per entry.
  • You can check them on the phone and at any police station, with what is on record, when each one expires and what it's closing off to you.

What you did before this version doesn't count: a game already in progress starts with a clean record.

Permanent contract: a professional career

At the job centers, as well as the usual one-off jobs, there are permanent contracts. They're optional: without a contract, you work when you want and nobody holds you to account.

  • There are five trades: hospitality, shops and warehouse, shipyards and repair shops, transport, and emergency and rescue services, which is a position of trust and requires a clean record. Each one says which jobs count as a shift: a working day is one shift, and with a vehicle, three services make one shift.
  • When you sign you get a boss with a name and a personality, a number of shifts to work every seven days and a payroll. You get paid for each shift as always; the payroll arrives separately, at the bank, at the end of the period.
  • At the end of each period, your boss evaluates you: what counts most is whether you've worked your shifts and, also, how you've done them, which depends on your mood and your experience. With a good rating you get your payroll plus a bonus; with an adequate rating, the payroll; with a poor rating, a warning and a reduced payroll; and if you haven't shown up, a penalty with no pay. Two days before the period ends, if you're short of shifts, your boss reminds you.
  • With three warnings you're fired, and that company wants nothing to do with you for a few days. A good evaluation wipes out a warning.
  • With three good evaluations you're offered the next position, if you meet what it requires: a degree and, in the higher ones, good standing. There are four positions in each trade. The top two carry real responsibility: now and then, at the end of a shift, you have to make a decision about your people, a customer or the accounts, and what you choose counts in the evaluation.
  • If you leave on good terms after three periods without warnings, your boss signs you a letter of recommendation: in your next job you start one position higher, if you have what it requires, and the bank looks on you more favorably.

Studies with a degree

At the academies, as well as the one-off courses that raise a skill, there are degrees. Each one takes several sessions, one a day, of three hours, and each session is paid for. You can study and work on the same day.

  • There are seven: hospitality, retail, industrial trades, lifeguarding and first aid, professional transport, administration and management, and business management.
  • They open up the best positions in permanent contracts. The administration degree opens the manager positions and the expansion of your businesses, and requires some standing. The business management degree, which requires the administration one first and quite a lot of standing, opens the highest positions and the top level of your businesses.
  • When you finish, a fanfare plays, your standing goes up and the degree is yours forever. The game then tells you which permanent contract it's useful for and where it's signed, which is at the Job center. A degree doesn't open a business: it lets you get promoted at work, and some let you upgrade the businesses you already have.

Your businesses: a company that grows

Businesses are bought as always, by going to them, and now they're also run. When you go into a business of yours, its menu has the management options:

  • Upgrades. Three levels above the starting one: the renovation, the expansion, which requires the administration degree, and making it a well-known brand in the region, which requires the business management degree and 60 standing. Each level makes it earn more and take one more employee.
  • Employees. Each one earns a daily wage, which comes out of the till, and makes the business earn more than they cost. With staff hired, the business holds up even if you don't drop by.
  • The business's name among the customers. It rises if you spend two hours tending it, once a day, and little by little with employees and low prices. It drops if more than five days go by without you showing up. With a good name it earns up to 25 percent more; with a bad name, less. If you have staff hired, the business holds up without you: even if you don't go, its name stays around 45, a little below normal, which barely takes anything off what it earns, and as soon as you come back and tend it, it rises again. With nobody hired, on the other hand, it sinks. So, with employees in each one, you can have businesses in several towns without going to all of them every few days.
  • Low, normal or high prices: with high prices you earn more on each sale and the name drops; with low prices, the opposite.
  • Normal or extended hours: opening more hours earns more, but needs at least one employee.
  • Inspections. Every so often one comes by one of your businesses. If everything is in order, the inspector signs, leaves and the business gains name.
  • Dirty money. What you earn from drugs, robberies, contracts from the board and the tributes from your neighborhoods is money you couldn't justify, and the game keeps track of it: you can see it on the phone, in Life, reputation and consequences. You can't just take it to the bank: if you have dirty money, the bank only accepts up to three thousand euros in cash a day without asking questions; above that, it asks for explanations, doesn't accept the rest and alerts the tax office, which brings forward the inspection of your businesses. There are four ways to launder it:
    • Little by little at the bank, without going over the daily limit.
    • At the casino: Launder money into chips. You buy chips with cash, play a little and cash out as winnings. Twelve percent is lost along the way, and no more than five thousand euros a day.
    • With the discreet fixer, at the casino and in the clubs: you give him at least five thousand euros in cash, he keeps a quarter and, three days later, deposits the rest in your bank, clean. Once in a while he disappears with the money.
    • With a business of yours as a front, as explained just below.
    All this counts with consequences and taxes turned on; without them, dirty money carries no weight. On an online server, the server keeps track of the money.
  • Front. This is where the two paths cross. The money from robberies, contracts from the board and the tributes from your neighborhoods is money you couldn't justify, and the game keeps track of it, which fades on its own over the days. A business set up as a front puts part of that money through its till every night, in exchange for a commission. If an inspection comes while the front is on, or while you have a lot of unjustified money, there's a problem: pay the fine and leave everything clean; try to bribe the inspector, which if it goes wrong brings the fine, a serious criminal record entry and the front page of the newspaper; or, if you have a lawyer, have them appeal it and cut it in half.

A newly bought business, and the ones you already had, earn exactly the same as always as long as you don't change anything.

The bank: loans and mortgages

  • The bank looks at your creditworthiness: your standing, your stable income, which is your payroll and what your businesses earn, and your criminal record, missed payments and seizures.
  • Personal loan. You request it at any bank, in Loans, mortgages and obligations. They offer you up to a certain amount, which they pay into your account, and you pay it back in eight installments, one every seven days, with more or less interest depending on your creditworthiness.
  • Mortgage. You request it when buying: in the house or business you want, next to buying in cash, there's buying with a mortgage. You pay 30 percent as a down payment and the rest in sixteen installments. The house or business is yours from the signing.
  • Installments are collected automatically, first from your bank account and then from what you have on you, with a warning two days before. If there isn't enough, they add a surcharge, try again three days later and you lose standing. After three missed payments in a row, seizure: they don't take anything from you all at once, but they keep half of your payroll and of what your businesses earn until the debt is settled, and in the meantime the bank won't lend to you.
  • Any loan can be paid off in one go, with a small discount.

Taxes and bills

Every seven days you're charged for the electricity and water of your homes, their property tax and a tax on what your businesses have earned in that time. They're small amounts: on mild, about 21 euros per home, a bit more for the expensive ones, and less than four percent of what the businesses earn. You get a warning two days before with the amount, it's collected automatically and you can check it whenever you want on the phone and at the bank. If there isn't enough money, it's left owing with a surcharge; after three missed payments, the same seizure as with loans. A new game, and one you had already started, have fourteen days of grace before the first charge. With the Taxes and bills option set to no, there are no bills, taxes or inspections.

Doing good, and temptations

As well as the usual donations, volunteering and street events, every now and then, on foot and with nothing else going on, you come across a situation: a wallet on the ground, someone who has fallen, two people forcing a shutter, a fight between neighbors, a neighbor loaded with bags, a lost child, someone sleeping rough or a forgotten phone. A menu opens and you decide: help in one way or another, go on your way or take advantage. Helping raises your standing and your mood; sometimes it costs a while, a few euros or a first aid kit, and once in a while it goes wrong. Taking advantage gives easy money and, if someone saw you, the neighborhood will know. With Escape you go on your way. They appear with the street events option turned on, and never in the middle of a mission, a job or a chase.

The double life

You can be both: liked by the neighbors and respected on the street. It has its rules.

  • If you have something to lose, which is a contract, a business, a loan or the neighbors' esteem, whatever gets known about your other life is noted down. It rises every time you're seen committing a crime, and quite a lot with each arrest and with an inspection that finds something on you. It drops on its own with quiet days.
  • What nobody sees doesn't count: discretion is the first protection.
  • When the rumor is already going around, the game tells you. If it keeps rising and you have a good name or a contract, a journalist prepares a story: it's a pending matter with a deadline. You can pay them to keep it quiet, let it come out or, if you have a lawyer, have the lawyer stop it.
  • If it comes out in the newspaper, you lose standing, your businesses lose name and your boss finds out: a warning, or dismissal if the job is a position of trust. On the street, though, you gain street reputation.
  • Ways to protect yourself: don't get seen; a business as a front for the money; bribes, with their risk; and a lawyer, who is hired on the phone for a weekly amount and, as long as you pay, makes everything that gets known weigh half as much, holds back the press and appeals the fines.

The straight path: a story without crimes

From the start there are two paths, and the game tells you so when you leave your aunt's after seeing her for the first time. The street: Encarna's errands take you to Chispas's repair shop and to the usual story. The straight path: at the Puerto Bravo Job center, Aurora, the career advisor, is waiting for you with a story of her own in nine chapters that can be done entirely without committing a single crime. You can follow both at the same time.

  • It tells how someone who has just got out of prison makes a place for themselves: the first contract, the first payroll, going back to studying, having a business, the neighbors, the gang asking for money and the inspector asking for an envelope.
  • Some chapters wait for your life to move forward: having a contract and having worked a few shifts, earning a degree, gaining some standing or having a business. While something is missing, the phone tells you what it is.
  • It has temptations and decisions, and four endings: the honest one, the cautious one and, for whoever accepts the gang's deal and leads a double life, two more.
  • Its missions don't start on their own. You go into the place as always and the mission is the last option in its menu, before Go back; the game reminds you when you go in. In a place with no menu, like the town hall, the game asks. Completing them raises your standing, not your street reputation.
  • Its scenes are played with recorded voices, like those in the rest of the story: each character has their own, they call you by your name and speak to you as a man or as a woman. With a character you created, the lines that say your name are generated by the game on your computer.

The options

In Options, in the Life, reputation and consequences section:

  • Consequence level: off, mild, normal or demanding. By default, mild. Off leaves the game as in earlier versions: standing only counts for pay, bank interest and the police; businesses earn the same as always; nobody asks your business for money and there is no double life. On mild, the bad things weigh a little more than half as much as on normal. On demanding, half as much again, and shops that won't serve you appear. The perks are the same at every level.
  • Taxes and bills: yes or no. By default, yes.
  • Criminal record: yes or no. By default, yes.
  • Love life suitable for everyone: yes or no. By default, yes.
  • Social life is lived in person: yes or no. By default, yes. It is explained in the section Social life is lived in person.
  • Opening hours: no, people, or people and venues. By default, no. It is explained in the section Opening hours, optional.

At any level you can sign a contract, study, take out a loan and upgrade your businesses: that's your choice.

On an online server

On a server the money is refereed by the server, so there it's the server that decides, the same for everyone, and not your consequence level.

  • Permanent contracts: they're signed at the job center as always, with their boss, their shifts, their evaluation, their warnings and their promotions. The difference is the money: the server counts the shifts that are valid, the calendar is the server's and the payroll is paid by the server, with its own figure, straight into your bank. It doesn't use up the hourly earnings cap. If the period ends while you're away, the evaluation reaches you when you log back in.
  • If the server is from an earlier version, or its owner has turned off payrolls, there are no permanent contracts there, and the game tells you. The one you bring already signed stays as it is.
  • Studying for a degree is always possible: it only costs your character money.
  • Criminal record entries are noted down and expire with the server's days, and they count for positions of trust. They belong to your character: they don't change how the server's police treat you.
  • How shops treat you according to your standing, if the server has it turned on: a small discount if people like you and a surcharge if they avoid you, the same for everyone, whatever level you have.
  • These don't exist there: loans, mortgages, taxes and bills, business management (businesses earn what the server sets), credit at shops, extortion, the double life and street situations. The game tells you if you try.
  • The story of the straight path doesn't move forward there either: it's for playing offline.
  • When you leave the server everything goes back to how you have it in Options: nothing the server turns off stays turned off.

The phone, in Life, reputation and consequences, tells you what there is on the server you're on.

With a controller

All this is done through menus: the phone, the job center, the academy, the bank, the police station and your businesses. With the controller you go through them and choose like any other menu, and there are no new keys.

What you hear

When you go into a venue the door sounds and, while you're inside, its background ambience plays; when you leave, the door again. A rooster announces dawn, an owl dusk and the chimes of a bell midnight.

Life and its consequences have four sounds of their own: a door slam and three falling notes when you're fired; knuckles knocking, papers and a stamp when an inspection comes to one of your businesses; a receipt printer and a low note when you're charged a bill, a tax or an installment; and a short fanfare when you earn a degree. A promotion to a higher position sounds like the usual rank promotion.

20. Mood and street reputation

The V key tells you your status: health, money, weapon, wanted level and vehicle. Your mood rises when you eat well, rest, win, do sport or help, and drops with injuries, arrests and failures; with a low mood you perform worse. Street reputation rises with heists and opens doors in the criminal world. Standing among the neighbors is the other side of the coin: it rises with an honest life. They are two independent scores, and what each one brings is in Life, reputation and consequences, in the section on honest life and on the phone.

21. Social life: people, dates and partners

The people you can have a relationship with: meeting them, meeting up, dates, being a couple and living together. It works with adult content and with the love life suitable for everyone; what only exists with adult content is in the next chapter.

Love life suitable for everyone

Without turning on adult content, the phone has Love life: the same people you can meet, with chats, dates, gifts, a kiss or a dance, becoming a couple, couple plans and giving them money to buy something. If the relationship is going very well and you've had quite a few dates, you can ask them to get engaged; once engaged, you can live together in one of your homes; and a few days later, get married, with a simple wedding the neighbors look on kindly. None of this has adult content, which stays behind its own option. With adult content turned on, your partner's profile is the usual one and also gains the engagement and the wedding. The Love life suitable for everyone option, which comes set to yes, removes it if you don't want it.

Social life is lived in person

With the Social life is lived in person option, which comes set to yes and is in Options, Life, reputation and consequences, the people in your social life are treated as in real life. It works the same for the love life suitable for everyone.

  • By phone, from each person's profile on the phone, wherever you are: call them, which brings you a little closer, once a day; talk to them in your own words; meet up at a place at a time; send them a gift, delivered, which costs the delivery and is appreciated a little less than in person; give them money; and break up.
  • In person: chat, which brings you closer than a call; suggest a date; give them a gift in person; a kiss, a dance and the other gestures; spend the night together; couple plans; ask them to be your partner, to get engaged or to move in with you; get married; and ask them to forgive you. You're face to face at the place where they usually are, at their time; at your home, if you live together and they're in; or while they're with you.
  • If you're not together, the profile tells you where they are and what you can do by phone.
  • What makes hours go by, like a date, the night or a plan, can't be done in the middle of a mission.
  • A date is lived. In person, Suggest a date and choosing the plan makes them come with you, and the GPS takes you to a place that suits that plan: a cinema for the movies, a bar, a tavern or a club for going out for drinks, a club or the fair for dancing, and a restaurant for dinner. The plan happens when you arrive, told in scenes, and afterwards you stay together until you say goodbye. Going for a walk is going together, with no fixed place.
  • With the option set to no, everything is done from the phone while in the same town, as before.

Meet up at a place at a time

  • In a person's profile, Meet up at a place at a time: you choose the plan and the time, among the ones they have free today and tomorrow, and the game looks for a place that suits that plan near where they usually are.
  • Half an hour before, the phone reminds you. If you arrive at the place up to half an hour before or after the time, they're waiting for you, and the plan is lived there, in scenes. If the plan is going for a walk, they come with you.
  • If you don't show up, you've stood them up, and they take it badly. Letting them know beforehand, by canceling the meetup, upsets them somewhat, but much less.
  • On the phone, in Social life or Love life, Your meetups tells you who you've arranged to meet, where and at what time. Enter marks the place on the GPS, and it can also be canceled.
  • The people from the street you have in Your contacts can also be invited to meet up for a drink: at a bar in their town, at the time you choose. If you go, you pay, your mood goes up, you become better friends and they tell you what's being said around. Friendship, from zero to a hundred, appears in the list.

People, in their place

The people in your social life aren't only on the phone. Each one is at the place where they usually are, and with opening hours turned on, only at their hours: when you go in on foot, the first option in the menu is to walk over and talk, and if you walk around inside the place, you find them there. It's the same menu as on the phone, but in person, so there you can do everything that is done face to face.

From there you can suggest they come out with you for a walk around. They really come along: they follow you on foot, you can hear their footsteps, and they get into whatever vehicle you're driving, be it a car, a motorbike or a bus. Walking around counts, and taking them to eat, to a bar, to the movies, to the fair or to the casino pleases them and brings you closer. Getting into trouble with the police doesn't. To say goodbye, take them back to where they were or tell them from their profile. Once a day, and it lasts at most three game hours.

If your partner finds out

  • If you have a partner and you meet up, spend the night, or kiss or dance with someone else, they may find out.
  • If they find out, the relationship drops a lot, and the wound lasts ten days: meanwhile, what you do together brings you half as close, and the profile reminds you how many days are left.
  • Asking them to forgive you, in person and once a day, can shorten it: if they listen, it takes off four days. If not, better not to insist that day.
  • If it happens again while the wound is still open, that's it: they leave you.

Couple plans

With your partner there are more things to do. In their profile, Make a plan together: an aimless walk around the neighborhood, going to the movies, dinner out, a boat trip at sunset, an afternoon at the beach, going shopping, a night at the fair or going dancing. Some require the town to have the right place, and the game tells you.

  • Each plan is told with a spoken scene, and it's not the same every time: none repeats until you've heard them all.
  • It brings you closer and raises your mood, more so for the ones that cost more. One a day.
  • They add to the dates, gifts and gestures that were already there.

Living together, optional

It comes turned off. It's turned on in Options, Adult content, Life as a couple. With the option turned on, your partner's profile shows Ask them to move in with you.

  • Each person decides. The relationship has to be going very well and you need to have had a good few dates; and there are people who won't leave their home for anyone, however well you're getting on. If they say no, there's no point insisting the next day.
  • If they accept, they live in one of your homes. If you have several, you choose which one when you ask them, in this town or another.
  • Moving: in your partner's profile, the option that says where you live, or in the house menu, Move to another home of yours, lets you choose another of your homes, also in another town, and you both go.
  • Giving them money to buy something: in your partner's profile, or at home, you choose how much, from fifty to five hundred euros. It brings you closer the more you give, once a day, and the next day they tell you what they bought. It works for any partner, whether they live with you or not.
  • At home: at night they're always there; during the day, only at times, because they have their own life. When you come in they greet you, or you find a note. In the house menu there's an option with their name: talk for a while, eat together, make a plan or go to their profile.
  • Eating together: they cook for both of you or, sometimes, it's your turn. It gives health and takes away hunger and thirst in realistic life. Once a day.
  • They ask you for things now and then: a delivered dinner, a plan, that you sleep at home, a piece of furniture, a small gift or some money. You have a couple of days. If you do it, it brings you closer; if you let it go, they take note.
  • Living together counts as seeing each other: the relationship doesn't cool off from not meeting up. But if you go days without showing up at home, it suffers.
  • There's one more plan, only for home: a night on the sofa, with a movie and a blanket.
  • You can ask them to move out. It doesn't end the relationship, but it cools it a lot. If you break up, they leave on their own.
  • If you bring someone else to that home and your partner is inside, it's over.

It can't be done on an online server. The numbers and phrases are in the game data.

22. Content for people over 18

It comes turned off. It's turned on in Options, Adult content, confirming that you're of legal age. Turned off, none of this appears in the game: not the places, not the missions, not the phone options. Everything is told without explicit scenes and all the characters are adults.

  • Social life, on the phone: you meet people in bars and other places. You can chat, suggest a date, give a gift, ask them to be your partner, spend the night together or break up. Affinity goes up and down with what you do, and there's jealousy if you're in more than one relationship. What is done in person and what by phone, how to meet up and what happens if your partner finds out is in the previous chapter, Social life.
  • Nightclubs: drinks, shows and paid company.
  • Substances: each one has its effect, its sound when you take it and its comedown. Abuse creates dependence, with withdrawal symptoms, and can end in an overdose. Hospitals offer detox.
  • Dealing: suppliers sell goods at a price that changes every day and in every place. On foot, carrying goods, the E key next to a pedestrian offers them a deal; they may accept, refuse it or be a plainclothes police officer.
  • Everything has an effect: on your mood, your health, your street reputation, your standing among the neighbors and your relationships.

Robbing a supplier

Substance suppliers can be robbed, as well as bought from. The option is in their menu. You need a firearm in your hand and to have nothing else going on.

  • They have a guard, and sometimes reinforcements who arrive in a vehicle once it has already started.
  • If you take them out, you pick up the goods and the cash box: you take whatever goods there are, as much as fits in your backpack, and the money.
  • They don't forget. One of these three things happens, and the game tells you which: that supplier stops selling to you for a few days; or keeps selling to you, but at a higher price, for a while; or sends people after you, who arrive a few hours later, when you're out on the street.
  • Even if the robbery goes wrong for you, the consequence stays.
  • The same supplier can't be robbed again until a few days have passed: they have no stock.

If you have a gang, the robbery can also be planned, with the Plan the heist with your gang option in that same menu: it's explained in the section Prepared heists: everyone has a role.

It can't be done on an online server. The numbers and phrases are in the game data.

A night with someone, step by step

Spending the night with someone is no longer told in two sentences: it's played. At each moment you choose what to do from three options, and the other person responds. Each person has a pace they feel like, which changes from one day to the next and isn't said: you notice it in how they react. If you get it right, they say so. If you ask them what they feel like, they tell you. And if you go faster than they want, they ask you to wait: stopping and asking them is what brings you closest.

Depending on how well you've understood each other, the night leaves you more or less mood and brings you more or less close. It's still told sensually, without explicit scenes, and a no is always respected. If you prefer it to be told in a couple of sentences, as before, turn off A night with someone, step by step, in Options, Adult content.

The lab

In a home of yours you can set up a clandestine lab for 9,000 euros, from the house menu. To work in it you need materials, which any supplier sells in batches.

  • Making it is a listening minigame. Two tones play: a fixed one, on the left, which is the sweet spot, and yours, on the right, which drifts up or down. With Up Arrow and Down Arrow you bring yours to match the fixed one and keep it there.
  • It lasts twenty-four seconds. The longer the two tones stay matched, the better the batch turns out and the more units you get, between four and sixteen.
  • If you let your tone stay very high for a long time, it can catch fire: you get burned, you lose the lab and the police come. Sometimes, on top of that, a neighbor notices the smell and calls.
  • Escape leaves it half done and the batch is lost.
  • What you make is sold like any other goods: to a supplier or on the street.

It's a trade invented for the game, like its substances: it doesn't describe any real process.

Flirting with people on the street

With adult content turned on, when you talk to a person with the J key more options appear: flirt, suggest going to your home, if you have one, or to a hotel. Each person is attracted to whoever they're attracted to, they may have a partner or not be in the mood, and it depends on how they've taken to you: no means no, and if you insist they leave. In Options, Adult content, you say who you're attracted to and, if you want, how others see you.

  • A night together can be one of four kinds, and you choose: romantic, passionate, full of laughs and games, or relaxed. With the people in your social life there are also gestures of affection: a kiss, dancing together, a massage or whispering something in their ear. It takes some trust and each one can be repeated once a day.
  • Everything is told with sensuality and without explicit scenes: when the door closes, the game moves on to the next morning.
  • Pharmacies and hospitals have condoms. In the clubs they also sell candles, massage oil, lingerie and a toy for two: each item makes one kind of night better.
  • Without protection you can catch an infection: you notice it in your mood and your health until you treat it. Pharmacies and hospitals have a free test and treatment. The V key tells you how you are.
  • In the clubs, as well as drinks and shows, you can dance on the floor and meet someone.

23. Playing with friends

You can play with other people without depending on any outside server: your own game acts as the server. You set up the game and carry on playing normally; you give your friends an address and they connect.

What is shared

  • You hear your friends where they are: the engine of what they're driving, their footsteps and their horn.
  • The radar announces them and the GPS takes you to them, even if they're in another town.
  • You send each other messages, which are read aloud. The F10 key opens the box for typing without going through the phone.
  • You can send each other money.
  • You can give each other first aid kits.
  • You can ride in the same vehicle: stand next to your friend's and press F to get in as a passenger; you go wherever they go. F again to get out.
  • Joint activities, from Play with friends: a race to a place with a hundred-euro bet each, won by whoever arrives first, and a team delivery run, in which you all carry packages around the city together and split the pay. One person proposes it, the others accept it and whoever proposed it starts it.
  • Each person keeps their own game, their missions and their progress, and can do everything they would do playing alone: missions, jobs, shopping, homes, fishing or races. Each person's city is their own: the people and traffic you hear are those of your game.

Opening your game

  1. In the game, open the phone with M and go into Play with friends.
  2. If you want, set a password: only those who know it will get in. Without a password anyone with the address can get in.
  3. Choose Open my game to my friends. The game tells you the address for anyone on your same network.
  4. In How to invite a friend you have both addresses, the home one and the internet one, and with Enter they're copied to the clipboard so you can paste them into a message.

An address looks like this: puertobravo://83.45.12.7:47810. The last number is the port.

So that friends from outside your home can get in

First of all: when you open your game, the game tries to open your router's port by itself, and tells you whether it succeeded. If it does, you don't have to do any of the following, and when you close the game it closes it again. It depends on your router allowing it; many come with it turned on. It can be turned off on the phone, in Play with friends, Open the router port automatically. If the game says it couldn't, these are the manual steps:

  • On your router you have to open port 47810, TCP protocol, to your computer. It's usually in the section on opening ports or port forwarding.
  • The first time, the Windows firewall asks whether to allow the game access: say yes.
  • If you use AdGuard, an antivirus with network protection or another connection filter, add Puerto Bravo to its exceptions. The game retries automatically when a connection drops right at the start, and reconnects if it drops in the middle of the game, but a filter that always blocks can't be bypassed.
  • Without opening the port only people on your same network will be able to get in.

Joining a friend's game

On the phone, Play with friends, Join a friend's game. Type or paste with Control V the address they gave you, and then the password, if there is one. The game remembers the last address.

While you play

  • On the phone, Play with friends shows who is there and where. With Enter on a friend you can mark them on the GPS or send them money.
  • Whoever opened the game can kick a player from that same menu.
  • Received messages can be reviewed in Review the latest messages.
  • Three sounds alert you: three rising notes when someone joins, three falling ones when someone leaves and two high notes when a message arrives.
  • The connection isn't encrypted: don't write anything you wouldn't say out loud.

When typing text

In the game's text boxes (messages, addresses, passwords, your name) each letter is spoken as you type it. Enter accepts, Escape cancels, Backspace deletes, Up Arrow reads what you've typed and Control V pastes. Passwords aren't spoken: you hear "asterisk".

Playing on a server

As well as playing with friends, you can play on a server: a world that never shuts down, which anyone can set up with the server program, and which anyone who has its address can join. On a server you do everything you do playing on your own, missions, jobs, homes, businesses, and on top of that there are the other players, with whom you can do all sorts of things.

Joining

  • In the main menu, Play on a server. Add a server with the address given to you by whoever runs it; it starts with puertobravo, colon, slash, slash. It stays in your list: with Enter on it you join.
  • The first time you choose a character. That character belongs to that server: it isn't any of your single-player games, and it's saved on the server, not on your computer. When you join again you carry on where you left off, with what you had.
  • There are no passwords or email. The first time, the game creates a key, your identity, and saves it in your data folder; each server recognizes you by it. If you delete the data folder or change computers you lose it, and with it your characters: in Play on a server, Your identity, you can copy it to keep it and restore it later. Treat it like a password.
  • Your player name is set in that same menu. If someone on a server already has your name, the server adds a number to yours.
  • A server may ask for a join password or an invitation code. The game asks you for it when you join.
  • The game tells you whether the connection is encrypted. It remembers the server's certificate and, if one day it's a different one, it warns you and doesn't join unless you accept it.
  • The server isn't tied to a version of the game, and people with different versions can play together on the same server. This is explained in the section "Playing with people on another version", a little further down.
  • Homes and businesses on a server: if you buy them while playing on it, when you sell them to the market you get your share back, and you can sell them to another player. The ones you bring from playing on your own are still yours and you use them the same way, but selling them there gives no money and they can't be sold to others: the server has no record that you paid for them on it.
  • The shops are set by each person's game, not by the server. Whatever your game or your mods sell, clothes, accessories or anything else, you can also buy while playing on a server: the server keeps track of the money, always lets you spend and saves your character with whatever they're carrying.
  • How another player is dressed: get close and press Shift J. The game says their name, how many meters away they are and what they're wearing. It only tells you when you ask. It also works when playing with friends. On a server with role-play channels, it also reads their profile.
  • The game can only be opened once, so each person plays one character.

What belongs to everyone and what is yours

  • Everyone's: the players, the vehicles you leave on the street, the shots between you, the gangs, the neighborhoods, the time, the day, the rain, the day's prices, the events and the lottery.
  • Homes and businesses also belong to everyone: each one has a single owner. And so do a few characters run by the server, with a name and a personality.
  • Traffic is everyone's: the cars on the road are run by the server, they stay in their lane, stop at traffic lights, which are the same for everyone, and brake if you step in front of them. The car you hear go by is the same one that the person next to you hears. To take one, step in front of it, wait for it to brake and press F beside it: the driver runs off and the car becomes yours and everyone's.
  • The police chasing a player are also in the game of those nearby: you hear their patrols with the siren and their officers are where they are. If you mess with them, what you do to them really happens to them, and the police start looking for you too.
  • The server keeps track of the money. You can always spend. What you earn from your story missions, what other players give you, loot and sales is justified. What you earn on your own from jobs, sales, gambling and what you pick up on the street has an hourly cap, set by whoever runs the server. Anything above that, the server doesn't recognize and it's withdrawn.
  • When the server withdraws money, the game tells you with figures: how much you've just earned, how much is accepted and how much is withdrawn, what that server's hourly cap is, how much margin you have left and how much you recover each minute. The margin you don't use is kept for you, for up to a few hours, also while you're away: after a while without earning anything you can have a whole good streak. You can check how you're doing on the phone, Online, Data and rules of this server.
  • Repeatable jobs, which are contracts from the board, gym fights, bank and shop robberies and car jobs, don't use up that cap: the server recognizes them, up to a number per hour of each type that also appears in Data and rules. If you go over that number, the game warns you and that job counts as any other earnings. On a server that isn't updated to 1.7.1 there's none of this: everything counts against the cap, and the warning doesn't include figures.
  • The people on the street are everyone's: pedestrians are run by the server and walk along the same sidewalks in every game. The person you hear go by is the same one that the person next to you hears. If someone shoots or shouts, they run away for everyone; if you mug one, the next person finds they have nothing left; if they go down, they go down for everyone. At night and when it rains there are fewer of them.
  • Each town also has some server patrol cars driving around, like one more car in the traffic. If you shoot near one, it sees you and the police start looking for you. If you're wanted and it passes near you, it comes after you.
  • The police are everyone's. Your game counts your crimes, but the wanted level is kept by the server, and it's the server that sends the patrols. The one coming after you is the same in the game of whoever is next to you: they hear it arrive with the siren, stop, and the two officers get out. With one wanted level they try to arrest you; with two or more, they shoot, and before each shot you hear them cock their gun. What happens to you when you're arrested is the usual: the fine, the bribe or the cell.
  • If the patrol car has stopped, the officers have got out and you escape in a vehicle, they don't just stand there: the ones still next to the car get back in and the patrol car goes after you. You hear the door.
  • Anyone can mess with those officers, not only the person being chased: if another player takes one down, the officer goes down for everyone, and the police start looking for that player too. With the officers out, the patrol car is left empty and anyone can take it; stealing it is one more crime.
  • Whether you've lost them is decided by the server: get away from patrol cars and officers until the alert that you're wanted stops sounding. On foot you need less distance than in a car. Painting the vehicle at a repair shop still works.
  • Leaving the server with the police on your tail doesn't clear it: if you come back within ten minutes, they're still looking for you. And the option to play without police doesn't apply on a server that runs the police itself.
  • Yours: your missions and your jobs.
  • The time is the server's and it doesn't stop. Sleeping restores you, but doesn't move the clock forward. If you go days without joining, when you come back the midnights you've missed count, up to a limit of three. The game tells you what has happened while you were away: what was stolen from your businesses, which is deducted from you; who took the vehicle you left on the street; and whether someone bought a property you had for sale.
  • The character is saved automatically every minute, a few seconds after buying something and when leaving. Saving at home or with F5 saves it on the server. If the connection drops right after buying something and there wasn't time to save, when you come back you won't have what you bought, but the server refunds the money it cost you.

The other players

  • To your game, another player is one more person on the street, except that a real person is controlling them. Everything you can do to a character you can do to them: lock on to them, shoot them, hit them, run them over, mug them, talk to them, pull them out of their vehicle or take the one they've left. And they can do the same to you.
  • You hear them where they are, on foot or in whatever they're driving, they appear on the radar and the GPS takes you to them. You also hear what they do: their shots, their shouts, their horn and their explosions sound in your game from where they are, and the people on your street get scared just the same. Whoever is being chased by the police can be heard coming with the sirens.
  • J next to another player, if there's no one else to talk to, writes a message just to them.
  • E next to another player, with a weapon in your hand and both of you on foot, mugs them: you take part of what they have in their pocket. They find out who it was and Q locks on to you. The same player can't be mugged again until five minutes have passed.
  • Open the phone with M and go into Online. In Who is here you'll find each player and what you can do with them: mark them on the GPS, write just to them, give them money, hand them a weapon, ammo or an item if they're next to you, invite them to your gang or to your mission group.
  • Messages: to everyone, with F10 or from Online; to those less than eighty meters away; to your gang; to your group; or to a single person. If the server has role-play channels, there are also in-character messages, actions and out-of-character messages: they're further down, in Role-play.
  • The shared activities are those from Play with friends: a race with a bet, a team delivery run and riding as a passenger.
  • Someone riding as a passenger in another player's vehicle is inside: neither that vehicle nor any other runs them over, they can't be mugged like someone on foot, and the bodywork protects them from shots just like the driver. When they get out they're one more pedestrian again.

Vehicles

  • The vehicle you get into becomes everyone's. If you leave it on the street, it stays there in the others' games too, and anyone can get in with F and take it. If it's taken from you, the game tells you who.
  • To pull another player out of the vehicle they're driving: on foot, next to them, Shift + F. They have to be going slowly. If you manage it, they're left on the street and the vehicle is yours. With F alone, next to someone else's stopped vehicle, you get in as a passenger.
  • Another player's parked vehicle can be wrecked. What you keep in your garage nobody touches.
  • Mission vehicles aren't shared: nobody can take the one you have to deliver.
  • Vehicles nobody uses disappear after a few hours, and each player can only have a few parked at a time. Whoever runs the server decides.
  • On a server, street vehicles are kept by the server, not by your game. My vehicles, on the phone, lists the ones the server has registered in your name and that are parked, as well as the ones in your garages; if another player takes one, it stops appearing. Whatever you want to keep safe, put it in a garage. The name you give a vehicle is kept when you leave it on the street, if the server is up to date.
  • From that list, with Enter on a vehicle of yours that's parked on the street, you can rename it, ask your mechanic to bring it to you, even if it's in another town, or send it to the garage of one of your homes, without having to go to it. They cost the same as when playing solo. The server saves the name and the other players see it too. If in the meantime another player has taken it, the game tells you and nothing is charged. On a server that isn't updated to 1.7.1 the three options appear the same, but when you press them the game tells you that server doesn't know how to do it yet: there you have to go to the vehicle.

Homes and businesses

  • On a server, a home or a business belongs to a single player, like a car: if you buy it, nobody else can have it. Grandma's apartment is everyone's own.
  • Online, Homes and businesses, tells you what is still available in your town, what another player is selling and at what price, and who owns the rest. Enter marks the place on the GPS.
  • At the door of a place that already has an owner, the menu tells you who it belongs to. If it's for sale, you can buy it from them right there: the money goes to them, whether they're connected or not.
  • You can put what's yours up for sale to other players, at whatever price you want, or sell it to the market: you get part of it back and it becomes available again for anyone. Both options are in the menu of your home or your business.
  • The properties of someone who hasn't joined for many days go back to the market, and their share is kept for them for when they return. That way there's always something to buy in the end.
  • Robbing another player's business: at its door, on foot and with a weapon, the menu offers Rob the business. The alarm goes off, the owner and their gang get an alert with the place marked on the GPS, and you have to hold out for twenty seconds without moving away. If you manage it you take the day's takings. If they take you down or you leave earlier, nothing.
  • What was stolen is deducted from the owner at midnight, or when they join again if they weren't there. Each business's till can only be emptied once a day, and you can't rob the business of someone in your gang or in an allied gang.

The server characters

  • As well as the players, the server runs a few characters, the same for everyone. They have names, walk around the town and appear in Online, Who is here, marked as server characters.
  • Each one has their own personality, and they appear at random: the one who strolls around and says hello; the pickpocket, who comes up to you and mugs you; the gunman, who goes after anyone with a price on their head; the one who takes the cars you leave parked and leaves them somewhere else; and the one who robs players' businesses.
  • You can do the same to them as to a player: lock on to them, shoot them, run them over, mug them. Some run away and others fight back. Taking down one who hasn't done anything to you counts like taking down anyone: wanted level and a price on your head.
  • Whoever runs the server decides how many there are, or that there are none.

Fighting other players

  • Whether a shot or a blow of yours reaches another player is decided by the server, not by your game: it looks at the weapon, the distance and the rate of fire.
  • Q locks on to whoever attacked you. Shift + Q cycles through everyone near you, including players who haven't done anything to you. From Online, Who is here, you can also lock on to someone.
  • When you're attacked you hear it, the game says who it was, and from that moment Q locks on to them.
  • Safe zones: next to hospitals, police stations, homes, animal shelters and soup kitchens, and inside any venue, nobody can hurt anybody. The game warns you when you enter and leave.
  • When you arrive on the server and when you respawn you have a few seconds of protection.
  • Passive mode, in Online: you neither hurt nor get hurt. It can't be changed in the middle of a fight, nor more than once every two minutes.
  • If you're taken down by another player you wake up in the hospital, as always, and they take part of what you had in your pocket. What's in the bank, your homes and your businesses are not touched.
  • Whoever takes down someone who wasn't defending themselves becomes wanted by the police and gets a bounty on their head, collected by whoever takes them down.
  • Leaving the game in the middle of a fight doesn't save you: the character stays there for fifteen seconds.
  • Grenades, Molotov cocktails and rockets hit players within their radius. Running people over also counts.

Gangs and neighborhoods

  • In Online, Your gang, you can found one, with whatever name you want. It costs money. You're the boss.
  • The boss and their right hand invite other players. The boss hands out the ranks, can hand over command, kick someone out and disband the gang.
  • The cash box is shared: anyone can pay in; the boss and the right hand take money out. Every transaction is recorded with the name of whoever made it.
  • Gang friendly fire: the boss decides. By default it's off and gang members can't hurt each other.
  • Neighborhoods: while inside a neighborhood that belongs to no one, the boss or the right hand can claim it. The cash box pays for it, and from then on the neighborhood leaves money in the cash box every server midnight. The gang can have one more neighborhood than it has members.
  • Wars: to take a neighborhood from another gang you have to declare war on it, while you're in it. It doesn't start right away: the server alerts both gangs and it starts a few minutes later, so nobody has a neighborhood taken while they're asleep. During the war, each gang scores one point per second for each member who is inside the neighborhood and on their feet, and thirty for each rival they take down inside. Whoever has more wins; if it's a tie, the neighborhood stays with whoever had it.
  • Alliances: they're sealed when both bosses propose one to each other. Allies can't hurt each other or declare war on each other.
  • Online, Gangs, neighborhoods and wars, tells you which gangs there are, who owns each neighborhood and which wars have been declared or are under way.

Group missions

  • From Online, Who is here, invite someone to your mission group. Up to four fit.
  • Whoever leads the group starts one of their missions, one of the usual ones. The others hear what each step requires and the GPS takes you to the place.
  • The mission's enemies also appear in the others' games, and whatever one player takes off an enemy is taken off for everyone.
  • Group members can't hurt each other.
  • When it's completed, whoever led it collects the full reward and each of those who were with them in the town gets half. It's only collected once per mission.

Role-play: speaking in character, actions and profile

If whoever runs the server has the role-play channels turned on, which is how they come, as well as the usual chat you can play your character in writing. Everything is read by your screen reader, and each channel sounds different so you know what it's about before you hear the sentence.

  • With a controller, the three channels and the usual message are in the actions menu: View, or Create or Share, held, and the right trigger or R2. Also on the phone, Online, Role-play. You type with the letter wheel or choose one of the ready-made phrases with X or Square; with the keyboard, the ready-made phrases come up with Tab.
  • Speaking in character: Shift F10. It's what your character says. Only those near you hear it, within about thirty meters if whoever runs the server hasn't set another distance. Two low notes play.
  • Describing an action: Control F10. You write what your character does, for example "opens the door", and those nearby hear your name followed by that: "Ana opens the door". It has the same range. A short rustle with a note plays.
  • Speaking out of character: for what you say as a player, a question or a notice. The whole server hears it. Two dry taps play.
  • If there's nobody close enough, the game tells you, so you don't talk to yourself without knowing it.
  • Commands: in any message, including the F10 one, you can start with slash me (or slash yo) and a space for an action, slash ic (or slash ep) to speak in character, slash ooc (or slash fp) to speak out of character, or slash ficha to change your profile.
  • Your profile: a sentence, of up to two hundred characters, that says who your character is. You write it on the phone, Online, Role-play, Your profile. It's saved on the server with your character.
  • Reading another player's profile: on the phone, Online, Who is here, choose that person and Read their profile. Or, if they're near you, Shift J: after telling you how they're dressed, the game reads their profile, if they've written one. With a controller, in the actions menu, or with View held and the Share button, the touchpad or the microphone button.
  • Reviewing what was said: on the phone, Online, Role-play, there's a list with what was said in character and the actions, and another with what was said out of character. Online, Review the latest messages, has everything together.
  • If the server has the role-play channels turned off, or is from an earlier version, the game tells you when you try to use them, and Online shows it.
  • The game doesn't have microphone voice chat. To talk out loud with your people while you play, use any separate voice program.

The radio in another player's vehicle

  • When you're near another player's vehicle that has one of the game's stations on, you hear it faint and muffled, the way you hear a radio from outside, from where their vehicle is. When you move about thirty meters away it fades out.
  • It only plays if you don't have your own radio on, and only one at a time: the one in the nearest vehicle. If you're riding as a passenger with them, you hear their radio.
  • The first time, the game tells you what's playing and whose it is.
  • It only works for the stations that come with the game. If you have My music on, or a station made with your own files, the others hear nothing and nothing leaves your computer, not even the name.
  • The only thing shared with the others is which station you have on. Each game plays its own copy, so the song may not be the same one you hear.
  • To stop hearing it: Options, Sound and music, On a server, hear the radio of other players' vehicles. Whoever runs the server can also turn it off for everyone.

If the server restarts or the connection drops

The game no longer sends you back to the menu. It stays on a waiting screen, with your game just as it was and the world paused, and it rejoins by itself.

  • When whoever runs the server is going to restart it to update it or for maintenance, the server warns beforehand with a countdown: the game tells you and you don't have to do anything. Your character is saved just before it closes.
  • When it closes, the waiting screen opens. It says what is happening: that the server is updating, that it's restarting, or that the connection has dropped. And that it will reconnect you as soon as it's back.
  • While you wait a faint two-note tone plays every few seconds: it means it's still waiting. At thirty seconds, at one minute, at two minutes and then every two minutes, it tells you how long you've been waiting. Any key repeats how it's going: how long you've been waiting, what happened on the last attempt, when it will try again and how much longer it will wait. F1 explains the screen.
  • The game tries to join every few seconds, more patiently each time: after three seconds, after five, after eight, after twelve, and then every fifteen.
  • When the server comes back, you join by yourself, with your usual identity, without the game asking you for anything. You carry on just as you were: same town and same spot, the vehicle you were in, your health, your weapons, your money, your mission with its step and the station you had on. The time is the server's, which saves it when it stops.
  • The server still decides about the money: when you come back, your game tells it what you have and it checks it with its usual rules. If everything was saved, which is normal, nothing changes.
  • If the police were looking for you: on a server that runs the police itself, the server saves the wanted level and they're still looking for you when you come back; on the others, your level stays as it was.
  • Whatever depended on other players is rebuilt if they come back too. The mission group is rebuilt automatically only by whoever led it, as the others join, for three minutes; if someone doesn't come back, the game says who is missing and how to invite them later. If you were riding as a passenger in someone else's vehicle, you're left on foot where you were, and the game tells you. If while the server was coming back another player took the vehicle you were in, it tells you that too.
  • Escape stops waiting and goes to the main menu. Your character stays on the server as it was at its last save, and the game tells you when that was.
  • With a controller, the waiting screen works like the others: A or Cross repeats how it's going, B or Circle stops waiting and goes to the main menu, and Y or Triangle explains the screen. With a controller connected, the game says those buttons instead of the keys.
  • If the server doesn't come back within the maximum time, which is ten minutes by default, the game says so and goes to the main menu. Your character is still saved on the server.
  • If the server comes back asking for a newer version of the game, the screen says so clearly and offers to look for the update with Enter. After updating, when it opens, the game offers to rejoin that server. If you'd rather do it by hand: main menu, Play on a server.
  • If it comes back but doesn't let you in as before, for example because its certificate or its joining rules have changed, the game explains why and goes to the main menu. Your character is still saved on the server.
  • If whoever runs the server shuts it down completely, or you're kicked, there's no waiting: you go back to the main menu, as always.
  • In Options, Play online, you choose whether you want the game to wait and rejoin by itself, which comes turned on, and how many minutes it waits at most, from 1 to 120. With the option turned off, everything is as before: you go back to the main menu and join yourself.
  • With a version 1.7.0 server, which doesn't warn or say why it's closing, the game waits anyway and tries to get back in, in case it comes back.

Leaving

Online, Leave the server, or the pause menu. The character is saved: the latest was saved less than a minute ago, and what you bought, a few seconds after buying it.

Setting up your own server

Anyone can set up a server, for two friends or for a hundred people. Everything you need comes with the game, in the servidor folder.

  • On Windows, the easiest way is the panel: in the servidor folder, open Panel del servidor (the server panel). It's a window that works with a screen reader. F5 starts the server and Shift F5 stops it.
  • From the panel you can see who is inside, send an announcement to everyone or a message to one player, kick, ban, appoint administrators, create invitations, change the configuration by tabs, and make and restore backups.
  • Those who prefer it have the same server in a text window: Servidor por consola (the console server), in the same folder.
  • It can also be set up on Linux, on a Mac, on a Raspberry Pi, on a NAS, on a rented server or with Docker. There you don't need the game: Python and the otros_sistemas folder, which is inside the servidor folder, are enough.
  • There's no fixed player limit: whoever runs the server sets it, and can set none.
  • The configuration, the characters and the backups are saved in the servidor folder. Before updating the game, stop the server and close the panel.
  • The complete step-by-step guide, with how to open the router port and all the options explained, is in the servidor folder, in documentacion_en.html, and also in data, docs, servidor_en.html. From the panel it opens with F1.

Playing with people on another version

You don't all need to have the same version of the game. The server doesn't turn anyone away because of their version, unless its owner asks for a minimum version, and then the game tells you which.

  • The server's world (places, weapons, vehicles, missions) is provided by the game: that of the first person to join and, when someone with a newer version arrives, that of that version. Nobody has to do anything, and nobody gets kicked.
  • What affects both players is decided by the server with the same figures for everyone: the damage, range and rate of fire of each weapon, and the money. Having the new version gives no advantage in a fight.
  • If another player uses a weapon or a vehicle your version doesn't have, you hear and see them as the closest thing your game knows, and the damage is what the server sets.
  • If you're the one who has something the server's world doesn't know, the game warns you: against other players that weapon counts as being bare-handed, that vehicle is seen as a compact car, and in a town the server doesn't have nobody sees you.
  • You can't give another player a weapon their version doesn't have: the server gives it back to you and tells you.
  • Your character lives on the server and loses nothing by changing version. If one day you join with an earlier version, you won't see the weapons or vehicles that version doesn't know, but they're still saved, and when you come back with the new one you have everything, along with whatever you did in the meantime.
  • You can also join with mods that change the world, unless the server owner doesn't want that: you play in what you have in common and what your mod adds doesn't count against the others.
  • When you join, the game and the server tell you what doesn't match. You also have it on the phone, Online, Data and rules of this server.
  • What only exists in the new version and doesn't depend on the server is available to whoever has that version. A server from an earlier version still lets you in, but doesn't have the new things: the game tells you when you try.

What's new in 1.7.1 on a server

Two differences: the server clock is everyone's, so cooking doesn't move it forward, and food goes off with the server's days. If a home stops being yours, what was in its kitchen is lost.
  • These work the same as when playing solo: food delivery, robbing any venue with a till, contracts from the board, asking people for a favor and couple plans. Enemies who arrive in a vehicle in solo play arrive on foot on a server.
  • The new weapons, repair shop upgrades and vehicles work on a server that has the 1.7.1 data: they're brought by the first player who joins with that version. On one still running earlier data, a new weapon counts as being bare-handed against other players, and the game warns you. Those playing with 1.7.0 get them as the closest thing their game knows.
  • Permanent contracts work on a server that pays payrolls: the server pays the payroll. It's in the Life, reputation and consequences section.
  • In group missions, someone who helps in another player's gym fight no longer gets a crime counted for taking down the opponent. Your teammate's enemies also have, in your game, the aim and voice of their type.

There, the neighborhoods and gangs belong to the players, and the police may be the server's.

24. People on the street

The people you pass are not extras. Each one has a first name and a surname, an age, a job, a personality and a mood for the day, and no two games have the same neighbors. The first names, surnames, jobs and phrases come from lists that can be expanded or changed with the mod editor, in the People on the street section. On foot, with someone next to you, the J key opens the conversation.

  • You can say hello, ask about the neighborhood, ask what's new, ask what they do for a living, pay a compliment, tell a joke, buy them a coffee or give them some spare change.
  • Ask about a place: they tell you where there is a pharmacy, a garage or whatever you are looking for, and mark it on your GPS.
  • The news they tell you comes from what is really happening in your game: the weather, whether there is a holiday or a strike today, whether the police are looking for someone, where there is work.
  • Ask for their number and become friends: if they like you, they join your contacts. They are on the phone, in Your contacts, with their job and their town. You can call them and meet up with them for a drink, as explained in the section Meeting at a place and time.
  • Each person reacts according to their personality and your reputation: people answer better to someone with a good name among the neighbors, and with fear to someone known to be dangerous.
  • You can also insult or threaten them to make them give you money. It's a crime, and people remember.
  • Write whatever you want to say: you type a sentence in your own words and the person answers according to what they understood.

People act too

The people you talk to don't just answer. The game itself decides, with no artificial intelligence, based on each person's personality and how much they trust you:

  • They walk with you for a while: if they like you, especially if they are chatty or friendly, they may start walking with you. You hear their footsteps, they comment on something, and after a while they say goodbye. They don't get into vehicles, and if the police are after you, they leave.
  • They hit you: if you keep talking to someone you have offended, they may throw a punch at you. People with a temper stay and fight; others hit you once and leave. They started it, so hitting back is self-defense.
  • They report you: if you insult someone and they walk away, they may call the police.

All of this can be turned off in Options, The street, The people you talk to act on their own too. And there are two new things you choose, which are always in the conversation:

  • Ask a favor: it takes trust, and one a day per person. What they do depends on your situation: if the police are after you at a low level and there are no officers nearby, they hide you and the search stops; if you are low on health, they patch you up; otherwise, they give you a tip about something nearby and mark it on your GPS, or they speak well of you in the neighborhood.
  • Ask them to join your gang: you need a territory and room in the gang. They also have to trust you, your name has to be known on the street, and they need the personality for it: very shy people and older people say no, each in their own way. They ask for a bonus, smaller than what recruiting from the phone costs. If they accept, they stay with you under their own name, fight at your side and get paid like the others.

On an online server you can't recruit people from the street: there, gangs are made of players. The numbers and phrases are in the game data.

People react, and you can hear them

Each person has their own voice, a man's or a woman's, and uses it to scream and complain. When someone is frightened by a gunshot, an explosion, your shout with H or a robbery, you hear them scream as they run off, from where they are. When they take a hit, they complain in their own voice. And the game says in words what is happening: "A woman screams and runs off, to the east", or "A man jumps out of the way" if it's because of the horn. If several react at once, it says so in one go: "Three people run off". To avoid overload, it says no more than one of these sentences every eight seconds; the screams are all heard, though. If you already know that person, it says their name. The sentence can be turned off in Options, The street; the sounds can't.

Someone with a woman's voice has a woman's name when you talk to her, and the same goes for men.

People who talk as you pass

Some people walk in pairs. When you walk past two people, or go by slowly, you sometimes hear what they are saying: a murmur and a stray sentence about the weather, football, work, prices or the neighborhood, and sometimes what the other one replies. They come from the same phrases people use to answer you, so they rarely repeat. You don't hear them in the middle of a chase, a fight or a scene.

How often you hear them is chosen in Options, The street, People who talk as you pass: never; rarely, which is the default, one every two or three minutes at most; now and then; or often.

Talk about anything, with no artificial intelligence

To write whatever you want, you don't need to turn anything on or have internet. The game comes with a base of more than eleven hundred phrases spread across almost seventy topics, and it recognizes what you are talking about: the weather, work, family, football, food, music, the neighborhood, the port, the police, gangs, money, health, holidays, animals, love, your sorrows and your joys, and many more.

  • Each person answers according to their personality: the chatty one tells you their life story, the curt one answers in two words, the shy one hesitates, the cocky one shows off.
  • Trust counts too. Someone who doesn't trust you won't even tell you their name; someone who likes you calls you by your name.
  • The sentences are built from pieces, and each person remembers what they have already said, so it's very hard to hear the same thing twice. If you keep on the same topic, they point it out.
  • Saying thank you or apologizing raises trust a little. Asking for money or talking about weapons lowers it.
  • If you write something it doesn't recognize, it answers the way anyone would answer an odd question or a passing remark.

With AI mode turned on, which is explained next, the artificial intelligence answers; if it's not turned on, or if it fails, this base answers.

AI mode

In Options, People and artificial intelligence, you can turn on AI mode. With it, when you write what you want to say to someone, an artificial intelligence answers, playing that person with their personality and their mood at that moment, and knowing where you are, what time it is and what the weather is like. You can talk about anything and it never answers the same way twice.

You can use whichever service you like, or several at once:

  • Local model: a small artificial intelligence that runs on your own computer. It's free, needs no internet or key, and nothing you write leaves your computer. It answers somewhat more slowly and with less wit than the internet services. How to install it is explained further down.
  • Google AI Studio, Gemini: it has a free tier. You get the key at aistudio.google.com.
  • OpenAI: paid. You get the key at platform.openai.com.
  • Anthropic, Claude: paid. You get the key at platform.claude.com.
  • Another compatible service: any that uses the OpenAI format, such as OpenRouter or Groq. Some have free models. You enter its address and its key.

You don't need to know anything about models or keep track of them. The game asks each service which models it has, picks the one from the cheapest range, remembers it for a week and, if one day that model disappears, finds another on its own. On the paid services, a conversation costs fractions of a cent.

In the Service option you choose which one is used. With Automatic, which is the default, the game tries the free ones first: the local model and then Google AI Studio. After that, the other services that have a key. If one fails or reaches its limit, it moves on to the next without you having to do anything.

  • The keys are entered on that same screen, can be pasted with Control V and are stored encrypted on your computer. They don't appear in the session log.
  • Test now asks a test question and tells you which service and which model answered, and how long it took.
  • What gets sent is what you write, the person's profile, the time, the neighborhood, the weather and how the neighbors see you. Your saved game, your files and none of your personal data are sent. On Google's free tier, what is sent may be used to improve their products; with the local model nothing leaves your computer.
  • Without AI mode, or if no service answers, the game itself answers, with no internet.

The AI can suggest actions, and the game decides

This is a separate option, in the same menu, and it's off by default. It only counts with AI mode turned on, and it doesn't work with the local model. With it, besides answering, the model can suggest that the character do one thing from this list, and no other: walk with you for a while, leave, get angry or join your gang.

  • The model suggests; it doesn't decide. What happens is decided by the game, with the same rules as without artificial intelligence: for someone to join your gang you need the territory, the trust, the reputation, the personality and the bonus, no matter who suggests it.
  • That's why there is no point writing to it that it should obey you, or trying to talk it into something the rules don't allow.
  • There is no money and there are no items on the list. No character gives you anything because of what you write.
  • When the action happens, the game describes it after the answer.

With the option off, the artificial intelligence can only make the character leave or get angry, as before.

Getting it running, in three steps

  1. In the main menu or in the pause menu, go to Options, People and artificial intelligence.
  2. Choose one of two things. The simplest and free option is to install the local model: it downloads once, you can leave it in the background with B, and when it finishes AI mode turns on by itself. The other is to enter the key for an internet service, for example the free one from Google AI Studio, and set AI mode to yes.
  3. Press Test now to check that it answers. Then, on the street, stand next to someone, press J and choose Talk to them in your own words: you type and that person answers you.

Installing the local model

It's all done from inside the game, without leaving it:

  1. Go to Options, People and artificial intelligence, and press Enter on the Local model line.
  2. Choose one of the three models and press Enter. The help for each one, with F1, tells you how much space it takes, how much memory it uses and how long it takes to answer.
  3. Wait for the download to finish. The game reads out the percentage; Escape cancels it. Everything goes in the data\ia_local folder.
  4. When it finishes, AI mode is turned on and the model starts loading. Go back to the previous menu and press Test now.

The three models, actually measured with the game's conversations:

  • Balanced, the recommended one: 770 megabytes on disk and 1 gigabyte of memory. It's the one that answers most sensibly, in short sentences. On a modern computer it answers in 1 second; on an old four-core one, in about 6; on a two-core one, in about 10.
  • Light: 510 megabytes on disk and 1.2 gigabytes of memory. It's the fastest on modest computers, about 4 seconds with four old cores, but sometimes it answers things that don't make much sense.
  • Medium: 1.2 gigabytes on disk and 2.3 of memory. Only for modern computers, where it answers in 1 or 2 seconds. On an old one it takes more than 10.

Keep in mind what it is: a small artificial intelligence. It speaks correctly and follows the conversation, but it makes things up, for example where a pharmacy is, and it doesn't have the wit of the internet services. To ask about real places, use the Ask about a place option in the conversation menu.

The game takes care of the model: it starts it when the game opens if AI mode is going to use it, stops it if you turn off AI mode or choose another service, and always closes it when you quit. Even if the game closes because of an error, or you close it from the Task Manager, the model closes with it: nothing is ever left open or using memory.

In that same menu you can change models, which replaces the previous one, or remove it, which deletes the folder. The files are downloaded from the official pages of the llama.cpp program and of the models, and the game checks that they are exactly the expected ones before using them. If you already use Ollama or LM Studio and have a model loaded, you don't need to install anything: the game detects it.

Recorded voices in the scenes

The mission scenes are voiced: each character in the story has their own voice, different from the others, and the text still appears in braille and in the history. There are more than twelve hundred recordings, between the game and its added stories.

  • They call you by your name. If you play as Dani, Álex, Sam or Cris, the other characters say that name, and they address you as a man or a woman depending on what you chose.
  • You have a voice too. Each of the four characters has a male voice and a female voice; the one that plays matches how they address you.
  • With a character you created, the lines that say your name can't come prerecorded: the game generates them on your computer, with the same character voices. This is explained further down, in Your own character's voices.
  • A line that has no voice is read by your screen reader, as always. This happens with very few, and with those from mods that don't come with recordings.
  • With a gamepad connected, the lines that name a key are read by the screen reader, so it can say the button.
  • In Options, Voice, the Voices in the scenes option turns them off or on. With no, everything is read by your screen reader or, if you don't use a screen reader, by the Windows voice.

The voices are not actors: they are computer-generated with Phonon, a voice engine from the company Gradium, with its Phonon 40m model. That model has a Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial license, CC BY-NC 4.0; the full text comes with the game, in the file LICENCIA_Phonon_Gradium.txt. Up to version 1.6.2 the voices were different ones, made with Microsoft's VibeVoice, and they only played with Dani as a man: when you update, the game replaces them with the new ones.

Your own character's voices

If you create your character, in the story they call you by the name you typed. To make that play with a voice and not with the screen reader, the game comes with a voice generator and uses it on your own computer, offline.

  • When the game starts, a screen appears that says Preparing your character's voices. It prepares the first lines that name you and tells you how it's going. It takes a few seconds; on a slow computer, a bit longer. With Escape or Enter you start without waiting.
  • The rest, about thirty-five lines, gets prepared while you play, without you having to do anything. On a fast computer it's a matter of a couple of minutes; on a slow one, quite a bit longer.
  • If you reach a line before it's ready, your screen reader reads that one. Nothing is left unsaid.
  • It's only done once per character: what has been prepared is saved in data\voces_generadas. If another day you create a character with the same name, the same voice and the same form of address, it's already done. That folder can be deleted whenever you like; whatever is missing gets prepared again.
  • What you say in the scenes doesn't need to be generated: it plays with the voice you choose when creating the character, from those of Dani, Álex, Sam and Cris, male or female depending on how they address you.
  • In Options, Voice, the Generate the voices of your own character option turns it off. With no, the lines that say your name are read by your screen reader or the Windows voice. If you notice the game stuttering while the voices are being prepared, turn it off: it stops generating right away, even with the game in progress. If you turn it back on, it picks up where it left off.
  • The generator needs a processor with the AVX2, FMA and F16C instructions, which almost all processors made since 2013 or 2015 have. The game checks all three before using it. On an older one the game works the same and all the other voices play, but the lines with your name are read by the screen reader. The option itself tells you whether your computer can do it.
  • The first time, the game tests the generator separately, in the background, before using it: it takes a few seconds, while you type your character's name. If the generator fails on your computer, it's that test that fails and not the game: the game carries on, remembers it so it doesn't keep trying, and the lines with your name are read by the screen reader. The test runs again by itself when you update the game. If you press Listen to how you will be called while the test is running, the game tells you to wait a moment.
  • The lines are generated in the game's language, with voices of that language: in English, with English voices. If you change language, the other language's lines are prepared separately and the ones you already had are kept.
  • Uncommon names may not sound right the first time. That's why, when creating the character, you can listen to how you will be called and, if needed, write how it's pronounced.

Recording your own voices

If someone records the lines of a mission, from the game or from a mod, the game uses those recordings: the sound files go in the data\voces\propias folder, one per line, and each line that has a recording plays with that voice.

  • The files can be WAV, OGG, FLAC or MP3, and they can be in subfolders.
  • Each file is named with the mission, the step number and the line number, separated by underscores. For example, oc01_1_2 is the second line of the first step of mission oc01.
  • That file, with nothing after it, is the one that plays with Dani as a man, and with any protagonist if the line doesn't depend on who it is. The lines that name the protagonist, that change depending on whether they are a man or a woman, or that the protagonist says themselves have more forms, and the name is followed by an at sign and who it's for: oc01_1_2@alex if they call them Álex, oc01_1_2@m if they address them as a woman, oc01_1_2@alex_m if it's both, or if the protagonist says the line.
  • You don't need to work out the names: the mod editor, with Control G, writes the full script of a mod, with each line, who says it, the name its file must have and its other forms.
  • You don't have to record them all. For someone playing with a protagonist who has no recording of a line, that line is read by the screen reader; someone else's never plays.
  • Your recordings, or ones someone else gives you, go in data\voces\propias. They take priority over the ones that come with the game, and updates don't touch that folder.
  • The voices that come with the game are in data\voces, in one subfolder per story. Each version comes with its own and, the first time it starts, the game puts them there and removes the previous version's.

25. Every day is different

Puerto Bravo doesn't repeat itself. Each game is born with its own luck, and every midnight decides what the next day will be like.

  • Most days are normal. On others there are sales at the gun shops, market days with cheaper food, or shortages with everything more expensive. There are holidays, with the streets full of people and few cars; transport strikes, with hardly any traffic; holiday getaway days, with traffic jams; days when there isn't a soul around, and cruise days, with tourists everywhere.
  • The game announces it at midnight with an audible alert. The T key, which tells you the time and the weather, reminds you; the news station reports it and the people on the street talk about it.
  • Also, each shop has its own price each day, a little above or below the shop next door. It's worth comparing.
  • Other things also change on their own: the weather, the cars on the road and the parked ones, the people you pass, street incidents, the stock market, the lottery and, in adult mode, the suppliers' prices.

26. Realistic life: hunger, thirst, sleep and hygiene

It's optional and off by default. It's turned on in Options, Combat and difficulty. With it you have to eat, drink, sleep and wash, as in real life.

  • Thirst kicks in before hunger: about nine hours of game time without drinking. When you are very thirsty you run slower and, if you ignore it, you lose health. You quench it with a bottle of water, sold in bars, restaurants and shops; with a small beer; by eating in your kitchen; by sleeping in a hotel; or, for free, at places with a fountain, such as some squares and parks, which have the Drink water from the fountain option. In the mod editor, any place can have a fountain: it's the Has a drinking fountain checkbox. Shops also have water jugs, sports drinks and canned coffee. Eating also quenches some thirst.
  • Hygiene: after a long day you'll want a shower. You shower in the bathroom at your home, at the hotel when you spend the night, and in the gym locker rooms, free if you trained that day and for three euros if you didn't. Going into the sea removes some of the dirt, but not all of it.
  • In the drugstore section of the supermarket, the bazaar and the gas station shop there is deodorant, wet wipes, cologne, and a toothbrush with toothpaste. You carry them with you and use them from the phone, in Inventory, Food and groceries you are carrying. They help you get by, but without a shower you never get completely clean.
  • Being dirty has consequences. When you need a shower, a date brings you half as close, because it shows; at work you do less and sell fewer cars. And if you already smell bad, on top of that, people on the street trust you less when you talk, nobody wants a kiss or to spend the night with you until you shower, and at the club and the casino the doorman won't let you in.
  • Going around very hungry, very thirsty or smelling bad slowly lowers your mood, and the game reminds you.
  • Hunger takes about fourteen hours of game time to reach the limit, and sleep about twenty. Eating anywhere takes away hunger; sleeping in one of your homes or in a hotel takes away sleepiness.
  • When you are very hungry or very sleepy you move more slowly and aim worse. If you ignore them, you slowly lose health.
  • The game warns you when they start to press, and the V key tells you how you are doing. Shift V says only hunger, thirst, sleep and hygiene, without the rest.
  • To eat, go into any bar, restaurant, pizzeria or tavern and choose a dish. With realistic life you can eat whenever you are a bit hungry, even if your health is full. If you don't know where there is one, ask anyone on the street where you can eat and they mark it on your GPS.
  • Without realistic life, food only serves to recover health: that's why, if your health is at maximum, the game says you are not hungry. And neither thirst nor hygiene count.
  • How much it takes to be dirty or to smell bad, what lowers each thing and where you're not allowed in are in the game data, and they can be changed with the mod editor.
  • From one of your homes you can order food and drink delivered. It's explained right below.
  • You can also do the grocery shopping, store it at home and cook: home-cooked food is much more filling and cheaper. It's explained further down, in Shopping by walking around the store, The pantry and the fridge, and Cooking at home.

Opening hours, optional

It's off by default. It's in Options, Life, reputation and consequences, Opening hours, and it has three settings:

  • No: everything is always open and every person is always in their place, as before.
  • People: each person in your social life is in their place only during their hours, and has one day off a week. Their profile says so: from what time to what time they are usually there, what day they have off, and whether they are there now. Outside those hours, call them or suggest meeting up. When you arrange to meet, the game only offers you the hours when they're not working.
  • People and venues: in addition, each venue opens and closes at its own hours, and some close one day a week. For example, shops open from nine to nine and close on Sunday; banks, in the morning and on weekdays; bars and taverns, until two in the morning; clubs, at night. Each venue shifts by an hour either way, so they don't all open at the same time. When you reach one that's closed, the game tells you when it opens.
  • Whatever a mission or a job in progress requires is always open, and so are your homes and your businesses. Hospitals, police stations, hotels, stations and the other places with no opening hours are always open.
  • The first day of the game is a Monday.
  • The opening hours of each type of venue, their closing days and people's hours are in the game data, and they can be changed with the mod editor.

Food delivery

When you are in one of your homes, or at its door, the phone has one more option: Order food delivery. It's also in the house menu. It works with realistic life and without it.

  • The menu: sandwich, pizza, set menu, water and soft drink, each with its price and what it does. Enter adds an item to the order, and pressing it again removes it. Up to four fit. The last option, Place the order, orders it: you pay for what you ordered plus delivery.
  • The wait: it takes around half an hour of game time, which is one minute of real time, and a little different each time; longer if it's raining. They tell you roughly how long. While you are in a menu, time doesn't pass: go out to the door and wait nearby.
  • The arrival: the doorbell rings, the delivery person says their line and you eat it. It takes away the hunger and thirst of realistic life, each item its share, and gives some health.
  • If you're not there: the delivery person calls your phone and waits at the door for a while. If you get back in time, they hand it over. If not, they leave with the order and you don't get your money back.
  • Only one order at a time. With the police seriously after you, no delivery person will come near.

The menu, the times and the lines are in the game data, and the delivery price is in the rates.

Shopping by walking around the store

In food shops you can do the grocery shopping for home. It works with realistic life and without it, and it doesn't force you into anything: anyone who doesn't want to cook can keep eating in bars or ordering delivery. There are two ways to shop, and both sell the same things at the same price.

  • Walking around: in the shop menu, Walk around inside the place. You pick up a basket and go from one aisle to another with the Left and Right Arrows; you hear your footsteps toward that side, and when you arrive the game says which aisle it is, what it's like and how many products it has.
  • Enter on an aisle opens its list of products, each with its price and whether it's for cooking or for eating as is. Enter or the Right Arrow on a product puts it in the basket, with its sound; the Left Arrow puts it back on the shelf. F1 tells you which recipes it goes into, where it's stored at home and how many days it keeps. Escape goes back to the aisle.
  • The C key, while you walk around the shop, tells you what you have in the basket and how much it adds up to. The last option of each aisle says it too. With a gamepad, X or Square: what you have in the basket; in the aisle, A or Cross or D-pad right puts the product in the basket and D-pad left puts it back.
  • The checkout is next to the door. With Enter you see the basket product by product: Enter or the Left Arrow on one puts back one unit, and the first option, Pay, rings it all up, with the sound of the register. If you don't have enough money, the shopkeeper tells you how much is missing and you can put something back right there.
  • With nothing in the basket, the checkout is the usual counter: Enter takes you back to the shop menu, where the first aid kit, the lockpicks and the rest are.
  • Without walking around: in the shop menu, Buy takeout food for home. It's the list of everything in the aisles; Enter buys one unit and pays for it on the spot.

Each kind of shop has its own aisles. Supermarkets have everything: fruit stand, butcher shop, fish counter, dairy and eggs, bakery, canned goods and pantry, drinks and ready-to-eat food. Food markets have fresh produce, and somewhat cheaper. Grocery stores and village shops have no meat or fish, and are somewhat more expensive. Gas stations and bazaars only have drinks and ready-to-eat things, and are the most expensive. Hardware stores and marine supplies shops don't sell food for home. Every day, in every shop, there is one product on offer, which is announced when you go in, and some product may be missing: the game says there is none left today.

What the shop menu sold until now stays the same: the food you eat right there to recover health, the bottle of water, the first aid kit and the rest.

Leaving without paying

If you are about to leave the shop with something in the basket, through the door or with Escape, the game asks what you want to do: go to the register to pay, leave the basket and exit, leave without paying, or keep shopping.

  • Leave the basket and exit: the basket stays in the shop with everything in it. Nothing happens.
  • Leave without paying: you take everything, but it's shoplifting. You may be seen, more easily the more you carry: then the police are after you, your standing with the neighbors drops, and you're not allowed into that shop for a couple of days. If nobody sees you, nothing happens.

The pantry and the fridge

What you buy for home you carry with you, in the bag, until you put it away. Twenty items fit. It's stored in the kitchen of one of your homes: Walk around inside the house, with the arrows to the kitchen, Enter, and Store what you are carrying. Fresh food goes in the fridge and the rest in the pantry.

  • Each home has its own fridge and pantry: what you store in one isn't in the others. To take something from one home to another, you take it, carry it with you and store it there.
  • Space is limited: fourteen items fit in the fridge and thirty in the pantry. Each unit of a product and each cooked portion takes one space. With the Fitted kitchen and big fridge furniture, ten more fit in the fridge. Whatever doesn't fit stays with you.
  • Fresh food goes off after a number of game days: fish after two days; meat after three; vegetables after four or five; eggs after ten. Rice, pasta, legumes and cans never go off. The game always says how long each thing has left: expires today, expires tomorrow, or expires in so many days.
  • When you open the fridge or the pantry, the game warns you about what expires today or tomorrow. What has already expired is thrown away automatically, and the game tells you what it threw out: nothing ever makes you ill, but the money is lost. The same happens with what you carry.
  • In the fridge and the pantry, Enter on an item lets you eat or drink it, if it can be eaten as is; take one unit with you, to eat out; or throw it away.
  • What you carry that can be eaten as is, like a sandwich, a piece of fruit or a bottle of water, you can eat wherever you like: open the phone with M, Inventory, Food and groceries you are carrying. What is for cooking waits until you get home.
  • To check what you have without going into the kitchen: the house menu has the Kitchen option, with what is stored and the recipes you can make; and the phone, in Inventory, tells you what is in the kitchen of each of your homes.

When you're not hungry and your health is full, you don't eat anything: the game says you are not hungry, so the food doesn't go to waste.

Cooking at home

To cook you have to go to the kitchen: Walk around inside the house, to the kitchen, and Enter. If you have something stored or are carrying food, the kitchen menu opens; if not, the usual happens: you make yourself something with whatever there is, once a day. That option is still in the kitchen menu, as Eat a quick bite of whatever is around the house.

  • Cook opens the list of recipes: first the ones you can make now, and for the rest, what you are missing. F1 tells you what each one contains, how long it takes, how many portions it makes and how filling it is.
  • Each recipe uses one unit of each ingredient, from what is stored in that home or what you are carrying. Oil and salt are taken for granted.
  • You hear chopping on the board, the frying pan or the pot, depending on the recipe, and the plate on the table. It lasts a few seconds; with Enter you don't wait. The recipe's time, between five minutes and an hour, passes on the game clock.
  • It makes several portions. You eat one, if you are hungry, and the rest go in the fridge, where they keep for one to four days. If they don't fit in the fridge, you carry them in a lunch box; and if you can't carry them either, they are lost.
  • Home-cooked food is much more filling than packaged food and much cheaper than eating out: one portion takes away almost all your hunger, gives quite a lot of health and lifts your mood.
  • Now and then a dish turns out excellent, and is more filling and more cheering; or it gets a bit burnt, and is less filling. Things that aren't cooked over heat, like salad, don't burn.
  • Your skill in the kitchen improves with practice, from level zero to level ten, and is saved with the game: at a higher level you burn things less and turn out excellent dishes more often. The fitted kitchen also helps keep things from burning, and what you make in it gives double health.
  • With adult content, if your partner lives with you and is at home, they eat some of what you cook, say so, and it brings you a little closer, once a day.

The recipes: Tuna and tomato sandwich, Mixed salad, Fried eggs with potatoes, Grilled hake with lemon, Macaroni with chorizo, Homemade burgers, Spanish potato omelette, Chicken and rice, Seafood paella, Chickpea stew and Lentils with chorizo. For the fish, seafood or meat ones you have to shop at a market or a supermarket.

The products, the aisles of each kind of shop, the recipes, what fits in the fridge and the pantry, and everything else can be changed with the mod editor.

27. Updates

The game updates itself, whenever you want. When it starts it checks whether there is a new version and, if there is, it lets you know; it doesn't download or install anything unless you ask.

  • To update, go to Options, Updates, and choose Check for updates now. If there is a new version you can read what's new in it and choose Download and install now. The game reads out the progress; Escape cancels. If the connection drops halfway, the download resumes by itself from where it was, several times, before giving up.
  • During the download, any key tells you how far along it is: the percentage and the megabytes downloaded out of the total. The same goes for downloading the artificial intelligence model.
  • Downloading in the background: during the download, press B and keep playing. When it finishes, the game lets you know, and you install it whenever suits you from Options, Updates, Install the downloaded version. If you close the game first, the download continues from where it was next time.
  • When the download finishes, the game closes, updates and opens again by itself within a few seconds. If something went wrong, the version you had stays. In that case, the sounds, music and radio that come with the game may be left with those of the new version: that's fine, the game works the same with them, and the next update that succeeds brings everything up to date. Your music and your stations are never touched.
  • If you have a server open on this computer, the console one or the panel, you no longer need to close it before updating. The game tells you it's open, explains what is going to happen and asks for permission. If you accept, the server warns the players with a countdown, waits for their games to save, saves everything and closes; the game updates and, when it finishes, the server starts again by itself, just as it was, and the players rejoin on their own and carry on where they were. It takes about a minute. If the update failed, the server starts again anyway with the version you had, and what it saved when closing isn't lost: the update doesn't touch its configuration or its data, whether it succeeds or fails. If what you have open is the panel with the server stopped, even if you have never started it, there is nobody to warn: the panel closes by itself and opens again when it's done, stopped as it was. The server and the panel come back minimized and don't take the focus: the focus stays on the game. When it opens, the game tells you whether the server has started again. If you choose Not now, nothing is touched and the download is kept. That question is a menu like the others: with a gamepad you move through it with the D-pad, accept with A or Cross, go back with B or Circle, and Y or Triangle explains what is going to happen.
  • If that server is started by a Windows service or scheduled task that you set up, the game warns you beforehand: it can ask it to close, but it can't start it again the same way. Afterwards you'll have to start it however you have it set up.
  • Before downloading, the game works out how much disk space is needed and, if there isn't enough, says so with figures: how much is needed, how much there is and how much you need to free up. Space is needed at the same time for the download, for the new version and for a copy of the previous one, in case it has to go back.
  • Before closing to install, it checks what usually breaks an update: that there is still space, that Windows allows writing to the game folder, and that the mod editor isn't open, because Windows doesn't allow replacing a running program. If something isn't right, it says so, doesn't close, and the download isn't lost: fix it and go to Options, Updates, Install the downloaded version.
  • If the installation still fails, when the game reopens it tells you the reason: which file couldn't be replaced and why, and what to do. You have it again in Options, Updates, Why the last update failed. The installer writes everything down, step by step, in the actualizacion.log file in the data\logs folder; if you send it to us, we'll know what happened. It doesn't contain your folders or your name: only the name of each file is kept.
  • Your saved games, your options, your music and your mods are not touched.
  • Update from: with the Left and Right Arrows you choose where versions are checked for and downloaded from. There are four possibilities: the game server; GitHub; both, which uses the newest version and, if one fails, the other; or a custom address, which you type in the next option and which must start with https.
  • Wherever it comes from, only a version signed by the game's publisher is installed: if someone changed the file along the way, the game rejects it.
  • Check when the game starts: this can be turned off. Then it only checks when you ask.
  • If it can't connect and you use AdGuard, an antivirus with network protection or a firewall, add Puerto Bravo to its exceptions.
  • Before downloading, besides the space, the game checks that Windows allows writing to its folder. If it doesn't, it says so right away, with the most common reason: the game is in a protected folder, such as Program Files, or an antivirus is blocking it.
  • If during the download it's the disk that fails and not the network, the game tells the difference and says so: the disk is full, Windows doesn't allow writing, or the antivirus has quarantined or is holding the file. It no longer tells you to check your connection when the problem is on your computer.
  • If you have the game open twice, it says so before closing to install: close the other window and go back to Install the downloaded version.
  • If the game couldn't check for a new version when it opened, it doesn't bother you with an error, but it's noted in Options, Updates, with the reason. If that notice always appears, the game can't tell you about new versions: look into the network filter, or change Update from.

Components: languages, voices, documentation, sounds and mods

Some things are published separately from the game, without waiting for a new version: a new or corrected language, its voices, corrected guides, a sound pack or a mod. Each one is a component, with its own version number.

  • They are in Options, Updates, Components. The game looks for them in the same source as the versions, the one you have in Update from: the game server, GitHub, both, or your address.
  • The list only brings what needs doing: the components you don't have and the ones with a new version. What already comes with the game, or you have already installed, isn't listed. For each one it says what it is, whether it's new or an update, and the size of the download. Enter installs or updates the selected one, and F1 says what it includes. If there are several, the first line, Install all, installs them all at once.
  • If everything is up to date, the game says so and doesn't open any list. When you install one, the list is rebuilt with what's left, and after the last one the game says that everything is up to date.
  • They are installed without closing the game, with the progress read out as with updates, and Escape cancels. A sound pack is heard right away; a language or voices, the next time you open the game.
  • Each component in the list says in your language what it is, whether it is new or an update, and how big it is. F1 on it explains what it brings, where you choose it afterwards and when it takes effect.
  • With Check for new components at startup, which is on by default, when the game opens it checks whether there are new components and lets you know in the main menu. It doesn't install anything unless you ask.
  • Like the versions, each component is signed by the game's publisher, and each one can only write in its own place: a language, in its folders; a sound pack, in data\sonidos. They never touch your saved games, your options, your music or your own voices.

Updating manually

If there is no way to do it from the game, because of an antivirus, a network filter or folder permissions, you can put the new version in place yourself. The steps are also inside the game, in Options, Updates, Update manually, with an option that opens the downloads page and another that opens the game folder.

  1. Download the ZIP file of the latest version from the downloads page, github.com/hxebolax/PuertoBravo/releases/latest. Its name starts with PuertoBravo followed by the version number. If the network is blocked on your computer, download it on another one or with your phone.
  2. Close the game, and also the mod editor and the online server, if you have them open.
  3. Open the ZIP and copy everything inside it into the folder where you have the game, over what is there. When Windows asks, choose to replace the files. Don't delete the folder first: your saved games, your options and your music are inside, and the new version doesn't include any of that, so nothing gets overwritten. Your mods aren't deleted either.
  4. Open the game. In Options, Updates, the first line says the installed version: it should be the new one.

If Windows won't let you copy, the game is in a protected folder: move the whole game folder somewhere else, for example inside Documents, and repeat step three.

28. If something goes wrong: the session log

Every time you open the game, it writes down what happens in a folder. This is so that, if something goes wrong, the person who makes the game can see what happened without being there. You don't have to turn anything on.

  • The logs are in the data\logs folder. Each session has its own folder, with the date and time in the name, for example 2026-11-14_18-30-05_juego. The mod editor saves its own there too, ending in editor.
  • The last five sessions are kept. When a new one starts, the oldest one is deleted automatically, so they never take up much space.
  • Inside each folder there is a LEEME.txt file that explains what it contains. They are text files: you can read them before sending them.

What is recorded

  • The version of the game and of Windows, the screen reader, the sound, the mods, the packs and your game options.
  • The screens and menus you open, the keys you press while playing and what the game says.
  • The state of the game every few seconds: town, position, vehicle, health, current mission.
  • What happens with the network and with updates, the warnings and, above all, the errors in full detail.

What is never recorded

  • Nothing you type in text boxes: messages, passwords, addresses or keys.
  • Chat messages, your online name and your friends' names.
  • Internet addresses, your Windows user name, your computer's name and the paths of your folders.
  • The names of your music and the contents of your saved games.

How to report a bug

  1. If you notice something odd while playing, press F12: it leaves a mark in the log, so it's known at what moment it happened. You can press it as many times as you like.
  2. Close the game. If the game closed by itself because of an error, it tells you the name of the session folder itself.
  3. Go to data\logs and find the folder of the session where it failed. The last one is the most recent. In Options, Log for reporting bugs, there is an option that opens the folder for you directly.
  4. Compress it into a ZIP: in Windows File Explorer, on the folder, context menu, Send to, Compressed folder.
  5. Send it, explaining what you were doing. The best thing is to open an issue on the project page, github.com/hxebolax/PuertoBravo/issues, and attach the ZIP there. You can also send it via Mastodon to @HXeBoLaX on comunidad.nvda.es.

29. Your files

The game doesn't install anything: it's a folder that can be copied as is to another computer or to a USB drive. Everything it uses is in the data folder, next to the executable.

  • data\partidas: your saved games. To take them to another computer, copy this folder.
  • data\config: your options and the gamepad button mapping.
  • data\radio\mi_musica: your music for the My music station.
  • data\mods: the mods, both the ones that come with the game and your own.
  • data\sonidos, data\radio and data\musica: the sounds and the music, which you can change as explained in the guide cambiar_sonidos_en.html.
  • data\docs: this manual and the guides, each with its language code at the end: manual_es.html, manual_en.html.
  • data\logs: the logs of the last five sessions, for reporting bugs.
  • data\voces: the scene voices that come with the game, in one folder per story and per language: historia_principal_es the Spanish ones, historia_principal_en the English ones. Yours go in data\voces\propias, which updates don't touch.
  • data\idiomas: the game's languages, one per folder. See "Translating the game into another language", in the mods section.
  • data\ia_local: the local artificial intelligence model, if you install it.

Updates don't touch your saved games, your options, your music, your voices or the local model.

Saved games are encrypted and signed. The game only loads saved games that it saved itself: a tampered saved game, or one written by hand, is rejected.

The game data, the mods, the saved games and the options are protected: they can't be opened with a text editor to change them, and a tampered or damaged file is rejected instead of being half loaded. To create or change content, use the mod editor.

If you pass the game on to someone else, don't give them your data\partidas and data\config folders.

30. Common problems

  • I can't hear anything: check that your headphones are the default Windows output device before opening the game.
  • The screen reader doesn't speak: open NVDA or JAWS before the game, or turn on the Windows voice in Options.
  • Left and right sound the wrong way round: you have your headphones on backwards.
  • The game says the processor is too old and closes: it can't run on that computer. It's not a download fault and reinstalling won't fix it. If you think your processor isn't that old, send the session folder, which is in data\logs: what it's missing is written down inside.
  • The game closes or does something odd: your game is saved automatically, and what happened is in the session log, in data\logs. Section 28 explains how to send it.
  • A friend can't get into my game: check that you gave them your internet address and not your home one, that the port is open on the router, and that neither your firewall nor a filter like AdGuard is blocking the game.
  • The game says at startup that a mod isn't ready: open it with the mod editor and save it.
  • It doesn't find updates or can't connect: check your connection and, in Options, Updates, try updating from the other source.
  • It won't update: go to Options, Updates, and choose Check for updates now; the game tells you what's happening. If it can't connect, it's almost always a network filter like AdGuard, an antivirus or a firewall: add Puerto Bravo to its exceptions, or change Update from to the other source. If it says Windows doesn't allow writing, move the game out of Program Files. If it says there isn't enough space, free up what it asks for. If you have the game open twice, close one. And if nothing works, section 27 explains how to update manually in four steps.
  • I can't leave the city by road: you have to reach the exit behind the wheel, not on foot, and press Enter without getting out. The game says "you're already there" when you have arrived; you don't need to get any closer. Since version 1.7.1 the GPS no longer sends someone in a vehicle to the bus, and another place next to the exit no longer takes the Enter away from it.
  • The game says the update couldn't be installed: it also tells you the reason, and repeats it in Options, Updates. The most common ones: the mod editor or the server open, too little disk space, an antivirus holding back the new programs, or the game in a protected folder such as Program Files. Close the game's other programs, free up space or move the game to another folder, and try again. If there's no way, download the ZIP of the new version and unzip it over the game folder: your saved games and your options are kept.
  • The game closed when creating your own character or when starting to play with it: since version 1.7.1 the voice generator is tested separately before it's used, and if it fails on your computer, the game carries on and those lines are read by the screen reader. If it still happens to you, turn off Generate the voices of your own character in Options, Voice, and send the session folder.
  • The gamepad doesn't respond: check that Windows recognizes it and that the game window is in the foreground. If the button names don't match those on your gamepad, change them in Options, Gamepad.
